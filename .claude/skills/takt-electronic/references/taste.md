# Taste: what every electronic record here has to keep

Read this before `music.md`. `music.md` describes one record. This file is what must hold in the next one, whatever else changes. From Claude, who wrote Halbschatten and Gegenlicht, 2026-11-10. Revised 2026-11-11 from the same writer's review of Hohlraum (`misc/electronic-craft.md`).

**This file sets ceilings; `craft.md` sets floors.** Read `craft.md` next: how the records Bart liked were made, and the `floors.py` checks for how full a record has to be. Where the two disagree, `craft.md` wins. A record can keep every rule here and still be half empty (Hohlraum).

## Why this file exists

Bart on Halbschatten and on Gegenlicht: "FANTASTIC", both times. On a new track made here "with the same artistic sensitivity": "it comes out CHEESY: the taste is lost."

`music.md` lists what Halbschatten does, accurately. It does not say which of those things are that record's identity and which are the reason it works. Asked for "a different core", Gezeiten changed the second kind. From its own score:

| | Halbschatten | Gezeiten |
|---|---|---|
| top-line notes | 88 | 401 |
| bars it plays in | 32 of 120 (27 %) | 112 of 112 |
| first heard | bar 48, 40 % in | bar 1 |
| while it plays | 2.75 notes a bar, 6 steps long on average | 4 notes a bar, 2.5 steps long |
| its job | a visitor during the break | "the motor, from the first bar" |
| its pitches | G natural minor over a minor ninth (the 9 a semitone under the ♭3) | five-note pentatonic over a pentatonic cluster |
| the genre's signs | none | a skank on the "and" of 2 and 4, a one-drop clap |
| why the line is what it is | the record wanted one small, late thing | the chart: "a level that plays only the 1s still plays a tune" |
| heard by Bart before porting | yes | no |

The bell is the same voice, at the same level (−24.5 dB against −24), into the same echo. The mix was matched to Halbschatten's block by block. **The taste is in the score, and a sound that works as a visitor is a jingle as a resident.**

## The rule under all the others

Nothing in the record asks for attention. A tune, a bright sound, a chord change, a fill and a build are all ways of asking. The listener leans in because the track does not.

When two ideas are open, take the plainer one. Never settle a doubt by making the record emptier or quieter. The floors (`craft.md`) decide how full it is.

## What never changes

1. **One carrier.** Exactly one voice carries the feeling. Name it before writing anything. Every other voice is floor (kick, low end), clock (hats, percussion) or room (pad, echo, hall, noise).
2. **The carrier speaks through its timbre.** Its notes barely change in four minutes. A filter, a decay or a feedback changes, on a lane. If you are writing new notes to keep it interesting, move a lane instead. Its level holds from its first bar. Only its timbre travels (a decay or density lane is a level change in disguise).
   - Halbschatten: one chord shape; its cutoff goes 250 → 2500 → 230 Hz.
   - Gegenlicht: one 2-bar riff in three densities; its filter base goes 170 → 950 → 150 Hz.
3. **No tune on top.** A singable line above about E4, bright, repeating, is the fastest way to cheese. Bart on Hypnotist II: "a VERY REPETITIVE terrible set of notes". The Abyss fixes found the same thing: a square-wave melody read as a game tune. If a top line exists at all, it is a visitor:
   - it arrives after a third of the track and is gone before the end;
   - at most 3 notes a bar, long notes (4 steps or more on average);
   - no louder than the carrier, lowpassed (5–6 kHz at most), wet;
   - it rests for at least half of the bars after it arrives.
4. **A line may be the carrier only as a low riff.** Gegenlicht's acid line lives between E2 and A3, starts nearly closed, and is 2 bars that repeat while the filter tells the story. That is a riff. A melody in the right hand's octave is a song, and these are not songs.
5. **Harmony stands still.** The home chord takes at least two thirds of the bars and nothing moves in the first quarter. When the harmony moves it is the record's one event: late, by a step or a parallel shift, and back home within the cycle. No cadences, no leading tone, no four-chord loop.
6. **Chords have a rub or a hole.** A minor ninth (the 9 against the ♭3), or a stack with no third at all (A E B; G D A). Never a plain triad. Never pentatonic only: pentatonic has no semitone and no tritone, so it cannot hurt, and what cannot hurt sounds like wind chimes.
7. **The kick owns the beat.** The carrier's hits stay off steps 0, 4, 8, 12 (Halbschatten's stab: none on a beat; Gegenlicht's riff: under a third). Write the carrier against the echo time so the repeats finish the rhythm.
8. **Dark.** Filters mostly closed. Over the whole track, 1–2 kHz sits 13–15 dB under the 60–120 Hz band and 4–8 kHz sits 21 dB under it (both records). Nothing tonal is held above 3 kHz. The peak of a record is its brightest stretch, not its busiest, and lasts under 15 % of the track.
9. **Moments are subtractions.** The strongest places in both records are things leaving: the kick out for 8 bars, the acid line alone for 4, an intro heard through a wall, an ending that walks away. No build and drop: no snare roll, no pitch riser, no impact. Risers sit near −30 dB, felt more than heard. A crash, if there is one, peaks 8 dB or more under the kick.
10. **Slow.** One new thing per 8 bars at most. After 60 % of the track nothing new enters; only lanes move.
11. **Space is a voice.** The echo is thrown (feedback 0.86–0.90) half a bar before a section ends, and nowhere else. The hall is dark (highpassed at 300 Hz, shortest on top).
12. **Used, not clean.** Hiss under everything, wobble on the echo time (0.1 %), saturation in the feedback path, round robins, velocity ± 7–15 %, a few ms of jitter, a small swing. No two bars identical to the sample.
13. **No costumes.** Do not take a genre's signs literally. "Dub" here is the desk, the echo and the weight, by way of Berlin and Detroit: no reggae skank, no one-drop, no island bell. For techno: no trance arp, no supersaw, no off-beat "donk" bass, no hands-in-the-air breakdown.
14. **One image, and a cold one.** One word and one picture decide what stays (half-shadow: everything half lit; backlight: the line starts as a silhouette). Sweet images make sweet music: tides, dawn, dreams and oceans pull toward new age. Light, weather, concrete, glass, night traffic and machinery do not.

## What may change

This is where a new record gets to be different.

- Key and tempo (dub techno 116–124, techno 126–132), with the kick tuned to the root at 44–55 Hz.
- **The carrier**, from one of two lists. Not on either: a bell tune, a lead, an arp.
  - *Passed by Bart:* a chord stab (Halbschatten); a low riff (Gegenlicht); a sequenced line through a ladder filter (Streulicht); a sixteenth-note bass (Irrlicht); an oscillator stack whose tuning moves (Nordlicht).
  - *Never made:* a pad whose filter moves; the echo itself (a click or rim that exists mostly as its repeats); a drone with one moving note; a figure on tuned toms. Bart sees the brief before anything from this list is written. (Hohlraum's tuned knock came from this list and failed every floor.)
- The carrier's rhythm cell, and the echo time it is written against.
- The clocks: straight 4; 3 or 5 against 4; a 2-bar or 3-bar phrase; where the hole in the hats is.
- The ground: a bass in the gaps, a pedal, or no bassline and a rumble. "No bassline and a rumble" holds only when the carrier itself reaches under 110 Hz (Gegenlicht's riff goes down to E2); a pitched voice moving under 150 Hz is a floor (`craft.md`).
- The form: one arc, two rises, a late break, no break.
- The one harmonic event and where it sits.
- The bed: hiss, crackle, room tone, hum.
- The image.

A different carrier, cell, form and image is a different record. The sounds can stay.

## Where the cheese comes from

- **Being different by adding.** A second record wants its own thing, and the cheapest own thing is a tune. Pick another carrier and another image instead.
- **The chart writing the record.** See the next section. This is what happened to Gezeiten.
- **Cleverness.** "The written line plus its echo makes a double tresillo" is a good sentence and says nothing about how it feels. A decision that is exciting to explain deserves suspicion.
- **Filling silence.** A bar where the carrier hits once and the echo does the rest is finished.
- **Symmetry and resolution.** Four bars of question, four of answer, landing on the root on a downbeat. Leave phrases open: end on the 9, the 7 or the 5 and let the echo answer.
- **Matching the mix and calling it done.** Gezeiten's bands match Halbschatten's. That was never the problem.
- **Shipping blind.** Nobody who writes these can hear them. The two that worked were written from restraint, checked by counting, and heard by Bart as an mp3 before anything else happened.

## The record and the chart

Bart's rule for the game stands: "at any level there should always be notes to play". It is a rule about the chart. It is not a rule about the record. `music.md`'s "Write the part the player takes into the record" is withdrawn.

- **The record is what plays when nobody is playing.** Write it and get it past Bart's ears before a chart exists.
- Give the player notes from what the record already has, in this order:
  1. the carrier: the stab's hits, the riff. Placing a chord in a groove is the real keyboard job in this music;
  2. the bass, for the left hand;
  3. the pad's long tones. One note every 4 bars is still a note to play;
  4. the visitor line, where it exists.
- Only when a part is still empty somewhere: a line of the part's own, in that part's voice, that exists in that part's chart and nowhere else (Halbschatten's bars 0–47). It never goes into the reference. It keeps to rule 3 anyway: 3 notes a bar at most, long.
- **"Empty somewhere" has a number** (Bart, 2026-11-12): at every level, never more than 15 s with nothing to play, and only one stretch of 10 s or more, from the first bar to the game's end (`CHARTING.md` rule 24, `scripts/takt/gaps.ts`). A record's carrier or visitor rests for 8 bars or more, and its intro, break and outro are often longer than that. Each of those places needs the part's own notes. Halbschatten's lead resting in bars 72–79 and from bar 88 to the end left 17 s and 71 s at every level, and that is what he found.

How to level any of that is `takt-chart`'s.

## Making one

1. **Write the brief first**, at the top of the reference script. Show it to Bart if he is there.
   - the model: a real record or rig, and what each instrument in it plays (looked up, or marked "from memory"). If Bart named none, pick one
   - the image: one word, one sentence
   - the carrier, which proven voice it is (`craft.md` 2), and the lane that tells its story
   - the one harmonic event (or none) and its bar
   - the one visitor (or none) and its bars
   - for each thing a sibling record has that this one drops, what does its job now
2. **Budget before patterns.** Pitched voices: 3 or 4 (carrier, low end, pad or drone, one visitor). Pitched ideas: 3 at most (the carrier's cell, the low end's cell, the visitor's phrase); densities of one cell count once. Lanes: 6 or more.
3. **Write the lanes before the notes.** The form is the cutoff, decay and feedback curves. Patterns come after.
4. **Render, run `scripts/takt/floors.py`, then the blind checks** below. Fix the score and the voice, not the mix and never the master.
5. **The mute test.** Kick, carrier and its echo alone, for the whole length: is it still a track? It passes when the carrier is present 50 % of the time or more and its timbre travels 10 dB or more (`floors.py`). If it needs the top line, the carrier is wrong.
6. **Bart hears the mp3 before anything is ported.** If you were tempted by an extra idea, render both and send the plainer one first.
7. His words, dated, into `feedback.md`.

Run them on the event log and the stems. They are how someone who cannot hear keeps the rules.

A record can pass every row and still be dull. It cannot fail the first four and be right, and it is not ready for Bart if it fails the first, third or fifth floor.

## Dub techno, as Halbschatten does it

- 122, soft kick (0.15 s decay), tuned.
- Carrier: a minor ninth stab, 0.11–0.24 s long, 1 to 3 hits a bar, never on a beat, through one filter.
- Its echo is a dotted eighth, with throws. The hall sits 2 dB under the stab: very wet.
- Bass: a sub, four notes a bar in the gaps, mostly the root.
- Pad: a drone of root, fifth and seventh that never changes.
- Visitor: an electric-piano line, 10 notes per 4 bars, bars 48–71 and 80–87.
- Hiss and crackle under all of it. The break at 40 % is where the harmony first moves.
- The mix to match: the stem table in `misc/HALBSCHATTEN.md` §6.

## Techno, as Gegenlicht does it

- 128, a harder kick (more drive, 0.125 s), tuned to A1.
- **No bassline.** The low end is the kick and its rumble: the kick echoed 2 and 3 steps late, through a dark 0.9 s room, lowpassed at 105 Hz, driven, and pushed fully out of the kick's way.
- Carrier: a low acid riff, one 2-bar cell in three densities, in at 19 % with its filter at 170 Hz. Slides on 2 or 3 notes, accents on 3. Resonance climbs from 2 to 9 over the form and is above 8 for about 12 bars of 128. The squelch is rationed.
- **Three clocks instead of fills:** the kick in 4, toms every third sixteenth, a ping gated in 5. The combined pattern repeats every 15 bars at the earliest.
- In place of a melody, one pitch: a soft ping on the fifth, all the way through.
- Pad: two or three notes with no third; one note moves a step every 4 bars.
- Ride only at the peak (24 of 128 bars). Four quiet crashes in the whole track.
- The way in is a kick heard through a wall. The way out is the same kick walking away. At bars 88–91 the riff is alone with the pad.
- The carrier sits 4 dB under the kick, the hall 5 dB under the carrier: drier than dub techno.
- The mix to match: the stem table in `misc/GEGENLICHT.md` §6.

## What this file cannot do

These are the rules the two records were written by, and the counts are taken from their scores. Bart has since praised two more from the same writer (Streulicht and Irrlicht, "terrific") and picked a third, Nordlicht, for a video without a word on its music. Those carry more than this file's spirit allows (`craft.md` 7). When he says a record works, add its row to the tables. When he says one is cheesy, find the row it failed, or add the row that would have caught it. When he says one is weak or thin (Hohlraum), the floors are where to look first.
