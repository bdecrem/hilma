# Study 16 — the full back story

Bart's CASBS office (got it in the 2026 grab). The most-occupied study on the
hilltop — **76 fellows since 1954** — and, for the "ghost in the machine" theme,
the densest mind-and-machine lineage at CASBS, with two Nobel economists for good measure.

## The "ghost in the machine" spine
Read chronologically, Study 16 is almost a syllabus for mind-as-machine:

- **Willard Van Orman Quine** (1958–59) — towering analytic philosopher. "Two Dogmas
  of Empiricism," holism, indeterminacy of translation. Dissolved the analytic/empirical
  line — groundwork for thinking about meaning and mind computationally.
- **Amos Tversky** (1970–71) — with Kahneman, mapped the *bugs* in the mind's
  machinery (heuristics, biases, prospect theory) → behavioral economics. (Kahneman,
  in Study **6**, got the Nobel after Tversky died in '96.)
- **John McCarthy** (1979–80) — **coined the term "Artificial Intelligence,"** invented
  Lisp, founded the Stanford AI Lab, Turing Award. The father of AI sat in this room.
  (Same year, in the wing Bart scouted: Haugeland #51, Dennett #52, Pylyshyn #53 — the
  philosophers of AI/mind. 1979–80 was CASBS's AI moment, centered on #16.)
- **Dan Jurafsky** (2012–13) — computational linguist; co-author of *Speech and Language
  Processing* (the NLP textbook), MacArthur fellow. The line runs straight from McCarthy's
  room to today's language models.
- **John Markoff** (2018–19) — the NYT journalist who chronicled it all; wrote the Stewart
  Brand biography *in this room*. (See john-markoff.md.)

## The poetic twist
**Albert Hofstadter** (1966–67) — philosopher of aesthetics, Heidegger's translator —
sat in Study 16. His son **Douglas Hofstadter** wrote *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (strange loops,
machine consciousness). The father of "the ghost-in-the-machine guy" haunts the office.

## The eminence layer
- **Theodore W. Schultz** (1956–57) — Nobel in Economics 1979 (human capital).
- **Paul M. Romer** (1989–90) — Nobel in Economics 2018 (endogenous growth — knowledge as
  the engine of growth).
- **Seymour Martin Lipset** (1955–56) — only person to head both the political-science and
  sociology associations.
- **Persi Diaconis** (1999–00) — Stanford mathematician and former professional magician,
  MacArthur fellow.
- **George E. P. Box** (1990–91) — "all models are wrong, but some are useful."
- **Richard McKeon** (1960–61) — the Aristotle scholar who appears as "the Chairman" in
  *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance.*
- Also: **Loren Eiseley** (*The Immense Journey*), **Kit Fine** (metaphysics/logic),
  **Deborah Tannen** (*You Just Don't Understand*), **Karin Knorr Cetina** (*Epistemic
  Cultures*), **Nell Irvin Painter**, **J. Nathan Matias** (tech accountability).

## Who to "commune with" (the CASBS prompt)
For Now What (cognition after AGI): **McCarthy** (invented the field), **Tversky** (the
mind's limits), **Quine** (what meaning is) — with **Albert Hofstadter** as the wink.

## Living ghosts you can actually meet
Most of #16's giants are gone, but three are still at Stanford and reachable:
- **John Markoff** — Distinguished Fellow, Stanford HAI. Most directly relevant to Now
  What (the AI-vs-IA debate). Draft outreach email in john-markoff.md.
- **Dan Jurafsky** — Stanford (linguistics / CS / NLP).
- **Persi Diaconis** — Stanford (statistics / mathematics).
