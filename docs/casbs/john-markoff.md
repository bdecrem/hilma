# John Markoff — Study 16 ghost (2018–19)

The one ghost in Study 16 who's alive, local, and directly relevant to Now What —
and the one you can actually have coffee with.

## Who he is
For ~28 years, **The New York Times' star technology reporter** — the paper of
record's eyes on Silicon Valley for four decades. He didn't just cover the industry;
he wrote its first drafts of history:
- Co-broke the **Morris Worm** story (1988) — the first big internet worm.
- Chronicled the hacker underground in **_Cyberpunk_** (1991, with Katie Hafner), then
  the Kevin Mitnick manhunt in **_Takedown_** (1996). The Mitnick coverage is his
  *controversial* chapter — critics (and Mitnick) said it inflated Mitnick into a
  supervillain and that Markoff was too close to the story. Good thing to ask him about.
- **2013 Pulitzer Prize** (Explanatory Reporting), as part of the NYT "iEconomy" series
  on **labor and automation** — i.e., he won journalism's top prize reporting on the
  Now What question itself.

## The books that matter for Now What
- **_What the Dormouse Said_ (2005)** — thesis: the **1960s counterculture built the
  personal computer** (the WELL, Engelbart, Homebrew, psychedelics). Title from "White
  Rabbit." The open-source/counterculture-tech origin myth.
- **_Machines of Loving Grace_ (2015)** — frames the central tension as **AI vs. IA:
  Artificial Intelligence (automate the human — McCarthy) vs. Intelligence Augmentation
  (amplify the human — Engelbart).** Title from Richard Brautigan's poem. *This is the
  Now What question.* If you read one Markoff book first, this is it.
- **_Whole Earth: The Many Lives of Stewart Brand_ (2022)** — the Stewart Brand
  biography, **which he was writing in Study 16** as a CASBS Berggruen Fellow (2017–18).

## Why he's the perfect ghost for this room
Markoff sat in **Study 16** writing about Stewart Brand — in the room **John McCarthy**
(who coined "artificial intelligence") once occupied. The journalist who spent his life
chronicling the AI-vs-augmentation war ended up in the AI founder's office, writing about
the man who tried to make computers humane. The room is the crossroads of the whole story.

## Status / how to reach him
Alive and active: **Distinguished Fellow at Stanford HAI** (Human-Centered AI) — a few
minutes from CASBS. Most reachable, most relevant person in the room. "Get coffee with
Markoff" belongs near the top of the Now What list.
