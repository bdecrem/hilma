books we couldn’t stop folding the corners of — chosen by real booksellers, with a few from claude for your shelf.
from the booksellers
1
★ this month’s favorite
Heart of Glass →
Jennifer Hillier · Aug 2026
Twenty-five years after three teenage girls met a charming drifter at a Seaside, Washington amusement park, the man convicted as the Carnival Killer recants, reopening an old crime and starting a new one.
The Poisoned Pen: “A twisty 'who really did it' with a satisfying ending that you root for long before you know it will come.”
2
 The Blue Flame →
George Pelecanos · Aug 4, 2026
Derek Strange, now in his seventies and retired from the force, takes on a case for a defense attorney in Washington, D.C., working it with questions and patience rather than technology; Pelecanos's first Strange novel in fourteen years.
NPR — Maureen Corrigan: “Does for Washington, D.C., what Raymond Chandler did for LA… I hope it's never extinguished.”
3
 The Amateur →
Chris Bohjalian · Aug 2026
A 1970s moral fable that reworks Nabokov's classic into a slow-burning literary thriller about polite society and what it permits.
CrimeReads — Molly Odintz: “Quite simply, a masterpiece. I cannot praise this novel enough. Delicate, haunting, complex, and startling; a wide-ranging condemnation of polite society.”
4
 All We Hide →
Robyn Gigl · Aug 2026
First in a new series: trans detective Lauren Kelly investigates the decades-old murder of a former high-school classmate while fighting discrimination inside her own department.
The Poisoned Pen: “Filled with characters so real you might be tempted to reach out and give them a call. Excellent.”
5
 Children of Wolves →
Lawrence Osborne · Aug 2026
A character study of expats and drifters at a seaside hotel in Turkey, with the action moving on to Istanbul.
Kirkus Reviews — 20 Best Books to Read in August: “A quietly gripping novel that begs for a sequel.”
6
 You'll Be Sorry →
Lisa Gardner · Aug 4, 2026
A standalone about a newlywed couple who move to a remote New Hampshire mountain town, an abandoned lodge, and a family of six who vanished thirty years earlier, until a dog starts digging.
Kirkus Reviews: “Thriller maven Lisa Gardner's latest delivers small-town chills, New England style.”
7
 One Good Eye →
Kevin Wade · Aug 11, 2026
Second Jeep Mullane novel from the Blue Bloods showrunner, following his Edgar-nominated debut Johnny Careless.
The Poisoned Pen: “Picked for August; John Sandford calls it a 'fast-moving classic crime novel.'”
8
 The Unknown →
Riley Sager · Aug 2026
A standalone set on a remote Vermont island that blends psychological suspense, gothic mystery, and a supernatural thread.
CrimeReads — Molly Odintz: “Combining horror and mystery in a delightfully creepy, pastiche-savvy standalone.”
9
 God's Country →
William Kent Krueger · Aug 2026
The latest Cork O'Connor novel, in which Cork uncovers a conspiracy surrounding Minnesota's Boundary Waters.
CrimeReads — Molly Odintz: “Cork uncovers a terrible conspiracy surrounding Minnesota's Boundary Waters.”
10
 The Intrigue →
Silvia Moreno-Garcia · Jul 14, 2026
Noir in 1940s Veracruz: a con artist targets the owner of a small-town boardinghouse, and her niece demands half the take.
NPR Books: “Recommended as an end-of-summer escape read alongside Richard Russo and George Pelecanos.”
11
 Promised Land →
Henry Wise · Sep 1, 2026
Sequel to Holy City: a missing-person case forces deputy sheriff Will Seems to confront how history anchors and corrupts the present in rural Virginia.
CrimeReads — Molly Odintz: “Wise brings gothic notes to the classic detective plot, creating an atmosphere all his own.”
12
 The Secret Dinner →
Raphael Montes (tr. Zoë Perry) · Aug 2026
Broke roommates in Brazil make rent by hosting private dinners for the ultrawealthy, where the menu is human flesh.
CrimeReads — Molly Odintz: “A group of broke roommates finds a novel way to make rent: hosting private dinners for the ultrawealthy, where they serve human flesh.”
