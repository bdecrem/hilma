# Dossier J — Costa Rica


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. Lawfully present migrants cannot vote in national, regional or local elections. The Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), Article 19, gives foreigners "the same individual and social duties and rights as Costa Ricans", but subject to the exceptions in the Constitution and the laws. The same article states that foreigners "may not intervene in the political affairs of the country". Article 90 defines citizenship as "the set of political rights and duties that belong to Costa Ricans over eighteen years of age". Article 93 makes suffrage a function exercised "by the citizens registered in the Civil Registry". The vote is therefore limited to Costa Rican nationals, whether by birth or by naturalisation. Article 94 makes even naturalised citizens wait twelve months after receiving their naturalisation letter before they can vote. Nothing in the law distinguishes residents by status or length of stay. Permanent residents, temporary residents, special-category workers, refugees and asylum seekers are all excluded, and no local-level exception exists for municipal elections of mayors, regidores or síndicos. The only route to the franchise is naturalisation under Constitution Article 14 and the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley de Opciones y Naturalizaciones, N.º 1155). Academic articles in the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones' journal have proposed amending Article 19 so that permanent residents could vote in municipal elections, but no such reform has been adopted.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. Lawfully present migrants cannot stand for national or local elected office. The Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), Article 19, bars foreigners from "intervening in the political affairs of the country". For national office, Article 108 requires a deputy to be a "citizen in exercise" and Costa Rican "by birth, or by naturalisation with ten years of residence in the country after having obtained nationality". Article 131 requires the President and Vice-Presidents to be "Costa Rican by birth and a citizen in exercise". At local level, the Municipal Code (Código Municipal, Ley N.º 7794, 1998) keeps elected office for nationals. Article 15(a) requires a mayor (alcalde) "to be Costa Rican and a citizen in exercise". Article 22(a) requires a councillor (regidor) "to be a citizen in exercise and Costa Rican" and, under Article 22(d), registered as a voter in the canton. Article 56 applies the same requirements to members of district councils (concejos de distrito). Citizenship itself belongs only to Costa Ricans (Constitution Article 90). As a result, no category of migrant can run for any elective post: not permanent or temporary residents, refugees, stateless persons or workers in special categories. Naturalised citizens can stand for most offices, although deputies need ten years of residence after naturalisation and the presidency is closed to them entirely.


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), articles 19, 33 and 41, and in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31. Article 19 of the Constitution gives foreigners "the same individual and social duties and rights as Costa Ricans, with the exceptions and limitations that this Constitution and the laws establish". It also places them under "the jurisdiction of the courts of justice and the authorities of the Republic". Article 33 provides that "every person is equal before the law and no discrimination contrary to human dignity may be practised." Article 31 of Ley 8764 gives foreigners the rights and guarantees recognised for Costa Ricans in the Constitution, to be read in line with ratified human-rights treaties. Its paragraph 1 states: "Every foreign person shall have the right of access to justice, to respect for the guarantees of due process, to the right of defence and to the right of petition and response." The protection covers all foreigners regardless of status. It applies before administrative bodies, including the Dirección General de Migración and the Tribunal Administrativo Migratorio, and before civil, labour and criminal courts. The exceptions in article 19 concern political rights, not access to the courts. Article 19 also bars foreigners from turning to diplomatic channels instead of the domestic courts, except where treaties provide otherwise. Habeas corpus and amparo before the Sala Constitucional are open to any person, including irregular migrants, under the Constitutional Jurisdiction Act (Ley de la Jurisdicción Constitucional, N.º 7135).

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. The closest provision is the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31(7). It gives every foreign person subject to migration control and procedures the right to be told the reasons for apprehension, to communicate, including with consular officials, to an interpreter where needed, and to full access to the administrative file. It guarantees only "access to a lawyer at one's own expense" ("acceso a un abogado por cuenta propia"). No provision in Ley 8764 or the Reglamento de Extranjería requires the State to provide or pay for counsel in deportation, expulsion, rejection or status-cancellation proceedings, or before the Tribunal Administrativo Migratorio. State-funded defence exists in criminal matters: the Criminal Procedure Code (Código Procesal Penal, Ley 7594), article 13, assigns a public defender to any accused person who does not choose one. It does not extend to administrative migration proceedings. A narrow exception applies to trafficking victims. The Law against Trafficking in Persons (Ley 9095), article 37(d), gives them access to free legal assistance and representation, which covers information on their migration situation. This is a victim-protection measure, not a general right to counsel in status proceedings. Unaccompanied children are represented by the Patronato Nacional de la Infancia (PANI) under Ley 8764 art. 65. In practice, asylum seekers and other migrants rely on NGOs and university legal clinics.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Code (Código Procesal Penal, Ley 7594), articles 14, 130 and 131. Article 14 provides: "When the accused does not correctly understand the official language, they shall have the right to have a translator or interpreter appointed, without prejudice to appointing one of their own choosing at their own expense." The appointment is made by the authorities; only an interpreter of the accused's own choosing is at their own expense. Article 130 requires all procedural acts to be in Spanish. It adds that "a translator or interpreter shall be provided, as appropriate, to persons who do not know Spanish" and that documents in another language must be translated when necessary. Article 131 provides for questioning through a translator or interpreter. The protection applies to every accused person and to anyone else who takes part in the proceedings without knowing Spanish, whatever their nationality or migration status. Article 31 of the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) extends constitutional guarantees to all foreigners. Its paragraph 7 also guarantees an interpreter "where necessary" in migration control procedures. The Code does not say in so many words that the service is free. The court appoints the interpreter, and only a privately chosen one is paid by the accused, so the cost falls on the State.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right through the absence of any criminal offence of irregular entry. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) treats unauthorised entry only as a ground for the administrative measure of deportation. Article 183, in Title X on 'Sanctions on foreign persons', defines deportation as the act ordered by the Dirección General to remove a foreigner "when they have entered the country clandestinely or without complying with the rules governing their entry or stay". Article 185 bars re-entry for five years. Article 33(3) imposes a migration fine of US$100 per month of irregular stay, or an entry ban of three times the irregular stay. Collection of the fine has been postponed several times by decree. The only criminal offence in Title XV (art. 249) targets smugglers, those who transport, facilitate or harbour migrants, and not the migrant who enters. Article 39 of the Constitution provides that no one may be punished except for an offence defined by prior law. The Law against Trafficking in Persons (Ley 9095), article 70, adds that trafficking victims are "not punishable criminally or administratively" for offences committed as a result of the trafficking. Asylum seekers are further covered by article 31 of the 1951 Refugee Convention, which is directly applicable. Migrants are therefore exempt from criminal sanctions but remain exposed to administrative sanctions: deportation, re-entry bans, fines and short-term apprehension (Ley 8764 art. 18(12)).


## Education

### q127 · Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right only in part. The relevant instruments are the Fundamental Education Law (Ley Fundamental de Educación, Ley N.º 2160, 1957), arts. 1 and 11; the Political Constitution, arts. 19 and 78; and the Regulation on Student Enrolment and Transfers (Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 40529-MEP), arts. 9–12. Article 1 of Ley 2160 states that "every inhabitant of the Republic has the right to education", and art. 11 obliges the State to organise adult education. Constitution art. 78 makes general basic and diversified education free in the public system. Art. 19 gives foreigners the same social rights as nationals. Under Decreto 40529-MEP, adult-education institutions (CINDEA, IPEC, night schools and night technical sections) enrol students aged 15 and over (arts. 10(f), 11). Adult students enrol themselves (art. 9). The consular card is accepted as identity for migrant and refugee students under the same procedure as for Costa Ricans (art. 12). For adult basic and secondary schooling run by the MEP, access therefore does not depend on status. No statute, however, guarantees migrants equal access to tertiary (degree-level) education or vocational training. The National Learning Institute (INA), the main public vocational-training body, requires an asylum-seeker card, a refugee DIMEX or another document proving regular migratory status. Admission and fees at the autonomous public universities are set by each university, not by a statutory equal-treatment guarantee. Beyond MEP adult schooling, the protection therefore reaches only regular migrants, asylum seekers and refugees.


## Equal protection

### q131 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), articles 19 and 33, as applied by the Sala Constitucional. Further provisions are in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 3, 7 and 31, and the Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), article 404. Article 33 provides that "every person is equal before the law and no discrimination contrary to human dignity may be practised". Article 19 grants foreigners "the same individual and social duties and rights as Costa Ricans", with exceptions only by the Constitution or law. The Sala Constitucional applies these provisions together to strike down unjustified distinctions based on foreign status. In ruling 2023-030411 it annulled a statutory rule reserving the Colegio de Médicos governing board to Costa Ricans as an "unjustified differentiation between nationals and foreigners" contrary to arts. 19 and 33. Earlier, in Voto 616-99, it annulled Labour Code art. 13. Ley 8764, article 31, guarantees foreigners the constitutional rights of nationals, read in line with human-rights treaties. Articles 3 and 7 base migration policy on human rights and the integration of migrants, and the law tasks the Migration Council with actions "that prevent discrimination" against the foreign population. Labour Code article 404 prohibits discrimination at work on grounds of "national ancestry". The ban covers all migrants, but explicit exceptions remain where the Constitution or law makes them (political rights, union leadership).


## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: only major convictions (e.g., criminal offenses) can lead to loss of residence status
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica regulates the effect of convictions on residence status in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 13(17), 70, 129, 186 and 187. Article 129 obliges the Dirección General to cancel the permanence and residence authorisation of a foreigner 'in the cases contemplated in article 70' (inciso 4) and when 'their record or actions constitute a threat to security and public order' (inciso 9). Article 70 bars legal permanence for a foreigner who 'has served a sentence for an intentional crime (delito doloso) in the last ten years, in Costa Rica or abroad', provided the act is a crime under Costa Rican law. Article 13(17) lets the Dirección General cancel legal permanence by reasoned resolution with due process, including where the person is linked to intentional criminal acts. Under article 186 the Ministry of the Interior may order the expulsion of a foreigner with legal permanence in any category whose activities compromise peace, public security or public order. Article 187 bars re-entry for ten years, or twenty-five years for intentional crimes against minors or violence against women, persons with disabilities or older adults. Only convictions for intentional criminal offences (delitos dolosos) or comparable security grounds lead to loss of status. Negligent offences, misdemeanours and administrative migration fines (e.g., the overstay fine in art. 33) are not grounds for cancellation. Cancellation is a reasoned administrative decision subject to appeal. Separate non-conviction grounds exist, such as unauthorised paid work (art. 129(8)). Recognised refugees may be expelled only for national security or public order after a legal procedure (art. 122).

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 6(6), 31 and 65, read with the Political Constitution (Constitución Política) article 7. Article 6(6) makes it a duty of the State's migration policy to 'guarantee that the national territory shall be asylum for every person with well-founded fear of persecution, who faces a danger of being subjected to torture, or who cannot return to another country, whether of origin or not, where their life is at risk, in accordance with the duly ratified international and regional instruments.' Article 31 requires all norms on foreigners' fundamental rights to be interpreted according to ratified human-rights treaties. Paragraph 9 bars expelling, deporting or rejecting asylum seekers and refugees to any country where their life is at risk. Article 65 requires rejections at the border to be carried out 'with full observance of article 31 of the Political Constitution'. The Convention against Torture (ratified 1993) has supra-legal rank under Const. art. 7. The Sala Constitucional treats human-rights treaties as directly enforceable through habeas corpus and amparo (Ley 7135, art. 2(a)). CAT article 3's absolute bar on returning anyone to a risk of torture therefore applies to all migrants. The Refugee Regulation (Decreto 36831-G) art. 100 requires the refugee commission to be trained on survivors of torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment. Art. 17 extends non-refoulement to people claiming statelessness or asylum. The ban on return to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, as distinct from torture, rests mainly on the directly applicable treaties (CAT, ICCPR art. 7, ACHR arts. 5 and 22(8)). No statute spells it out.

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política) article 31 and in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 31(8)-(9), 106, 115 and 116. It is further developed in the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G), articles 14 and 134. Constitution art. 31 makes the territory an asylum for the politically persecuted and forbids expelling them to the country where they are persecuted. Ley 8764 art. 115 provides that 'refugees and asylum seekers who, owing to well-founded fears of being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, gender, membership of a particular group or political opinions, are outside their country of nationality ... may not be deported to the territory of the country of origin.' Article 31(9) adds that no asylum seeker or refugee may be expelled, deported or rejected to any country where their life is at risk. Article 116 gives an asylum claim suspensive effect on extradition; recognition ends extradition proceedings based on the same facts. Article 106 adopts the 1951 Convention definition, adding gender. Article 65 requires rejections at the border to respect Const. art. 31. Regulation art. 14 (as reformed by Decreto 44501-MGP, 2024) gives asylum seekers 'protection against return and the same guarantees as a recognised refugee' until their claim is decided. Art. 134 makes any authority that learns of a wish to seek asylum responsible for respecting non-refoulement. Art. 17 extends the principle to people claiming statelessness or asylum. The protection covers recognised refugees and asylum seekers, including those at the border.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 6(6), 31(9) and 94(12), read with the Political Constitution (Constitución Política) article 7. Article 31(9) provides that 'no foreign asylum seeker or refugee may be expelled, deported or rejected to another country, whether of origin or not, where their right to life is at risk.' The test is risk to life, not a Convention ground. It therefore protects asylum seekers whose claims do not fit the refugee definition but who face a threat to life. Article 6(6) goes further: migration policy must guarantee asylum to any person who 'cannot return to another country, whether of origin or not, where their life is at risk', in line with ratified international and regional instruments. Article 94(12) lets the DGME grant a special category 'for humanitarian reasons, in accordance with international human rights instruments'. The Immigration Regulation (Decreto 37112-G) defines a humanitarian reason as a situation of 'high vulnerability in detriment of [the person's] condition as a human being'. Under Const. art. 7 and Sala Constitucional case law, the ICCPR (arts. 6-7) and ACHR (arts. 4, 5, 22(8)) apply directly. ACHR art. 22(8) forbids deporting any foreigner to a country where their right to life or personal freedom is at risk. The statutory bar in art. 31(9) is limited to asylum seekers and refugees. Protection for other migrants rests on art. 6(6) and the directly applicable treaties.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right, though in a limited way, mainly through a binding ruling of the Constitutional Chamber. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), art. 31(8), says that "every foreign person has the right to seek and receive asylum in the national territory". The Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G) puts several duties on the authorities. Under art. 123(a), the Refugee Unit must "provide information on the procedure for determining refugee status". Under art. 124, border, police, migration and judicial authorities must know the referral procedure. Under art. 127, an asylum claim may be "inferred", including for "persons in administrative or judicial detention and persons unaware of their possible refugee status". Under art. 134, any official who learns that a foreigner wishes to apply must ensure non-refoulement and refer the claim at once. None of these texts expressly requires every person to be told about asylum before rejection at the border (Ley 8764 arts. 64–65) or deportation (arts. 183, 209–210). The Constitutional Chamber (Sala Constitucional) filled that gap in Voto N.º 2025-019485 (file 25-006713-0007-CO). It held that the migration authority violated the rights of third-country nationals deported from the United States and held at CATEM, partly through "the failure to inform them of the possibility of requesting refuge". It ordered individual status decisions for each person. Under the Constitutional Jurisdiction Act (Ley 7135), art. 13, the Chamber's case law binds all authorities erga omnes. The duty therefore rests on case law and the regulation's referral rules rather than on an express statutory clause.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) and the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G) contain only direct non-refoulement rules. Ley 8764, art. 31(9), provides that no asylum seeker or refugee "may be expelled, deported or rejected to another country, whether or not it is the country of origin, where his right to life is at risk". Art. 115 bars deporting refugees and asylum seekers to their country of origin. Regulation art. 39 bars expelling or returning them "to the territory of a country where their life, safety or freedom is in danger". Regulation art. 40 allows a lawfully present refugee to be expelled only on national-security or public-order grounds, with a reasonable period to "seek legal admission in another country". Constitution art. 31 forbids expelling a politically persecuted person to the country where they are persecuted. These rules do reach third countries, but only where the danger exists in that country. No provision bars sending a migrant to a country that is likely to refoule them onward. The general deportation and rejection rules point the other way: Ley 8764 arts. 64 and 184 allow removal "to the country of origin or to a third country that admits" the person, with no requirement to check onward protection. Inter-American Court case law (Pacheco Tineo v. Bolivia; Advisory Opinion OC-21/14) treats indirect refoulement as prohibited, and the Sala Constitucional generally follows that case law. However, no Costa Rican statute or decision applying it to chain refoulement was found.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), arts. 31(1), 183, 186, 209–210 and 215, and in the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G), art. 40. Art. 31(1) guarantees every foreign person "access to justice, respect for the guarantees of due process, the right of defence". Deportation is ordered by the Dirección General only on the individual grounds listed in art. 183: clandestine or irregular entry, entry or stay obtained with false documents, overstay, or failure to leave after a formal order to do so. Under art. 209, the procedure starts by "indicating the facts and charges imputed". Under art. 210, a deportation resolution follows only once "the illegality of the entry or stay" is established, and it must be duly notified. Expulsion of lawful residents (art. 186) requires a "reasoned resolution" of the Ministry, based on the person's activities endangering peace, public security or public order. Art. 215 sets out the investigative procedure. Refugees lawfully present may be expelled only on national-security or public-order grounds, "by virtue of a decision taken in accordance with the legal procedures in force" (Ley 8764 art. 122; Regulation art. 40). Unaccompanied minors may not be rejected (art. 65), and minors are not subject to deportation or expulsion except in their own interest (art. 185). There is a limit: deportation after failure to obey an order to leave needs no further procedure (art. 214).

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), arts. 31(1), 209, 215–217 and 122, and in the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G), art. 40. In deportation proceedings, the Dirección General must state the facts and charges and grant, "immediately, an oral hearing so that the foreign person can exercise his right of defence" (art. 209). The deportation resolution follows only after that stage (art. 210). In expulsion proceedings, the legal department investigates and gives the person "three working days to offer exculpatory evidence" before an opinion and a decision (art. 215). An expulsion order may be challenged within three days by revocation before the Dirección General or appeal to the Administrative Migration Tribunal (Tribunal Administrativo Migratorio). "The challenge of the expulsion order shall suspend its execution" (art. 216), and the order is executed only once final (art. 217). Lawfully present refugees may present exculpatory evidence, appeal and be represented (Ley 8764 art. 122), at a confidential oral hearing (Regulation art. 40). The protection is weaker for other groups. "No appeal shall lie against rejection and deportations" ordered by the competent authority (art. 222), so irregular migrants have only the hearing before the DGME. Rejection at the border can be challenged only at a Costa Rican consulate, "without any suspensive effect" (art. 65). Habeas corpus and amparo before the Sala Constitucional (Ley 7135) remain available to all.


## Family

### q73 · Right of settled migrants to family reunification.
- Law: Conditional
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica conditionally protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), arts. 78 and 79, and the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo 37112-G), arts. 72, 137, 138 and 203. Temporary residence is available to the person 'who has lived with their spouse and minor or disabled children' of the temporary-resident categories (Law 8764, art. 79(8)). Permanent residence is open to 'the foreign person, their spouse and first-degree blood relatives who have held temporary residence for three consecutive years' (art. 78(1)). Reglamento art. 137 creates special categories 'by link with a permanent resident': 'the spouse or de facto union (condition declared by a judge), fathers, mothers, minor sons and daughters and adult children up to twenty-five years, the latter in a condition of economic dependence'. 'By link with a temporary resident', it covers 'economically dependent parents' and adult children up to 25, 'in both cases, economically dependent'. Similar links exist for researchers, teachers, professionals and volunteers (art. 137(e)). These family statuses last one year and are renewable. Their renewal depends on the sponsor's permit, and they 'will not generate rights to permanent residence' (art. 138). Dependent adult children may not work (art. 203). Immediate family (spouse and minor children) can therefore join both permanent and temporary residents. Non-dependent relatives can join only permanent residents, and only parents. Temporary residents can bring parents and adult children only if those relatives are financially dependent. De facto partners need a judicial declaration of the union. Temporary, seasonal and other worker categories under Law 8764 art. 94 have no family-link category. Refugees and Costa Rican citizens' relatives have separate routes.

### q75 · Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), arts. 41, 48 and 49, read with the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley 8764), arts. 25, 29 and 225. A refusal of family-based residence by the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería can be challenged by revocation and appeal within three working days (Law 8764, art. 225). The appeal goes to the Administrative Migration Tribunal (Tribunal Administrativo Migratorio), which is competent for 'appeals against the final decisions of the Dirección General … in migration matters' (art. 29). Its decisions 'exhaust the administrative route' (art. 25). That tribunal is an administrative body. The judicial remedy lies afterwards in the contentious-administrative jurisdiction: Const. art. 49 establishes it 'as an attribution of the Judicial Power, with the object of guaranteeing the legality of the administrative function', and the law must protect 'at least the subjective rights and legitimate interests' of those concerned. Const. art. 41 guarantees 'prompt and complete justice, without denial'. Const. art. 48 gives 'every person' the amparo remedy before the Sala Constitucional for constitutional rights and human rights in applicable international instruments. The Sala has applied amparo to family-reunification refusals. In Voto 2009-01024 it held that the DGME's refusal of an entry visa to a foreign resident's spouse breached the principle of family reunification. It found that nationals and foreigners alike have 'all the reactive instruments to challenge any formal or material action of public authorities tending to weaken that fundamental right' (Const. arts. 19 and 51). The remedy is open to any foreigner affected by a refusal, whatever their status.

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in Executive Decree N.º 41329-MGP, Regulation for the Recognition of Migration Rights of Same-Sex Couples (Reglamento para el Reconocimiento de Derechos Migratorios a Parejas del Mismo Sexo, La Gaceta, 18 Jan. 2019). It is read together with the gender-neutral family categories of the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto 37112-G), art. 137(b), which covers 'the spouse or de facto union' of a permanent resident. As published, the decree regulates the DGME's recognition of migration rights 'derived from unions formed by same-sex couples, whether through a marriage bond or a de facto union duly recognised in some foreign legislation'. These rights include temporary residence, permanent residence, special categories and any other regularisation process. Same-sex marriage has been legal since 26 May 2020, following the Sala Constitucional's 2018 ruling striking down the ban (as reported). Since then, same-sex spouses of Costa Ricans and of residents are covered by the ordinary spousal routes. These are art. 79(1) and art. 73 of the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley 8764) and the residence-link categories of Reglamento arts. 72 and 137. The same conditions apply as for opposite-sex couples, including proof of the marriage and, for spouses of Costa Ricans, annual proof of cohabitation.

### q79 · Right to family reunification without resource conditions
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. Family reunification is subject to resource conditions under the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo 37112-G) and the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley 8764). Reglamento art. 31 requires CCSS social-security insurance at the moment the status is granted. It applies to permanent and temporary residents and to the special categories of art. 137 subsections (a) to (g), the family-link categories, except dependants of volunteers and professionals. The insurance must be held 'uninterruptedly until renewal of their foreign-resident identity card'. Law 8764, art. 78, likewise requires permanent residents to prove continuous CCSS insurance for renewal. The definition of 'dependant' in the Reglamento covers relatives 'who depend economically on the holder of a migration status'. The family-link categories for parents and for adult children up to 25 require 'economic dependence' (art. 137(b)-(c)), and dependent adult children may not work (art. 203). In the financially defined categories, dependants come in through the principal's file, and the principal must show the required income. A pensioner (pensionado) must prove a life pension 'not lower than one thousand dollars a month (US$1000)', and spouses and children apply as dependants under the same article (art. 100(k)-(l)). No route lets settled migrants bring family members regardless of insurance or financial conditions.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política, 1949), article 13(3) and (4), implemented by the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley N.º 1155), article 10, and backed by the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness, which has supra-legal rank under article 7 of the Constitution. Article 13 makes Costa Ricans by birth "3) the child of foreign parents born in Costa Rica who is registered as Costa Rican, by the will of either parent while a minor, or by his own until the age of twenty-five" and "4) the infant of unknown parents found in Costa Rica". Under article 10 of Ley 1155, either parent may go to the Civil Registry during the child's minority and ask that the child be registered as Costa Rican by birth, and the person may also opt personally between the ages of 21 and 25. Every child born in the territory to migrant parents, including a child who would otherwise be stateless, therefore has a constitutional entitlement to Costa Rican nationality by birth. Foundlings are Costa Rican by operation of law. The entitlement depends on registration, not on automatic attribution. For stateless migrants born abroad, Ley 1155 waives the criminal-record bar to naturalisation for persons declared stateless by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The Regulation on the Determination of Statelessness (Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 39620-RE-G of 2016) sets the procedure for that declaration. For adults born abroad, nationality still requires ordinary naturalisation, so the guarantee is complete only for children born in Costa Rica.


## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right for regular, insured migrants. It does so through the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), art. 19, and the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), arts. 7(7) and 31. Article 31 gives foreigners "the individual and social rights and guarantees recognised for Costa Ricans in the Political Constitution". Article 7(7) requires every migration procedure to guarantee migrants' "insurance with the social security" and makes holding Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social (CCSS) insurance "one of [its] basic requirements". Public health care is delivered through CCSS insurance, so a foreign resident who is insured as a worker, a voluntary member or a dependant gets the same services as an insured Costa Rican. Asylum seekers holding a provisional card can also join the CCSS. For migrants without status, protection is partial. The Children and Adolescents Code (Código de la Niñez y la Adolescencia, Ley 7739) applies to every child "without distinction… of nationality" (art. 3). It gives children "direct and free medical care from the State", which public services must give at once whatever the child's nationality. Care cannot be refused for "lack of identity documents" (art. 41). Children who have no other cover are insured at the State's expense (art. 42). Pregnant girls and adolescents are entitled to prenatal and delivery care (art. 50). CCSS rules, as described by UNHCR, also guarantee emergency care to everyone, but uninsured patients are billed afterwards. Prenatal care is extended to all pregnant women. Irregular adults cannot obtain CCSS insurance, including the State-paid insurance for the poor, which requires an IMAS assessment and an identity document. Outside emergencies and pregnancy they therefore do not have access on the same terms as citizens. Access is thus guaranteed for regular migrants and partly for irregular ones.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right only for foreigners who are lawfully and durably settled. It does so through the Operations Regulation of the National Financial System for Housing (Reglamento de Operaciones del Sistema Financiero Nacional para la Vivienda, BANHVI, 1996), arts. 5 and 6. The regulation implements the Law on the National Financial System for Housing (Ley del Sistema Financiero Nacional para la Vivienda, Ley 7052). That law funds the Bono Familiar de Vivienda (family housing subsidy) through the Fondo de Subsidios para la Vivienda (FOSUVI). Article 5 sets the general eligibility conditions: being a family, owning no home and earning no more than four minimum wages. Article 6 ("Nationality") provides that "in addition to nationals, foreigners whose migration status and family and work circumstances show reasonable prospects of residing legally and permanently in the country, with their own sources of income, may be beneficiaries of FOSUVI." Foreign residents who meet these conditions therefore qualify on the same terms as citizens. BANHVI's verification guide requires them to prove settlement ("arraigo") with a certificate from the DGME (Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería) stating their permanence status, current migration status and work permit. In practice BANHVI describes this as "legalised residence", and for some programmes as permanent residence. In a 2023 amparo ruling the Sala Constitucional (Constitutional Chamber) held that refugees cannot be refused the subsidy because of their refugee status. BANHVI circular DF-CI-0002-2026 now tells authorised entities not to reject applications from refugees or holders of the provisional asylum-seeker card on that ground. Irregular migrants, tourists and others with no prospect of lawful permanent residence are excluded.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes (including irregular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), arts. 19 and 78; the Children and Adolescents Code (Código de la Niñez y la Adolescencia, Ley N.º 7739), arts. 3, 59 and 60; and the Regulation on Student Enrolment and Transfers (Reglamento de Matrícula y de Traslados de los Estudiantes, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 40529-MEP, 2017), arts. 5, 12 and 13. Article 19 of the Constitution gives foreigners "the same individual and social duties and rights as Costa Ricans". Article 78 makes preschool, general basic and diversified education compulsory and, in the public system, "free and paid for by the Nation". The Children's Code applies to every minor "without distinction" of nationality or any other condition of the child or the parents (art. 3). It declares free, compulsory schooling a fundamental right, and failure by the State to guarantee it a violation of law (art. 59). It also requires equal conditions of access to and continued attendance at schools (art. 60(a)). The MEP decree is explicit. Every minor has the right to free public education, and that right may never be subject to discrimination on grounds of "irregular migratory status" or of being a student coming from abroad (art. 5). The consular card is a valid identity document for enrolling migrant or refugee students, and the procedure is the same as for Costa Rican students (art. 12). Children with no proof of prior schooling are placed by age through special placement tests (art. 13). The General Migration and Foreigners Law (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) art. 31 confirms that foreigners enjoy the social rights the Constitution recognises for Costa Ricans. The protection therefore covers all migrant children, including irregular migrants, asylum seekers and refugees, on the same terms as nationals.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), articles 19 and 68, and in the Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), article 167. Article 19 gives foreigners "the same individual and social duties and rights as Costa Ricans", subject to exceptions set by the Constitution and the laws. Article 68 then names wages directly: "No discrimination may be made with respect to wages, advantages or working conditions between Costa Ricans and foreigners, or with respect to any group of workers." The Labour Code turns this into an equal-pay rule. Under article 167, "for equal work, performed in an equal post, working day and conditions of efficiency, equal wages correspond", including payments in kind such as housing, and "no differences may be established on grounds of age, sex or nationality." Article 404 of the Labour Code, as rewritten by the Labour Procedure Reform Act (Ley de Reforma Procesal Laboral, N.º 9343) and amended by Ley 10437, prohibits all discrimination at work on grounds including "ascendencia nacional" (national extraction). Workers can enforce this through the special summary procedure for discrimination claims. The protection attaches to the employment relationship, not to the worker's migration status, so it covers every foreign worker. Separately, article 175 of the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) bars employers from hiring foreigners who are not authorised to work. The second sentence of article 68 says that "under equal conditions the Costa Rican worker shall be preferred". That is a hiring preference, not a pay differential.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Employment is tied to specific employer; change of employer possible, but requires new work permit application
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica regulates this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 80, 85 and 175, and in the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 37112-G), articles 86, 112 and 307. The Constitution, article 56, guarantees "the right of free choice of work". Under article 80 of Ley 8764, however, temporary residents "may only carry out the remunerated or lucrative activities, on their own account or as employees, that the Directorate General authorises". The authorisation takes into account non-binding opinions from the Ministry of Labour and other criteria of convenience. Worker permits are built around a particular job and employer. Article 86 of the Regulation requires the employment contract and a sworn statement from the employer. Article 112 grants the special category for workers in a specific occupation "requested to work by a legal person" for one renewable year. Article 307 sets out a separate procedure for a foreigner who "requests a change of employer". The worker must file a reasoned application with fees and again meet "the requirements established in this Regulation for each category or subcategory". Article 175 of the Law bars anyone from employing foreigners "not authorised" for the activity. A worker can therefore change employer only through a new authorisation. Other groups have broader rights. Permanent residents are not limited to a particular activity. Recognised refugees, and asylum seekers holding the provisional document under the Refugee Regulation (Decreto 36831-G, as amended by Decreto 44501-MGP), may work for any employer.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. Article 80 of the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) states that temporary residents may only carry out the paid activities that the migration authority (Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería) authorises. It adds that "the dependants of said temporary residents may study or work, subject to prior authorisation by the Directorate". Article 79 lists the temporary-residence subcategories, including executives, technical staff, scientists, professionals, investors and their spouses and children, and spouses who have lived with these principal migrants (subcategory 8). These family members get residence as dependants, without an automatic right to take up employment. The Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 37112-G), for example article 86(k), treats the spouse as a dependant who proves the marital link alongside the principal's application. Any work by the spouse requires a separate authorisation, which falls under the general rule in article 175 of Ley 8764 that foreigners may not be employed unless "authorised to carry out such activities". The spouse of a Costa Rican citizen holds residence in their own right (art. 79(1)) and is not a dependant of a principal migrant. Family members who receive derivative refugee status are treated as refugees under the refugee rules rather than as dependants under article 80.

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment automatically implies the loss of immigration status
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica for migrants whose status rests on a job. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) makes keeping the original grounds of the permit a condition of staying. Article 129(1) says the Dirección General "shall cancel the authorisation of stay and residence" when the foreigner "ceases to meet the requirements taken into account when authorising their entry or legal stay". Under the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo 37112-G), the employer requests the temporary-worker category (art. 108). That request needs a Ministry of Labour recommendation authorising that company or employer to hire foreign labour (art. 109). Specific-occupation workers likewise depend on a hiring legal or natural person (arts. 112-113). Temporary residents may only do the paid work the Dirección General authorises (Ley 8764, art. 80). Neither the law nor the regulation gives any grace period for finding a new employer. Losing the job removes a requirement of the permit and is a mandatory ground for cancellation, applied by reasoned resolution under the due-process procedure of Title XI (Reglamento, art. 289). The worker can only apply to change category while in the country, if they meet the requirements of another category (Ley 8764, arts. 96 and 125). While such an application is pending, the duty to leave on expiry of the permit is suspended (art. 33(3)). Migrants whose status does not depend on work keep it after losing a job. These include permanent residents, family-based residents, refugees, asylum seekers holding the provisional document and stateless persons.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica), articles 19 and 68, and in the Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), articles 200 and 404. Article 19 gives foreigners the same individual and social rights as Costa Ricans. Article 68 extends the ban on differential treatment beyond pay: "No discrimination may be made with respect to wages, advantages or working conditions between Costa Ricans and foreigners." Foreign workers with a valid permit are therefore covered on equal terms by the Labour Code's rules on hours of work, overtime, weekly rest, paid holidays and leave, sick-leave benefits and protection against unfair dismissal. These rules do not distinguish by nationality. Article 200 says expressly that "foreign workers, and their dependants, shall enjoy the benefits provided for in this Code" in the occupational-risks (health and safety and work-injury) regime. Article 404, rewritten by the Labour Procedure Reform Act (Ley N.º 9343), prohibits all discrimination at work, including on grounds of "ascendencia nacional" (national extraction), and the Act created a summary procedure for discrimination claims. Article 13 of the Labour Code, the former quota on foreign workers, was annulled by the Constitutional Chamber (Sala Constitucional) in Resolution 616-99. Some limits remain. Article 68 gives Costa Ricans hiring preference "under equal conditions", and constitutional article 60 bars foreigners from holding leadership or authority in trade unions. Neither affects individual conditions of work.

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Access to some, but not all types of social security protections (e.g., no equal access under this program; or access depending on visa type)
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right for contributory social security in the Organic Law of the Costa Rican Social Security Fund (Ley Constitutiva de la Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social, Ley N.º 17), arts. 2–3, and the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, Ley N.º 8764), art. 7(7). Article 3 of Ley 17 states that CCSS coverage and enrolment "are compulsory for all manual and intellectual workers who receive a wage or salary", with no nationality condition. Article 2 defines the compulsory insurance as covering sickness, maternity, invalidity, old age and death benefits. Ley 8764 art. 7(7) requires every migration procedure to "guarantee the social security insurance of migrants", making CCSS insurance "one of its basic requirements". A migrant worker with a valid work permit therefore joins the health (SEM), pension (IVM) and occupational-risk schemes from the first day of work, on the same contributions and benefit rules as nationals. Non-contributory protection is narrower. The CCSS non-contributory pension (Régimen No Contributivo) and other social-assistance benefits are paid from FODESAF. Under the Law on Social Development and Family Allowances (Ley de Desarrollo Social y Asignaciones Familiares, Ley N.º 5662), art. 2 (as amended by Ley 10875 of 2026), the beneficiaries are "Costa Ricans, legally resident foreigners" and, regardless of status, minors. Workers authorised to work without holding residence are therefore excluded from these tax-financed benefits. This group includes special-category holders such as temporary or seasonal workers, cross-border workers, asylum seekers with work-authorising documents and holders of the special temporary category. Access is equal in the contributory system but depends on permit type for non-contributory benefits.

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: Yes, immediate equal access to all types of unemployment benefits
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), arts. 28–30, and the Worker Protection Law (Ley de Protección al Trabajador, Ley N.º 7983), arts. 2(a), 3 and 6. Costa Rica has no operating unemployment-insurance scheme. Article 2 of the CCSS Organic Law (Ley 17) lists "involuntary unemployment" among the risks of compulsory social insurance, but no benefit has been implemented. Income protection on job loss comes instead from two instruments, both open to every worker without nationality or residence conditions. First, art. 29 of the Labour Code obliges the employer, when an indefinite contract ends through unjustified dismissal or another cause beyond the worker's will, to "pay a severance allowance (auxilio de cesantía)" scaled by years of service up to eight years. Art. 28 adds notice pay, and art. 30 protects these sums from set-off or seizure. Second, Ley 7983 art. 3 requires every public or private employer to pay 1.5% of the monthly wage into a Labour Capitalisation Fund (Fondo de Capitalización Laboral). Under art. 6(a), "when the employment relationship ends, for any cause", the worker may withdraw the full balance within fifteen days. Art. 6(d) adds withdrawal rights during suspension of the contract or reduced working hours. Because these rights attach to the employment relationship, a migrant worker with a valid work permit has them on the same terms as a Costa Rican worker from the start of employment. Constitution art. 19 (equal social rights for foreigners) and Labour Code art. 404 (ban on discrimination by national origin) reinforce this. Like nationals, migrants have no state-paid unemployment allowance.

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right for some temporary migrant workers in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), arts. 78, 79 and 125. Article 78(1) lets a foreigner, their spouse and first-degree relatives "who have enjoyed temporary residence for three consecutive years" opt for permanent residence. Article 79 lists the temporary-residence subcategories, granted for up to two years and renewable. They include the executives, representatives, managers and technical staff of companies established in the country, and specialised employees, whether self-employed or employed (subsec. 3). They also include scientists, professionals, interns and specialised technicians (subsec. 5). Article 125 says the Dirección General "shall authorise the change of category and subcategory, in accordance with article 78", for a fee of US$200. After three years of continuous temporary residence, these workers can therefore move to permanent residence. They must keep continuous CCSS insurance, which is a renewal condition under arts. 78 and 80. The route is not open to all temporary workers. Seasonal and other temporary workers, cross-border workers, specific-occupation workers and project-linked workers are admitted as special categories (art. 94). Article 95 provides that "the special categories shall not generate rights of definitive permanence", except for asylees and stateless persons. These workers can only seek permanence by first qualifying for another category under art. 96, for example through a family link with a Costa Rican (art. 78(2)).

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. The route to permanent residence depends on visa type. Under the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), art. 78(1), only those who have held temporary residence for three consecutive years may opt for permanent residence. Temporary residence covers the art. 79 subcategories, such as corporate executives and technical staff, specialised employees, scientists, professionals and technicians. Most labour migrants are instead admitted under the special categories of art. 94. These include cross-border workers, temporary workers, and specific-occupation workers, business visitors and intra-company transferees (art. 94(1)-(3)). They also include workers linked to specific projects and projects of public interest (art. 94(11)). Article 95 provides that "the special categories shall not generate rights of definitive permanence, except those of asylees and stateless persons." The Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto 37112-G) grants these worker categories for one year, renewable, on the employer's request and a Ministry of Labour recommendation (arts. 108-109, 112-113). It provides no conversion to permanent status. Seasonal agricultural and other temporary workers can reach permanence only by first qualifying for a different category under art. 96, for instance through a family link with a Costa Rican citizen (art. 78(2)). Time spent as a temporary worker does not count. Recognised refugees have their own route to permanent residence after three years (art. 126).

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, same as citizens
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), art. 31. It also protects it in the Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), arts. 404, 420 and 454, as reformed by the Labour Procedure Reform Act (Ley de Reforma Procesal Laboral, N.º 9343). Article 31 of Ley 8764 gives foreigners "the individual and social rights and guarantees recognised for Costa Ricans in the Constitution". It guarantees every foreigner "the right of access to justice, to due process, to defence and to petition". The Labour Code makes no distinction by nationality in contract rights or remedies. Article 404 prohibits "all discrimination at work" on grounds including "ascendencia nacional" (national origin), and art. 406 bars dismissal on those grounds. Article 420 gives the special labour jurisdiction power to decide "individual and collective conflicts" requiring the application of labour law. Migrant workers can therefore sue an employer for unpaid wages, overtime, severance or other breaches of contract on the same footing as Costa Rican workers. Under art. 454, workers whose monthly income does not exceed two base salaries are entitled to free legal assistance in labour proceedings. Workers may also file complaints with the Ministry of Labour's Inspection Directorate, which refers breaches to the labour courts. Nationality-based limits concern union leadership and the foreign share of a workforce, not contractual remedies.

### q135 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of race or ethnicity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica), articles 19 and 33, the Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), article 404, and the Penal Code (Código Penal, Ley N.º 4573), article 380. Article 33 of the Constitution provides that "every person is equal before the law and no discrimination contrary to human dignity may be practised". Article 19 extends constitutional rights to foreigners. In employment, article 404 of the Labour Code, rewritten by the Labour Procedure Reform Act (Ley N.º 9343) and amended by Ley 10437, "prohibits all discrimination at work on grounds of age, ethnicity, sex, religion, race, ... national extraction, social origin". This covers migrants by race, ethnicity and national extraction, and a summary judicial procedure exists for such claims. Article 380 of the Penal Code, titled "Racial discrimination" and last reformed by Ley 10156 (2022), makes it an offence for any person, or the manager of a public or private institution or a business, to apply "any harmful discriminatory measure founded on racial considerations". The penalty is a fine of 20 to 60 day-fines, and repeat offenders may also be suspended from public office. The International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination, ratified in 1967, ranks above statute under article 7 of the Constitution and is applied directly by the Constitutional Chamber (Sala Constitucional) through amparo (Ley de la Jurisdicción Constitucional, N.º 7135, art. 2). No comprehensive anti-discrimination statute covers housing, services and other areas outside employment. The criminal sanction is only a fine.


## Legal personhood

### q27 · Right not to have identity documents confiscated.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right, by implication, in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 18, 33(2) and 211, read with the Penal Code (Código Penal, Ley N.º 4573), article 369. Ley 8764 gives the power to hold a foreigner's documents only to the migration authorities. Under art. 18, the Professional Migration Police (Policía Profesional de Migración) "may retain the passport or travel document of the foreign person in order to verify their migration status" while carrying out migration control. Art. 211(4) lets the Dirección General order the "temporary seizure of documents" as a precautionary measure in an administrative proceeding, and that order can be challenged. Art. 33(2) obliges every foreigner to "carry, keep and present" their identity and migration documents, which assumes the holder has them. A private party who withholds a worker's document so that harm may result commits the offence in Penal Code art. 369: anyone who "suppresses, conceals or destroys, in whole or in part, a document so that harm may result" is punished. The Law against Trafficking in Persons (Ley N.º 9095), art. 6, lists the concealment of documents among the activities connected to trafficking. A Ministry of Labour and Judiciary guide for foreign workers states that "no employer may take or retain the passport of foreign workers." Limits: no statute expressly bans employers or recruiters from confiscating documents. The protection rests on the officials-only retention powers, the criminal offence of concealing documents, and the trafficking framework. It covers all migrants, regular or irregular.

### q29 · Right to birth registration.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Organic Law of the Supreme Electoral Tribunal and the Civil Registry (Ley Orgánica del Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones y del Registro Civil, N.º 3504), articles 48 to 50, and in the Children and Adolescents Code (Código de la Niñez y la Adolescencia, Ley N.º 7739), articles 3 and 23. Art. 48 of Ley 3504 states that "every birth that occurs in Costa Rican territory must be registered" with the Civil Registry. It makes no distinction by the nationality or migration status of the parents. Art. 49 obliges both parents to declare the birth, and failing them the person caring for the child, the head of the hospital or house where the birth occurred, or relatives. Art. 50 sets a one-month deadline and lets the declaration rest on the certificate of the doctor, midwife or nurse who attended the birth. People over ten years old are registered by resolution of the Civil Registry. Código de la Niñez art. 3 applies the Code to every minor "without any distinction" based on the nationality "or any other condition" of the child or the parents. Art. 23 gives every child the right to a name, a nationality and an identity document issued by the Civil Registry at the State's expense. The duty to register therefore covers children of regular and irregular migrants alike. One limit: the Registry's published procedure for late registration of home births asks a foreign mother to prove her entry and stay in Costa Rica at the time of the birth.

### q31 · Right to government document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 33(2) and 138; the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 37112-G), articles 174 and 175; and the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 36831-G, as amended by Decreto 44501-MGP of 2024), articles 54 and 55. Art. 138 of Ley 8764 gives the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería exclusive power to issue "identity and travel documents for foreign persons" and "individual or collective identification documents for foreign workers". The Regulation sets their form, content and validity. Art. 33(2) obliges foreigners to carry the document proving "their migration status in Costa Rica". Arts. 174 and 175 of the Reglamento de Extranjería govern the issue of the "documento de permanencia" (the DIMEX residence card) to permanent and temporary residents, including the "document that proves legal stay". For asylum seekers, art. 54 of the Refugee Regulation requires the Refugee Unit to issue a provisional document. It regularises their status for up to two years and gives "the possibility of carrying out paid work, self-employed or as an employee". Art. 55 states that the document identifies the holder before public and private bodies, protects against return, and is issued to minors as well. The documents record identity, migration category and, by class, the right to work. Irregular migrants have no document until they regularise.

### q33 · Right against destruction of document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Penal Code (Código Penal, Ley N.º 4573), article 369, read with articles 366 and 228. Art. 369 ("Suppression, concealment and destruction of documents") punishes anyone who "suppresses, conceals or destroys, in whole or in part, a document so that harm may result". The penalty is the one set for the corresponding falsification offence. For public and authentic documents, art. 366 sets one to six years' imprisonment, and two to eight years where a public official commits the act in the course of duty. Residence cards (DIMEX), refugee and asylum-seeker documents, work permits and passports issued by the DGME under Ley 8764 art. 138 are public documents, so their destruction falls under this provision. Art. 228 separately punishes anyone who "destroys, renders useless, makes disappear or damages in any way" a thing belonging wholly or partly to another. The Law against Trafficking in Persons (Ley N.º 9095), art. 6, also lists "the suppression, concealment and destruction of documents" among the criminal activities connected to trafficking. These offences apply to anyone, private or official, and protect all document holders, whatever their status.


## Liberty

### q15 · Right to communicate with consular officials.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this obligation in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31(7). Article 31 lists the rights of foreigners and must be read in line with ratified human-rights treaties. Its paragraph 7 provides that every foreigner subject to migration control and procedures "must be informed of the reasons for their apprehension, be offered the possibility of communication, including that required for consular assistance". It also guarantees access to a lawyer at the person's own expense, an interpreter where needed and full access to the administrative file. The Migration Control Regulation (Reglamento de Control Migratorio, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 36769-G) puts this into operation. Article 20 subjects anyone held in the Centro de Aprehensión to Law 8764, art. 31(5) and (7). Article 12(b) requires the Migration Police to escort a detained foreigner to their consular representation for identification and documentation. Article 31(5)(b) of the Law itself assumes the consulate's involvement: the 30-day limit on administrative detention starts once the consulate of the country of origin has identified the person. The guarantee covers all foreigners in migration proceedings, whatever their status, including irregular migrants held for verification or deportation. Outside migration proceedings, foreigners arrested on criminal charges rely on article 36 of the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations, which applies directly with supra-legal rank under article 7 of the Constitution. The Law frames the obligation as offering the possibility of consular communication. It does not impose automatic notification of the consulate, which fits asylum seekers, who may not wish to contact their state.

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Migration Control Regulation (Reglamento de Control Migratorio, Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 36769-G), article 20, read with the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 6(7) and 31(5). Article 20 allows the Professional Migration Police to apprehend foreigners for up to 24 hours to verify their migration status, "except minors, who shall be placed at the disposal of the Patronato Nacional de la Infancia". Children are therefore excluded from migration apprehension and handed to the child-protection agency. Article 14 adds that unaccompanied minors, or minors found in a situation of risk, must be accompanied by a migration officer until they are delivered to the PANI. Under article 6(7) of Law 8764, migration policy must guarantee the rights of migrant children in accordance with international conventions, with particular regard to their best interests. The Children and Adolescents Code (Código de la Niñez y la Adolescencia, Ley 7739) applies to every minor regardless of nationality or status. Limits: the exclusion is set by executive regulation, not by statute. Law 8764, art. 31(5)(c), still refers to detention conditions that respect "generational" specificities, and the Law contains no express statutory ban on detaining children with their families. The protection covers all migrant children, accompanied or not, regular or irregular.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) expressly permits detention on the basis of migration status. Article 31(5) states that foreigners may be detained only as provided by the Constitution and the laws. It then authorises limits on liberty "for the application of migration legislation", described as exceptional and subject to there being no less burdensome measure. Two forms are allowed. The first is precautionary apprehension of up to 24 hours to verify migration status, extendable in special situations by reasoned resolution of the Director General (art. 31(5)(a); see also art. 18(12) on Migration Police powers). The second is administrative detention pending deportation. Once the consulate of origin has identified the person, that detention may not exceed 30 calendar days, a limit the DGME may also extend in special and justified situations (art. 31(5)(b)). The DGME may open or close "centros de aprehensión" for foreigners facing deportation, and the Migration Police must transfer such persons to them. The Migration Control Regulation (Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 36769-G, arts. 5 and 20) defines administrative detention as the restriction of liberty of a foreigner who enters or stays irregularly, for as long as needed to carry out rejection, expulsion, deportation or regularisation. Safeguards exist: the measure must be exceptional, dignified conditions are required (art. 31(5)(c)), procedural rights apply (art. 31(7)), and detention can be challenged by habeas corpus before the Sala Constitucional. These safeguards limit migration detention but do not prohibit it for irregular migrants.

### q21 · Right to appeal legality, conditions, and length of detention
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right as it applies to legality, conditions and length of detention in: 1) Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica), art. 48, which gives "every person" the right to habeas corpus "to guarantee their personal liberty and integrity". Art. 19 extends constitutional rights to foreigners. 2) Constitutional Jurisdiction Act (Ley de la Jurisdicción Constitucional, N.º 7135), art. 15. Habeas corpus lies against acts or omissions of "any authority" and against "disturbances or restrictions" that authorities "unduly" impose on liberty and personal integrity. Through integrity, the action covers detention conditions. Art. 16 extends the action to connected violations, and under art. 26 a successful petition voids the measure and awards damages. 3) General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764). Art. 31(1) guarantees every foreigner access to justice and due process. Art. 31(5) sets the reviewable limits: precautionary apprehension of at most 24 hours, administrative detention of at most 30 days after consular identification, and placement in a place guaranteeing "dignified and respectful treatment". Under arts. 194 and 211, an apprehension order is a precautionary measure that may be appealed to the Administrative Migration Tribunal (Tribunal Administrativo Migratorio) within three working days. The appeal does not suspend the measure. Under art. 229, when a detained person files any appeal, the DGME (Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería) must decide whether to keep the apprehension or replace it with a less burdensome measure. These remedies cover all foreigners, whatever their status.

### q23 · Right to an individualized detention decision.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), arts. 31(5), 211 and 229, read with the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), arts. 19 and 37. Art. 31(5) allows foreigners to be detained only as the Constitution and the law provide. For migration purposes, liberty may be restricted only "under exceptional assumptions and conditions, provided that no other less burdensome measure exists". After the 24-hour precautionary apprehension, the DGME may open deportation proceedings only once the infraction is "confirmed" and "another less burdensome measure" has been ruled out. Extending the 24-hour or 30-day limits requires a "justified resolution" in each case. Art. 211 makes apprehension one of several precautionary measures the DGME "may" order in the individual proceeding. The others are periodic reporting, bond, temporary seizure of documents and house arrest. Art. 211 also makes each measure appealable under art. 194. Art. 229 requires the DGME, whenever a detained person files an appeal, to decide whether to keep the apprehension or replace it with a less burdensome measure. Read together, these provisions require a case-by-case necessity and proportionality decision rather than automatic detention. They apply to all foreigners subject to migration control, including irregular migrants. Under art. 31(9), asylum seekers are also protected from removal.

### q25 · Right to be free from torture and inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in: 1) Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica), art. 40: "No one shall be subjected to cruel or degrading treatment", and any statement obtained through violence is void. Art. 19 grants foreigners the same individual rights as Costa Ricans, and art. 48 protects personal integrity through habeas corpus. 2) Penal Code (Código Penal, Ley N.º 4573), art. 381 bis, added by Ley 10213 of 2022 to replace the repealed art. 123 bis. It punishes torture with three to fifteen years' imprisonment. Its listed grounds of discrimination expressly include "migratory status", and it covers officials who order, instigate or fail to prevent torture. 3) General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764). Art. 31(5)(c) entitles detained foreigners to a place guaranteeing "dignified and respectful treatment" that accounts for gender, age and disability. Art. 31(7) guarantees "dignified and adequate treatment" on apprehension. Art. 7(6) commits the State to provide asylum to anyone facing a danger of torture. 4) The Convention against Torture and the American Convention on Human Rights (art. 5) have supra-legal rank under Const. art. 7 and are enforceable through habeas corpus (Ley 7135, art. 15). The protections cover all migrants regardless of status.


## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. National law does not expressly prohibit officers or agents from using force as a means of preventing migrants from entering the territory. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) provides that the Professional Migration Police is a specialised body of the Fuerza Pública governed by the General Police Law (art. 15), and requires it to carry out migration control at entry and exit 'with full respect for human rights'. It also authorises officers to refuse entry and order rejection (rechazo) of foreigners who do not meet entry requirements or who are caught evading migration control or entering through unauthorised points (arts. 44 and 64). The General Police Law (Ley General de Policía, N.º 7410) sets a general rule for all police forces, including the Border Police and the Migration Police: they must 'use force only when strictly necessary and to the extent required for the performance of their functions' (art. 10(d)). This is a general necessity and proportionality standard for police action, not a prohibition on using force to prevent migrants from entering. Neither law nor its regulations contains a specific ban on force at the border or against people trying to enter. Constitutional guarantees of life and against cruel treatment (Political Constitution, arts. 21 and 40) apply to everyone but do not address border enforcement specifically.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. No statute or regulation expressly prohibits officers or agents from using lethal force to prevent migrants from entering the territory. The Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica) declares that 'human life is inviolable' (art. 21) and forbids cruel or degrading treatment (art. 40). These are general guarantees applying to everyone and are not specific to border control. The General Police Law (Ley General de Policía, N.º 7410), which governs all police forces including the Border Police and the Professional Migration Police, allows force 'only when strictly necessary and to the extent required for the performance of their functions' (art. 10(d)). It does not set out a specific rule on firearms or lethal force against people trying to enter the country. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764) places the Migration Police under Ley 7410 (art. 15) and regulates rejection at the border (arts. 44, 64), but it contains no rule on lethal force. As in the MRD's Mexico coding, a general constitutional protection of life without a specific prohibition tied to entry enforcement is coded as No.

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: Yes, the national law provides exemption
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Law against Trafficking in Persons and creating CONATT (Ley contra la Trata de Personas y Creación de la Coalición Nacional contra el Tráfico Ilícito de Migrantes y la Trata de Personas, N.º 9095), art. 70. Under the heading 'No punibilidad', it provides that 'victims of the crime of trafficking in persons are not punishable, criminally or administratively, for the commission of misdemeanours or crimes committed during the execution of the crime of trafficking and as a consequence of it'. The same law gives foreign victims migration protection, including the right to remain in the country and to receive documentation under Ley 8764, fee-free migration documents, and voluntary, safe return. It also requires the Immediate Response Team (ERI) to coordinate migration protection measures for non-national victims (art. 24(f)). For smuggled migrants, the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), art. 249, criminalises only the person who 'conducts or transports persons' to enter or leave the country evading migration controls, or who facilitates false documents or harbours them. The migrant is treated as the object of the offence, and penalties rise when the migrant is a child or their life is endangered. Irregular entry or stay is not a crime: it leads to administrative consequences such as rejection, deportation or a migration fine (arts. 33(3), 64). The exemption is express for trafficking victims. For smuggled migrants it follows from their non-criminalisation rather than from an express clause.


## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Convictions do not affect citizenship status
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), article 16, as amended by Law No. 7514 of 1995: "Costa Rican status is not lost and cannot be renounced." The rule covers Costa Ricans by naturalisation as well as by birth. The loss grounds in the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley de Opciones y Naturalizaciones, Ley N.º 1155) used to include acquiring another nationality and a naturalised citizen's absence of more than six years (art. 3). Those grounds, and the related arts. 4–5, were tacitly repealed by the 1995 constitutional reform, as the official annotated text records. No provision allows a naturalised person to be stripped of Costa Rican nationality for a criminal or administrative conviction handed down after naturalisation. Criminal records matter at the application stage. Art. 11(4) of Ley 1155 requires that the applicant has not been tried for intentional crimes during their stay. Art. 15, as reformed by Law No. 10508 (2024), bars naturalisation for certain serious offences and for intentional crimes punished by more than three years in the past ten years. The only route to undoing a naturalisation is art. 18 (also reformed by Law 10508). It makes a naturalisation "null by operation of law" if it was obtained fraudulently, for example where the person "supplied any false information" or "had been convicted before" of an art. 15 offence when applying. The Civil Registry rules on annulment after hearing the person, with appeal to the Supreme Electoral Tribunal. This is a nullity tied to the original grant, which the indicator excludes. A later conviction does not reach citizenship. A 2023–24 bill (expediente 23.800) to strip nationality from naturalised citizens linked to organised crime was criticised as unconstitutional under art. 16. It was enacted only as the eligibility and nullity reform in Law 10508.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), articles 14 and 15. It also does so in the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley de Opciones y Naturalizaciones, Ley N.º 1155), articles 2 and 11, and in the TSE Regulation on naturalisation procedures (Reglamento relativo a los trámites, requisitos y criterios de resolución en materia de naturalizaciones, Decreto TSE n.º 12-2012), articles 8, 9 and 11. Art. 14 makes Costa Ricans by naturalisation of nationals by birth of other Central American countries, Spaniards and Ibero-Americans "who have officially resided in the country for five years". It does the same for all other foreigners "who have officially resided in the country for at least seven years", in both cases subject to the requirements set by law. Under art. 15 and art. 11 of Ley 1155, the applicant must show adulthood, good conduct, a known profession or means of living, and Spanish literacy. They must also pass a test on Costa Rican history and values, and have no conviction for intentional crimes during their stay. They swear to respect the constitutional order, promise to keep residing in the country, and renounce their prior nationality unless a dual-nationality treaty applies. Under Reglamento art. 8(d), the application uses the identity document issued by the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería "by virtue of the grant of any migratory category". Official residence is proven through migration-movement records and witnesses (art. 11). Time spent as a lawful temporary or permanent resident, including on a work-based permit, therefore counts. Applications go to the Civil Registry's Section of Options and Naturalisations, with consultation to or appeal before the Supreme Electoral Tribunal (Ley 1155 art. 14). The procedure is free of charge. Ley 1155 art. 15 (reformed by Ley 10508, 2024) excludes people with recent serious convictions.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), article 14, and in the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley de Opciones y Naturalizaciones, Ley N.º 1155), articles 11 and 15 (as reformed by Law No. 10508, 2024). It also does so in the TSE Regulation on naturalisation procedures (Reglamento relativo a los trámites, requisitos y criterios de resolución en materia de naturalizaciones, Decreto TSE n.º 12-2012), articles 8(d) and 73–76. Recognised refugees hold an identity document issued by the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería. They can apply under the general residence route after five years of official residence (Central Americans, Spaniards and Ibero-Americans by birth) or seven years (others). The Reglamento accepts a DGME document issued "by virtue of the grant of any migratory category". The 2024 reform of art. 15 of Ley 1155 shows that refugees are expected to naturalise. It exempts from the criminal-record bar "foreign persons recognised as refugees" whose earlier conviction was known to the Commission on Restricted Visas and Refuge (Comisión de Visas Restringidas y Refugio) or the Administrative Migration Tribunal when refuge was granted. A parallel exemption covers stateless persons. Since Decreto TSE 2-2017, a faster route exists for people declared stateless or "stateless refugees" (refugiado apátrida). They need only "have officially resided in Costa Rica for two years", counted from the date they applied for that status, plus good conduct and no art. 15 conviction (Reglamento arts. 73–74, 76). The pathway does not cover asylum seekers whose claims are pending, or holders of the 2022–2026 special temporary categories, unless and until that time is accepted as official residence. Ordinary refugees must also meet the general requirements, including a legalised or apostilled birth certificate from the country of origin.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), articles 14(5) and 17. It also does so in the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley de Opciones y Naturalizaciones, Ley N.º 1155), and in the TSE Regulation on naturalisation procedures (Reglamento relativo a los trámites, requisitos y criterios de resolución en materia de naturalizaciones, Decreto TSE n.º 12-2012), articles 23–24 and 36 ff. Art. 14(5), as amended by Law 7879 of 1999, makes Costa Ricans by naturalisation of foreign persons who lose their nationality on marrying a Costa Rican. It also covers those who, "after having been married to Costa Ricans for two years, and having resided for that same period in the country, express their wish to acquire Costa Rican nationality". The Sala Constitucional (Res. 3435-92) reads the clause gender-neutrally. Under Reglamento art. 24, the applicant must show adulthood and two years of marriage to a Costa Rican, registered with the Civil Registry. They must also show two years' residence in Costa Rica after the marriage and no conviction for an offence in art. 15 of Ley 1155. This is much shorter than the five-to-seven-year residence route. Art. 17 of the Constitution provides that "acquisition of nationality extends to minor children" under the law. The Reglamento regulates "naturalisation by transcendence of either parent" (art. 4(j), arts. 36 ff.), so a minor can take nationality through a naturalised parent. Ley 1155 art. 11 b gives a special simplified route to people over 25 born in Costa Rica to foreign parents. Children born abroad to a Costa Rican parent may opt for nationality by birth (Ley 1155 art. 1). Proceedings are free and handled by the Civil Registry, with review by the Supreme Electoral Tribunal. Sham marriages are excluded because the grant follows the validity of the registered marriage.

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: Conditional, depending on the country of origin or some other consideration
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right only conditionally, in the Law on Options and Naturalisation (Ley de Opciones y Naturalizaciones, Ley N.º 1155 of 1950), article 11, last paragraph, as amended by Ley N.º 7234 of 1991. Article 11 lists the requirements for naturalising before the Civil Registry. It then requires the applicant to swear to respect the constitutional order, to state in writing that he will keep residing regularly and stably in the country, and "at the same time, to express in the same manner that he renounces his nationality, except in the case of nationals of countries with which dual-nationality treaties exist." Whether a naturalising migrant may keep the nationality of origin therefore depends on the country of origin. Nationals of states that have a dual-nationality treaty with Costa Rica are exempt from the renunciation. All other applicants must file a written renunciation. Its effect in the state of origin depends on that state's own law, but Costa Rican law requires it as a condition of naturalisation. The Political Constitution (Constitución Política, 1949), article 16, as amended by Ley N.º 7514 of 1995, says that "the quality of Costa Rican is not lost and cannot be renounced". That provision protects Costa Ricans who acquire another nationality. It does not lift the renunciation requirement that article 11 of Ley 1155 places on foreigners who naturalise. Article 14 of the Constitution sets the residence periods for naturalisation (five years for Central Americans, Spaniards and Ibero-Americans by birth, seven years for other foreigners, and two years of marriage plus residence for spouses of Costa Ricans) and leaves the other requirements to the law.

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to regularize after certain conditions are met
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, Ley N.º 8764 of 2009), articles 6(1), 7(6), 93 and 94, and in special regularisation regimes adopted under them. Article 6(1) says that migration policy "shall promote the regularisation and integration of immigrant communities in Costa Rican society". Article 7(6) commits the State to "the necessary facilitation of regularisation processes for persons who are in the national territory". Article 93 empowers the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería (DGME) to authorise entry and stay "through special migration categories, in order to regulate migration situations that, by their nature, require different treatment". Article 94 lists such categories, including temporary workers and workers in specific occupations. Under these powers the Executive and the DGME have opened time-limited regularisation channels. One is the exceptional regime for workers in the agricultural, agro-export and agro-industrial sectors (Decreto Ejecutivo N.º 42406-MAG-MGP of 2020, reformed by Decreto N.º 44281 of 2023), which gives qualifying workers who were already in the country a one-year special category. The other is DGME Resolution DG-0064-05-2026, the Complementary Special Temporary Category (Categoría Especial Temporal Complementaria), published in La Gaceta N.º 98 of 29 May 2026. It is open from 1 September 2026 to 1 September 2027 to Nicaraguan, Venezuelan, Cuban and Colombian nationals who filed asylum claims between 1 June 2014 and 7 May 2026 and whose claims are pending, manifestly unfounded or finally denied. Applicants must have stayed in the country continuously. The category runs for renewable two-year terms and authorises any paid work. Article 73 also lets foreign spouses of Costa Ricans regularise their stay. These routes are conditional and limited by nationality, sector or application window, and there is no general standing regularisation mechanism for all irregular workers.


## Opinion and expression

### q83 · Right to freedom to hold opinion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), articles 19 and 28, and in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31. Article 28 provides that "no one may be disturbed or persecuted for the expression of their opinions or for any act that does not infringe the law", and that private actions which do not harm morality, public order or third parties are "outside the action of the law". The guarantee is framed for every person ("nadie"), not for citizens only. Article 19 extends to foreigners the same individual rights as Costa Ricans. Its proviso that foreigners "may not intervene in the political affairs of the country" restricts political action, not the inner freedom to hold opinions, so holding opinions is protected without interference for all migrants, regular or irregular. Ley 8764, article 31, confirms that foreigners enjoy the individual rights and guarantees recognised for Costa Ricans and that these are to be interpreted according to ratified human-rights treaties. Those treaties include ICCPR article 19(1), which Costa Rica applies directly. The right to hold opinions is thus commensurate with that of citizens.

### q85 · Right to freedom of expression.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica. The Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), article 29, provides that "everyone may communicate their thoughts orally or in writing and publish them without prior censorship", subject to liability for abuses as defined by law. Article 19 gives foreigners the same individual rights as Costa Ricans, but expressly provides that foreigners "may not intervene in the political affairs of the country". That constitutional bar has no counterpart for citizens. It removes from foreigners the protection to express themselves on, and campaign in, national political matters, which citizens fully enjoy. The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31, grants foreigners the rights of Costa Ricans only "save the limitations" set by the Constitution, so it carries the art. 19 bar forward. According to TSE-published doctrine, the Sala Constitucional grounds the bar on national sovereignty. The bar has mostly been applied to electoral participation (vote, party financing), but its wording reaches political affairs generally. In the MRD's approach, a constitutional ban on foreigners intervening in political affairs (as in Mexico's art. 33) means freedom of expression is not commensurate with citizens'. Non-political expression is protected for all migrants.


## Peaceful assembly and association

### q87 · Right to freedom of association.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica commensurate with citizens. The Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), article 25, gives all "inhabitants of the Republic" the right to associate for lawful ends. Article 60 lets employers and workers unionise freely. However, article 60 adds that "foreigners are prohibited from exercising direction or authority in trade unions". The Labour Code (Código de Trabajo, Ley N.º 2), article 345(e), implements this by requiring that members of a union's board of directors "be Costa Ricans or foreigners married to Costa Ricans with at least five years of permanent residence in the country". For this purpose only Central Americans by origin are treated as Costa Ricans. Migrant workers may therefore join unions, and Labour Code article 404 bans discrimination at work on grounds including "national ancestry" and union affiliation. But migrants, other than Central Americans by origin and long-resident foreign spouses of Costa Ricans, cannot hold union leadership. The restriction is constitutional, so ordinary equality guarantees (Const. arts. 19 and 33; Ley 8764 art. 31) cannot override it. As in the MRD's coding of Mexico, a bar on foreigners holding union leadership means freedom of association, including trade-union rights, is not commensurate with that of citizens.

### q89 · Right to freedom of assembly.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Costa Rica commensurate with citizens. The Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), article 26, provides that "everyone has the right to meet peacefully and unarmed, whether for private business or to discuss political affairs and examine the public conduct of officials". Meetings in private premises need no prior authorisation, and those in public places are regulated by law. Article 26 itself thus treats political discussion as a core purpose of assembly. Article 19, however, while granting foreigners the same individual rights as Costa Ricans, provides that foreigners "may not intervene in the political affairs of the country". The General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31, recognises foreigners' constitutional rights only "save the limitations" the Constitution sets, so the art. 19 bar applies. Migrants, regular or irregular, may assemble peacefully for private, social, cultural or religious purposes. But the constitutional order denies them the political-assembly component that article 26 guarantees to citizens. This parallels the MRD's coding of Mexico, where assembly on political matters is reserved to citizens. The right of peaceful assembly is therefore not commensurate with that of citizens.


## Thought, conscience, and religion or belief

### q81 · Right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Political Constitution of Costa Rica (Constitución Política de la República de Costa Rica, 1949), articles 19, 28, 33 and 75, and in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31. Article 75 establishes Roman Catholicism as the religion of the State but guarantees "the free exercise in the Republic of other cults that are not opposed to universal morality or good customs". The guarantee is not limited to nationals. Article 19 gives foreigners "the same individual and social duties and rights as Costa Ricans", subject only to exceptions set by the Constitution and the laws. None of those exceptions concerns thought, conscience or religion. Article 28 protects everyone from being persecuted for their opinions or for any act that does not break the law. Its ban on political propaganda that invokes religion applies equally to clergy and lay people, whatever their nationality. Article 33 declares everyone equal before the law and forbids discrimination contrary to human dignity. Ley 8764, article 31, confirms that foreigners enjoy the individual and social rights recognised for Costa Ricans, to be read in line with ratified human-rights treaties, and article 7(4) directs migration policy to respect migrants' customs and diversity. The protection therefore covers all migrants, regular or irregular, commensurate with citizens. The confessional character of the State applies to nationals and foreigners alike and does not create a migrant-specific limitation.


## Victims of crime

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Code (Código Procesal Penal, Ley 7594), articles 70, 71, 72 and 75, read with the Political Constitution (Constitución Política), article 19, and the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), article 31(1). Article 70 defines victims as "the person directly offended by the crime" and their relatives, with no reference to nationality or migration status. Article 71 gives every victim procedural rights "even if not constituted as querellante". These include dignified treatment, consideration of social, cultural or ethnic differences, information on their rights, access to the file, the civil damages action and the private complaint. Under article 75, in public-action offences "the victim... may provoke the criminal prosecution, join that already initiated by the Ministerio Público or continue its exercise" as querellante. Article 72 lets "every person with civil capacity" file a complaint for private-action offences. Article 19 of the Constitution and article 31(1) of Ley 8764 guarantee foreigners, regardless of status, the same rights as nationals and "access to justice". The Law on Protection of Victims, Witnesses and Other Participants in Criminal Proceedings (Ley 8720) provides protection measures for all victims. The Law against Trafficking in Persons (Ley 9095), article 2(a), requires its protections to apply without discrimination by "nationality... or any other social or migratory condition." Article 37 of that law adds a reflection period of at least three months and free legal assistance for trafficking victims.

### q45 · Right of victims of crime to immigration status or relief from expulsion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 94(5), 94(10) and 107, and in the Law against Trafficking in Persons (Ley contra la Trata de Personas, N.º 9095), articles 37(i)–(j) and 70. Article 94 lists among the special migration categories: (5) "complainants or witnesses in judicial or administrative proceedings"; and (10) "victims of trafficking in persons or surviving children of femicide victims". Paragraph 10 was amended by Ley 10263 of 2022. Article 94(12) lets the DGME create further categories for humanitarian reasons. Article 107 provides that the DGME "may grant temporary stay to victims of trafficking in persons, upon accreditation and recommendation of the Equipo de Respuesta Inmediata". Article 37(i) of Ley 9095 gives trafficking victims "migration protection, including the right to remain in the country... and to receive the documentation that accredits such circumstance". Article 37(j) exempts them from fees for that documentation. Article 37(f) grants a reflection period of at least three months, and protection does not depend on cooperating with the investigation (art. 2(b)). Article 70 makes trafficking victims not punishable, criminally or administratively, for offences committed as a result of the trafficking. The avenue is limited to trafficking victims, surviving children of femicide victims, and complainants or witnesses in proceedings. Other crime victims have no specific relief from deportation, except through the DGME's discretionary humanitarian category.


## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 6(7), 11, 31(5)(c) and (7), 33 and 65; the Children and Adolescents Code (Código de la Niñez y la Adolescencia, Ley N.º 7739), articles 3 and 17; the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo 37112-G), articles 33, 43 and 140-141; and the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G), articles 10 and 47. Article 6(7) of Ley 8764 makes it state migration policy to guarantee the rights of migrant children under the international conventions, and states that "the best interest of these persons shall be especially taken into account". Under article 65, unaccompanied minors, and people whose majority is uncertain, may not be refused entry "under any circumstances". Border authorities must inform the Patronato Nacional de la Infancia (PANI), which takes over temporary representation and places the child in a shelter. Article 11 requires PANI to take part in the Migration Council whenever a minor is involved. Article 33 exempts minors from migration fees. Detention must respect "generational" and age-specific needs (art. 31). Ley 7739 applies to every child regardless of the nationality of the child or the parents (art. 3). Article 17 requires migration law to be applied "in safeguarding the interest" of foreign minors. Reglamento 37112-G creates a special category, renewable for as long as PANI keeps legal representation, for foreign children in PANI care who cannot obtain another status (art. 140). It also applies the best-interest principle to documentation (art. 33). Reglamento 36831-G bars the apprehension of children seeking asylum, whether accompanied, unaccompanied or separated, and requires alternatives to detention (art. 47). It also requires a differentiated approach for unaccompanied minors (art. 10). These protections cover all migrant children, whatever their status.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 3, 31(5)(c) and (7), 94(10) and 106(1), and in the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G), articles 4, 6, 10 and 11. Article 3 of Ley 8764 bases migration control and integration on "gender equity" among other principles. Detained foreigners are entitled to a place that guarantees dignified treatment respecting "the specificities of gender" (art. 31(5)(c)). On apprehension, treatment must take account of "the specificities by reason of gender, age, disability and others" (art. 31(7)). Article 94(10), as amended by the Comprehensive Reparation Law for Femicide Survivors (Ley N.º 10263 of 2022), gives a special migration category to victims of trafficking and to surviving children of femicide victims. Gender is a ground of persecution in the refugee definition (art. 106(1); Reglamento art. 4). Reglamento 36831-G requires authorities to apply a "differentiated approach" to "women and men victims of gender-based violence" at every stage, from entry to integration (art. 10). Article 11 obliges migration officials, especially those in the Refugee Sub-process, to apply a "Gender Approach" based on sex and gender variables. Most of these protections are found in the asylum framework and in the rules on detention and apprehension. Outside trafficking and femicide cases, there is no stand-alone residence category for migrant women who are victims of domestic violence. Such cases depend on the discretionary humanitarian category in art. 94(12).

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Costa Rica protects this right in the General Law on Migration and Foreigners (Ley General de Migración y Extranjería, N.º 8764), articles 4, 31(5)(c) and (7), and 33; the Immigration Regulation (Reglamento de Extranjería, Decreto Ejecutivo 37112-G), articles 2 (definitions), 6 and 43; and the Regulation on Refugees (Reglamento de Personas Refugiadas, Decreto Ejecutivo 36831-G), articles 47, 62, 64 and 87. Detained foreigners are entitled to conditions that respect "the specificities of... disability" (art. 31(5)(c)), and on apprehension treatment must account for "gender, age, disability and others" (art. 31(7)). Article 33 of Ley 8764 (as reformed by Ley 10493 of 2024) exempts "adults with disabilities" from the US$25 and US$5 migration fees; Reglamento article 43 does the same. The Reglamento's definition of "dependant" covers children and siblings "of age with a disability" with no age limit. Disabled adult relatives can therefore obtain residence as dependants, where able-bodied adult children over 25 cannot. Reglamento article 6 lets an applicant whose disability prevents signing use a fingerprint, or have officials record "NO FIRMA" with two witnesses. In the asylum system, the special coordination procedure that replaces detention also applies to persons with disabilities (art. 47). Their document fees may be waived (art. 62). Relatives with disabilities qualify for refugee family reunification outside the ordinary categories (art. 64), and refugees with a Costa Rican sibling or child with a disability may seek permanent residence (art. 87). These protections apply to migrants generally, to regular residents and their dependants, and to asylum seekers and refugees.
