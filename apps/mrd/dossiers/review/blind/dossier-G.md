# Dossier G — Sweden


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The national law in Sweden protects the right to vote in local elections in chapter 1 sections 7 and 8 of the Local Government Act (2017:725) but only for those who have been registered as having their domicile in Sweden in the population registry for a period of at least three years preceding the election day. Immigrants with pending applications do not have a right to be registered, even if they are legally present in Sweden, i.e. asylum seekers with ongoing investigations. The right to vote in local or regional elections is available to all citizens of EU states that have their domicile in their local or regional entity. The Instrument of Government (1974:152) chapter 3 section 4 states that only Swedish citizens have the right to vote in national elections.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in Sweden protects the right to stand for election at the local and regional level, only for certain categories of migrants. The Instrument of Government (1974:152) chapter 3, section 4 states that only Swedish citizens have the right to stand for election in national elections. According to the Local Government Act (2017:725) chapter 4, section 3, only migrants who have been registered in the population registry for a period of at least 3 years (in other words, only those who have a right to vote at the local and regional elections) can vote for public office at the local/regional level.


## Due process

- Law: Yes (applies to all three)
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in the Constitution, The Instrument of Government (1974:152) chapter 1 section 9.

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 18 sections 1 and 1a.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Code of Judicial Procedure (1942:740) chapter 5 §6; and Chapter 23, section 16.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 20 section 4 criminalizes unauthorized entry into Sweden across an external border according to the codex on the Schengen borders. Unauthorized entry through an internal border according to the Schengen borders is on the contrary not criminalized and there are no civil sanctions for this.

Even though there is no requirement related to migration status or residence, the Higher Ordinance Education Act imposes a language requirement and application & tuition fees on migrants from non-EEA countries.

## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: Only major convictions (e.g. criminal offenses) can lead to loss of residence status.
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Sweden regulates the ability of migrants' criminal and other convictions affecting residence status in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 17, chapter 7 section 6 and chapter 8a section 1, 4-7. The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 17 requires that convictions are considered when considering an application for residence permit. According to the legislative history only criminal convictions of a certain dignity should be considered, but in case law from the Supreme Migration Court (MIG 2019:23), less serious but repeated offenses can also be considered.

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Sweden bans refoulement on the basis of a risk of return to torture or cruel, inuman or degrading treatment or punishment in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 12 sections 1-3; Chapter 1, section 6a and Chapter 4, section 2 of the Aliens Act; and The Act (1994:1219) on The European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms article 3.

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Sweden bans refoulement on the basis of a risk of return to persecution based on race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion in Chapter 12, section 2 and Chapter 4, section 1 of the Aliens Act (2005:716).

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Sweden bans refoulment on the basis of a risk of serious deprivations of fundamental human rights in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 12 section 1.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Sweden bans expulsion to any country where a migrant is likely to be subjected to refoulement through a subsequent expulsion in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 12 section 1-2.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Administrative Procedure Act (2017:900) section 5, The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 8 and 8a, The Act (2022:666) on Terror Crimes and The Act (2022:700) on special control over certain aliens.

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 13 sections 1-3 and The Administrative Procedure Act (2017:900) sections 23 and 25.


## Family

### q73 · Right of settled migrants to family reunification.
- Law: Conditional
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Sweden provides settled migrants with legal avenues to facilitate reunification with immediate or non-dependent family members in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 sections 3 and 3a, with conditions in section 3b. The Swedish legislation on family reunification is based on the European Council Directive 2003/86/EC of 22 September 2003 on the right to family reunification. There are criteria which must be met in relation to support obligations and which family members are accepted.

### q75 · Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden provides judicial remedy to appeal the refusal by the authorities to allow family formation/reunification in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 14 sections 1 and 3.

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden recognizes same sex relationships under the family reunification framework in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 sections 3 and 3a. The Marriage Code, Chapter 1, section 1, is neutral on the subject of the gender of the persons that are married or cohabitants, but in the preparatory legal documents, (prop. 2020/21:191 s. 87), the legislator states that the expressions husband/wife and cohabitant refers to both couples of opposite and same sex. This was the practice even before the amendment to the law made in 2020.

### q79 · Right to family reunification without resource conditions
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protections of this right. Chapter 5, code section 3 b of the Aliens Act restricts the right to family reunification for some categories of migrants by imposing requirements on income and accommodation. The Swedish legislation on family reunification is based on the European Council Directive 2003/86/EC of 22 September 2003 on the right to family reunification. The legal provisions states that residence permit shall be given to a number of immediate and non-depedent family members. The legal provisions are conditional in the sense that The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 3b states that residence permit according to chapter 5 sections 3 and 3a may only be granted if the alien residing in Sweden is able to financially support themselves and the alien applicant and has housing of approprate size and standard for themselves and the alien applicant. However, according to The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 3c the conditions in section 3b does not apply if the alien residing in Sweden is a child or if they are a citizen of Sweden, another EESstate or Switzerland and the alien applicant is their husband/wife or cohabitans partner and the alien residing in Sweden and the alien applicant has been cohabiting abroad for a longer period of time or their relationship in another way is well-established. According to Aliens Act, chapter 5, section 3b, migrants that are given refugee status are given the benefit of not being required to be able to financially support family member, if an application for reunification is made within three months of being granted refugee status.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The Act (2001:82) on Swedish citizenship offers certain privileges to stateless persons and particularly stateless children in relation to obtaining citizenship,for example in relation to establishing identity and duration of residency requirements, but it does not guarantee citizenship.


## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right for regular migrants in the Health and Medical Services Act (2017:30) chapter 8 section 2. Emergency medical care is available to all migrants, independent of status, and persons under 18 years of age have the same right to medical care as those legally residing in the region, as mentioned in The Act (2008:344) on health- and medical care for asylumseekers et.al. sections 4, 5 and 6 and the Act (2013:407) on health and medical care to certain aliens residing in Sweden without neccessary permits, sections 5, 6 and 7.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in Social Services Act (2001:453) chapter 2 section 1, chapter 2a section 1 and chapter 4 section 1 and 3. This right is also protected in the Act on Reception of Asylum seekers, code section 3.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Education Act (2010:800) chapter 7 section 2 and 3 and chapter 29 section 2. Chapter 7 section 2 states that children residing in Sweden are obliged to attend school and chapter 7 section 3 states that all children who are obliged to attend school have the right to free primary education in a public shool. Chapter 29 section 2 states that a child is to be considered residing in Sweden if they should be registered in the population register. The National Registration Act (1991:481) regulates the population register and section 3 of the act states that a person who has moved to Sweden is conidered to be a resident if they are presumed to regularly spend their resting hours in the country for at least one year. An alien who is given a permit to reside in Sweden for a period of at least one year can therefore be registered in the population register and have te same right to primary education as Swedish citizens. The Education Act (2010:800) chapter 29 section 2 also lists a number of categories of migrant statuses that, when applying the act, should be considered residents in Sweden and enjoy the same rights. Notably, this also applies to asylum-seekers and those staying in Sweden irregularly.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No universal protection of this right for all migrants due to the lack of legal regulation of wages in general, even if the Discrimination Act (2008:567) chapter 2, section 1 prohibits employers from discriminating against their employees on the basis of belonging to a minority group.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Employment is tied to specific employer; change of employer possible, but requires new work permit application.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 6. Section 2a of the Act indicates fewer restrictions after two years. Furthermore, aliens with a permanent residence permit (the Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 6 section 1) and certain asylum seekers have free choice of employment.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: Yes, but limited (e.g. by sector or occupation)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Sweden protects this right in a limited way in the Aliens Ordinance Act (2006:97) chapter 4, section 4a. When an alien applies for work permit they can apply for residence permit for family members at the same time. According to the Act, residence permit should be granted for the same period as the main applicant. If the period for the work permit for the main applicant is more than six months, the family member can also be given a work permit.

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment implies loss of immigration status unless a new job is found within a specified time period.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in the Aliens Ordinance Act (2006:97) chapter 5 section 3.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

The national law in Sweden protects this right in the Discrimination Act (2008: 567) chapter 2, section 1 which prohibits employers from discriminating against their employees and those seeking employment. There is no legal requirement to provide most benefits due to the Swedish system of delegating the terms of employment to the parties of the labor market (employers, employees and their representatives). However there are some benefits like the Annual Leave Act (1977:480) section 1 and 2 that applies to all. The Annual Leave Act (1977:480) section 1 and 2 states that an employee has the right to paid vacation according to the law and that this right cannot be waived through a contract between the employee and the employer. There are additional labor laws that make some work benefits mandatory.The Discrimination Act would prohibit an employer to differentiate between between citizens and migrants regarding work conditions and protection.

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Equal access to all types of social security protections after some period of time.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects migrant workers’ rights to social security protections in The Social Insurance Code (2010:110) chapter 5 sections 2, 3, and 12 as well as chapter 6 section 2.

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: Equal access to all types of unemployment benefits after a duration of stay.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Act (1997:238) on Unemployment Insurance section 1 and 12. The Act (1997:238) on Unemployment is neutral regarding the migration status of the individual, meaning that immigrants qualify the same way as citizens. There is a qualification period before benefits are granted; 6 moths of work during a 12 mont period, but this applies regardless of migration and citizenship status.

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are met.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 3a section 3 and chapter 5 section 5. The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 3a section 3 states that an EEA-citizen has a right to reside in Sweden if they are workers or seeking employment. After five years of lawful residency they have a permanent right to reside in Sweden. Chapter 5 section 5 applies to all other aliens than EEA-citizens. The legal provision states that a permanent residence permit can be given to an alien who have had a work permit for a total of four years during a period of seven years preceding the application.

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 5. Any migrant with a temporary work authorized visa has the ability to access permanent residence eventually assuming certain conditions are met.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, same as citizens.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Labour Disputes (Judicial Procedure) Act (1974:371), The Code of Judicial Procedure (1942:740) chapter 11 section 1 and The Instrument of Government (1974:152) chapter 1 section 9.

### q135 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of race or ethnicity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Sweden prohibits discrimination against migrants on the grounds of race or ethnicity in The Instrument of Government (1974:152) chapter 2 section 12 and The Discrimination Act (2008:567) chapter 1 sections 1, 2, 4, 5 and chapter 2.

## Liberty

### q15 · Right to communicate with consular officials.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this obligation in The Act (1989:152) on "Obligation to Notify etc. when Aliens are Deprived of their Liberty" (Lag (1989:152) om underrättelseskyldighet m.m. när utlänningar är berövade friheten), sections 1 and 2.

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No universal protection of this right. The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 10 section 2 states when a migrant child can be detained. There is no explicit rules prohibiting the detention of children based on their status as migrants. Children wihout guardians has stronger protection against being detained as there is a provision stating that this should only be allowed in exceptional cicumstances, The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 10 section 3. A provision stating for how long a child can be detained is in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 10 section 5.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

National law in Sweden does not prohibit the detention of migrants on the basis of their status as a migrant. However, the circumstances in which migrants can be detained are strictly limited (the primary permitted reasons are determination of identity and execution of a decision on expulsion) but most forms of migrant detention are prohibited under the Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 10 section 1.

### q21 · Right to appeal legality, conditions, and length of detention
- Law: Yes (applies to legality and conditions of detention)
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National Law in Sweden protects this right in relation to legality in The Aliens act (2005:716) chapter 14 § 9; it protects this right in relation to conditions in The Aliens act (2005:716) chapter 14 § 10. Decisions on detention are not defined in length, thus the length of the detention cannot specifically be appealed.

### q23 · Right to an individualized detention decision.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 10 section 1 and section 2.

### q25 · Right to be free from torture and inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Instrument of Government (1974:152) chapter 2 sections 4 and 5 and in the Act (1994:1219) on the European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms, convention article 3. Rights awarded in The Instrument of Government (1974:152) and the Act (1994:1219) on the European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms are general and applies to aliens as well as nationals. Article 3 of the convention states that no one shall be subjected to torture or to inhuman or degrading treatment.


## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. There is no legal provision in Sweden that specifically regulates use of force in relation to immigrants entering the state's territory. The Police Act (1984:387) section 8 states that a police officer carrying out service duties must observe what is prescribed by law to act in a way that is justified considering the purpose of the action and the circumstances at hand; section 10 gives police the right to use force only when other means for carrying out a duty is inadequate and the use of force is justified. If the border police was acting to prevent aliens from crossing the border in an irregular way or to uphold the public order at a border, the legal provision would likely warrant the use of force.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Police Act (1984:387) section 8 and 10. The Police Act (1984:387) section 8 states that a police officer carrying out service duties must observe what is prescribed by law to act in a way that is justified considering the purpose of the action and the circumstances at hand, section 10 gives the police the right to use force only when other means for carrying out a duty is inadequate and the use of force is justified. The use of lethal force for the purpose of preventing migrants from entering the territory of Sweden would certainly not be considered justified.

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: Yes, the national law provides exemption.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in the Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 15 which provides an opportunity for authorities to apply for a temporary residence permit for an alien that has been the victim of a crime in order for them to participate in a criminal investigation and court hearing. This could be done regarding victims of human trafficking or smuggling. Such an application would legalize their stay in Sweden and likely exempt them of any liability for violating the Aliens Act (2005:716).

- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Act (2001:82) on Swedish citizenship, section 14.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden provides pathway to citizenship for economic migrants via obtaining a permanent residence permit after meeting relevant requirements and then applying for citizenship after meeting further relevant requirements, in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 5 and The Act (2001:82) on Swedish citizenship section 11.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden provides pathway to citizenship for humanitarian migrants via obtaining a permanent residence permit after meeting relevant requirements and then applying for citizenship after meeting further relevant requirements, in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 sections 1a and 6 and The Act (2001:82) on Swedish citizenship section 11.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden provides pathway to citizenship for family migrants via obtaining a permanent residence permit after meeting relevant requirements and then applying for citizenship after meeting further relevant requirements, in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 3g and The Act (2001:82) on Swedish citizenship section 11.

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: Yes, all migrants can obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden allows migrants to obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin in The Act (2001:82) on Swedish citizenship.

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.

## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 1 section 10 and chapter 5 section 6 and through the Act (2018:1197) on The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child UN Convention on the Rights of the Child that implements the convention as law in Sweden.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Sweden protects this right in The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 16 and in Chapter 4, section 1.

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No universal protection of this right. The Aliens Act (2005:716) chapter 5 section 6 states that if a residence permit cannot be granted on other grounds, a permit may be granted to a migrant if, in an overall assessment of the migrant’s situation, there are such particularly distressing circumstances that he or she should be allowed to stay in Sweden. During the assessment, the migrants state of health must be taken into account. This usually applies only regarding life threatening conditions. There is no other legal provision that provides special protection for migrants with disabilities.
