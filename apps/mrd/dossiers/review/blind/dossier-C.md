# Dossier C — Spain


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. However, this right is protected for some groups of migrants, in particular circumstances. For example, LO 4/2000 Immigration Act, Article 6.1. Article 6.1, Immigration Act 4/2000 states that migrants residing in Spain may hold the right to vote in municipal elections in the terms established in the Constitution, in international treaties where appropriate, and in other provisions in domestic law. LO 5/1985 on the General Electoral System, Article 176. Migrants can only vote in municipal elections (not regional or national elections) as established by LO 5/1985 on the General Electoral System, Article 176. In general, this right is available to European Union citizens residing in Spain, and migrants who are citizens of countries with reciprocity agreements (Norway, Ecuador, New Zealand, Colombia, Chile, Peru, Paraguay, Iceland, Bolivia, Cape Verde, Republic of Korea and Trinidad and Tobago) after the relevant residence requirements are met.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. However, this right is protected for some groups of migrants, in particular circumstances. Act 4/2000, Article 6.2. stipulates that citizens of the European Union and those migrants' from countries with bilateral treaties with Spain can run for public office in Spain. Most of the bilateral agreements between Spain and third countries (Norway, Ecuador, New Zealand, Colombia, Chile, Peru, Paraguay, Iceland, Bolivia, Cape Verde, Republic of Korea and Trinidad and Tobago) do not recognize the right to passive suffrage, only an active right after the relevant residence requirements are met, with the exception of Norway. Notwithstanding, EU citizens enjoy both passive and active rights as a result of them directly benefitting from the provisions of European Union Treaties on this topic.


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Spanish Constitution, Articles 13.1,, 24. Article 24 of the Spanish Constitution, in concordance to Article 13.1, extends to foreigners equal protection and remedies by judges and courts in the exercise of their legitimate rights and interests. 2) The LO 4/2000 Immigration Act, Articles 20, 21. Articles 20 and 21 of the 4/2000 Immigration Act recognizes that migrants have the right to a fair trial and to lodge an appeal against administrative decisions.

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Articles 22, 62 bis 1 h), 63.3. Article 22 of the Immigration Act 4/2000 grants migrants in Spain the right to free legal counsel in the administrative and judicial procedures related to their legal status as long as said proceedings are related to expulsion, return, entry refusal, asylum, or summary removal proceedings. 2) The Free Legal Aid Act 1996. The Free Legal Aid Act 1996 establishes the procedure to apply for free legal aid in such cases including the appointment of an attorney by the bar. It is also important to point out that the LO 1/1996, de 10 de enero, de Asistencia Jurídica Gratuita, states that the right to counsel at the government's expense is available to all foreigners who are in Spain, regardless of their immigration status, when they prove insufficient resources to litigate.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Code, Article 123. Article 123 states that migrants have the right to benefit from translation and interpretation services if the person does not understand or is not fluent in the Spanish language. This includes: a) the right to be assisted by an interpreter who uses a language that they understand during all the proceedings; b) the right to be assisted by an interpreter during conversations outside of the criminal proceedings with their own lawyer if they are directly related to the criminal proceedings. Translation and interpretation expenses will be borne by the Administration.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in the LO 4/200 Migration Act, Articles 55, 57. Unauthorized entry can result in administrative sanctions, but is not punishable as a criminal offense.


## Education

### q127 · Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants).
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Articles 9.1 and 9.2. Article 9.2 of the Immigration Act 4/2000 states that resident foreign nationals shall have the right to education of a non-compulsory nature in the same conditions as Spanish citizens. Specifically, they "shall have the right of access to levels of education and training...to the obtainment of the corresponding qualifications in each case, as well as access to the public system of scholarships and grants." However, equality in access to education services only applies to minor migrant children according to the Immigration Act 4/2000 (amended in 2009); below 18 years of age, they have a right to access to the education system, including basic, free and compulsory education as well as post-compulsory education. Adult migrants only enjoy equal access to education services as long as they are lawful residents in Spain.


## Equal protection

### q131 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 15/2022, Article 2. 2) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 23.2. The Immigration Act makes a distinction between legal and illegal migrants as it declares, as a principle, “the recognition of equal rights and obligations for all those who live or work legally in Spain." The concept of "migrant" must be understood as subsumed in that of "foreigner" for Spanish legislation. Spanish legislation uses the words "immigrant" and "foreigner" in its wording. It is worth highlighting the fact that LO 15/2022 does not use the words “immigrant” nor “foreigner” but that of “people.” However, according to Constitutional Court Judgement numbers 107/1984 and 115/1987, immigrants can not be discriminated against in certain fundamental rights (life, protection against torture or ill-treatment, freedom of thought and religion, freedom from arrest, etc), but the Constitution (Article 23, regarding the right to suffrage and the right to work as a civil servant) can introduce a reasonable and proportionate differential treatment on grounds of migrant status for certain rights, like the right to enter, leave or abide in the country or the right to work.


## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: any conviction (even minor administrative offenses) can lead to loss of residence status
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Expulsion from Spanish territory is an administrative sanction according to LO 4/2000 the Immigration Law, Article 57. Administrative sanctions can be applied in cases of administrative offenses (LO 4/2000 the Immigration Law, Article 57.1). Furthermore, the penalty of a prison sentence for criminal offenses may be replaced by expulsion in some specific cases (according to LO 10/1995 of 23 November, of the Penal Code, Article 89.1). Expulsion entails, in any case, the loss of any authorization to remain in Spain, according to LO 4/2000 the Immigration Law Article 57.4.

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 12/2009, Reguladora del Derecho de Asilo y de la Protección Subsidiaria, Articles 20.1 c), 20.1 d) and 44.4. 2) LO 4/2000, Additional Provision 10. Despite of these provisions, there are two land borders in Spain (Ceuta and Melilla) where this issue is very controversial. The Spanish Government considers that refoulement on the basis of risk of return to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment is covered by law because of the special regime of Ceuta and Melilla, according to LO 4/2000, Additional Provision 10. In 2020 and 2021, the Constitutional Court handed down two sentences that declared Additional Provision 10 of the LO 4/2000 in accordance with the Spanish Constitution.

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the 12/2009 Asylum Act, Aticles 5, 20.1 d), 36.1 a. 2) LO 4/2000 Additional Provision 10. Despite these provisions, there are two land borders in Spain (Ceuta and Melilla) where this issue is very controversial. The Spanish Government considers that refoulement on the basis of risk of return to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment is covered by law because of the special regime of Ceuta and Melilla, according to LO 4/2000, Additional Provision 10. In 2020 and 2021, the Constitutional Court handed down two sentences that declared Additional Provision 10 of the LO 4/2000 in accordance with the Spanish Constitution.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the 12/2009 Asylum Act Article 5 and 6. Article 6 explicitly refers to serious violations of fundamental rights as ground to refugee protection and Article 5 provides refugees with protection against refoulement.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: No.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Article 64.5 of the LO 4/2000 Immigration Act and Articles 23.6 and 246.7 of the 557/2011 Royal Decree recognize the right to apply for asylum but do not introduce an obligation to public authorities to inform asylim-seekers about the possible availability of asylum before being expelled or prevented entry.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. There is no explicit ban on chain-refoulement, and this is decided on a case-by-case basis. Article 36.1 of the Asylum Act which states that non-refoulment protections will apply in accordance with international treaties. European Court of Human Rights case-law has clearly declared that expulsion to a country that does not respect the non-refoulement prohibition is not lawful. In addtion, several articles in the LO 4/2000 the Immigration Law (36, 54.2 c), 57.6, 66.3 d)) could be read to imply a ban on the expulsion to any country where a migrant is likely to be subjected to refoulement through a subsequent expulsion, as both articles state that a transport company returning a migrant has the obligation to bring the migrant “…to any other State where their admission is guaranteed.” Despite these provisions, though, this matter has not been settled by court interpretation.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the 4/2000 Migration Act, Article 57. A migrant can only be expelled when justified by facts relevant to the individual concerned due to Protocol No. 4 to the European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms. The Convention is binding upon Member States of the Council of Europe and it expressly prohibits collective expulsion of aliens. However, in the European Court of Human Rights judgement, ND and NT v. Spain, the Grand Chamber agreed that such push-backs at the border do not need to be decided after any administrative procedure, and no guarantees are needed. Therefore, immediate removals at the borders will not be considered under the prohibition of collective expulsions.

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Royal Degree 557/2011, Article 23.4. 2) The LO 4/2000 Migration Act, Articles 21, 22.3, 57.9, 65.1. During expulsion proceedings, migrants have the right to be heard and submit written comments to the administrative authority before the adjudication of the case. After the administrative decision, there is a right to an administrative appeal before going to the courts to seek judicial review.


## Family

### q73 · Right of settled migrants to family reunification.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Articles 16.2, 17, 18.1. According to Article 18, a migrant can reunite their families under the following conditions: 1) one year of prior lawful residence in Spain and authorization for another year at least; 2) showing enough income to cover the living expenses of each family member; 3) showing evidence of housing conditions to accommodate all reunited family members; 4) health insurance for each family member. Reuniting ascendants older than 65-years old is subject to additional requirements, but is still possible. 2) The Royal Decree 557/2011, Articles 17-18, 52-61.

### q75 · Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Administrative Judicial Procedures Act of 1998. 2) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 21.1. All types of administrative resolutions concerning migrants' rights are subject to appeal before the judicial authority

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Spanish Civil Code, Article 44. 2) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 17.1. Article 44 of the Spanish Civil Code makes same-sex marriage legal in Spain, and Article 17.1 of the Immigration Act 4/2000 extends reunification to same sex spouses, since it accepts civil partnerships for family reunification.

### q79 · Right to family reunification without resource conditions
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. According to Article 18.2 of LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, the sponsor must prove that he/she has adequate housing, insurance coverage, and sufficient financial means to cover his/her needs and those of their family before visas for family reunification are granted.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Civil Code, Articles 17.1, 22.1. Article 17.1 states that migrants in Spain are guaranteed citizenship if they would otherwise be stateless. 2) Royal Decree 865/2001. 3) LO 12/2009.


## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants).
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 12. 2) National Health System Quality and Cohesion Law, Articles 3, 3bis, 3ter. Article 3.ter establishes that “Foreigners not registered or authorized as residents in Spain have the right to health protection and healthcare under the same conditions as people with Spanish nationality, as established in article 3.1." 3) Royal Decree-Law 7/2018. Article 3.1 Royal Decree 7/2018 establishes that “All persons with Spanish nationality and foreign persons who have established their residence in Spanish territory are holders of the right to health protection and health care." In addition, irregular migrants who have not registered with their local councils, but who are pregnant or suffer an accident and require emergency care are guaranteed access.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 13. Article declares that only those migrants with long-term resident status are entitled to access to public housing programs on an equal basis with citizens. The possibility of access is only for migrants who are legal residents, but this right is only enforceable in exactly the same conditions as citizens once foreigners obtain a long-term resident permit.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in LO 2000 the Immigration Act, Article 9. Equality in access to education services only applies to minor migrant children according to the Immigration Act 4/2000 (amended in 2009); below 18 years of age, they have a right to access to the education system, including basic, free and compulsory education as well as post-compulsory education.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. However, national law in Spain protects this right for those legally resident in Spain in LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 2.bis, (which states that the law "[recognizes]... equal rights and obligations for all those who live or work legally in Spain"), 2e), 10, 23.2, 36. Legal provisions mostly provide for equal treatment in terms of labour wages for regular migrant workers with a valid work permit, e.g., Article 10 of the Immigration Act taken together with Article 23.2 (prohibition of discrimination by employers) and Article 36 with regards to access to work and work authorization. Such provisions lay down a regime that implies equal pay and equal work conditions for all legal resident workers, without regard to their nationality.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Migrant workers have completely free choice of employment.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Articles 38.5, 38.7. 2) Royal Decree of the Migration Act 557/2011, Articles 63.1 and 63.5. There are two restrictions on the free choice of employment (geographic scope and occupation) ONLY within the first one-year duration of the work permit. Additionally, a recent modification of the regulation from the Royal Decree 629/2022, has made it possible to allow the migrant to freely choose employment or self-employment after one year from granting the first work permit.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: Yes, unrestricted right to work.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Royal Decree 55/2011, Article 58.4. 2) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 19.1. The Royal Decree 55/20011 states that the residence authorization for family reunification of the spouse, the civil partner or the reunified child will enable the holder to work so that no working authorization is needed. This authorization enables them to work for others or for their own account in any part of Spanish territory, occupation or activity sector.

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment implies loss of immigration status unless a new job is found within a specified time period.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain conditionally protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Articles 31.7, 38.6. 2) Royal Decree 557/2011, Articles 71.2, 71.3. There are three general scenarios that require clarification on this issue: first, immediately after arrival, if the initial visa for residence and work permit can not be transformed into a work and residence permit because the employer is not able to hire the foreign worker, there is a special speedy procedure available to the migrant to ask for administrative permission to find a similar job in a very short period of time. Second, residence and working permits requires having worked a minimum amount of time during the previous authorization for them to be renewed. Third, long-term residents do not require a work authorization so their work could be discontinuous and this does not affect their status.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO Act 4/2000 the Immigration, Articles 2bis, 2 e), 10, 14, 23. In principle, Articles 2bis and 2e) implicitly state “the recognition of equal rights and obligations for all those who live or work legally in Spain." This means migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections. Three other provisions of the LO 4/2000 Migration Act, on Articles 10.1, 14 and 23 taken together recognize legally resident foreigners, who meet the requirements accorded by law, the right to exercise a paid activity on their own or another's account, as well as to access the Social Security system. They are entitled to services and social benefits, both general and basic as well as specific ones, under the same conditions as Spanish Citizens and any discrimination against them under this provision is prohibited and sanctioned by the law. 2) Royal Decree 1620/2011. 3) Royal Decree 16/2022.

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Yes, immediate equal access to all types of public retirement pension schemes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the Immigration Act 4/2000, Articles 2.bis, 2e), 10, 14, 36.5. Article 14 states states that resident foreigners have the right to access social security benefits and services under the same conditions as Spanish citizens, both general and basic as well as specific ones, under the same conditions as Spanish citizens.

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: Yes, immediate equal access to all types of unemployment benefits.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in the Immigration Act 4/2000, Articles 2.bis, 2e), 10, 14, 36.5.

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are met.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 32. 2) Royal Decree 557/2011. As a general rule, those who have had a temporary residence permit in Spain for five years continuously and who meet the conditions established by law will have the right to switch to a long-term residence permit. Additionally, Article 148.3, Royal Decree 557/2011, also provides for several cases where long-term residence authorization can also granted: "...c) Residents who were born in Spain and, upon reaching the age of majority, have resided in Spain legally and continuously for at least the three consecutive years immediately prior to the application; d) Foreigners who have been Spanish of origin and have lost their Spanish nationality; e) Residents who, upon reaching the age of majority, have been under the guardianship of a Spanish public entity during the immediately preceding five consecutive years; f) Stateless persons, refugees or beneficiaries of subsidiary protection who are in Spanish territory and whose respective status in Spain has been recognized."

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 32. There are only a few exceptions to this rule. First, student visas or students permits are not residence permits, therefore the time spent in Spain does not contribute to a long-term residence permit. Additionally, seasonal workers are subject to a separate regime, and cannot tranisition to permanent residency in the same way as other temporary migrants.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, same as citizens.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Spanish Constitution, Article 24. According to Article 24 of the Spanish Constitution, all persons have the right to obtain effective protection from the judges and the courts in the exercise of their rights and legitimate interests, and in no case may there be a lack of defense. 2) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Articles 20, 22, 36. Articles 20 and 22 Immigration Act 2000 guarantees the right to effective legal protection, and the right to free legal assistance, respectively. Finally, Article 36 provides for the authorization for the exercise of lucrative activities, which states that the lack of residence and work authorization, without prejudice to the responsibilities of the employer to which it gives rise, including those of Social Security, will not invalidate the employment contract with respect to the rights of the foreign worker, nor will it be an obstacle to obtaining the benefits derived from assumptions contemplated by international agreements for the protection of workers or others that may apply,

## Legal personhood

### q27 · Right not to have identity documents confiscated.
- Law: Yes, right for migrants explicitly specified.
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the LO 4/2000 Immigration Act, Article 4. 2) The LO 4/2015 on Protection of Public Security, Article 13.2. Article 13.2 of the Organic Law 4/2015 states that the migrants have the right to not be deprived of their identity documents except by judicial decision in criminal proceedings.

### q29 · Right to birth registration.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in the Law of Civil Registry 20/2011, Articles 44, 45, 46, 47, 48. Registration of the birth of a child is mandatory in Spain for every child, national or foreigner, regardless of status.

### q31 · Right to government document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the LO 4/2000 Migration Act, Articles 4, 4.2, 27.2b), 29.2, 34.2. In particular, Article 4 states that foreigners in Spanish territory have the right and the duty to keep documentation proving their identity issued by the competent authorities of the country of origin, as well as one proving their status in Spain. Moreover, all foreigners who have been issued a visa or a permit to remain in Spain for a period exceeding six months will obtain a special foreigner's identity card, which they must personally request within one month of their entry into Spain or since the authorization is granted, respectively. Holders of a residence and seasonal work visa will be exempted from said obligation. 2) LO 4/2015 on Protection of Public Security, Article 13.

### q33 · Right against destruction of document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Rarely

National law in Spain protects this right in the Immigration Act 4/2000, Articles 4.1, 4.3. Article 4.3 of the Immigration Act 4/2000 prohibits the taking of a migrants' documents and implicitly destroy them, but it does not include any penalty. Instead, the law provides the right to keep and preserve these documents in Article 4.1, so anyone that violates this right is acting illegally.


## Liberty

### q15 · Right to communicate with consular officials.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in LO 4/2000 Immigration Act, Articles 22.3, 60.4, 62.5, 62 bis e), 64.3, which establishes that expulsion procedures that include administrative detention will be communicated to the Consular authorities. 2) The Code of Criminal Procedure, Article 520.2, which states that notice will be sent by Spanish authorities to the Consulate in the event of criminal arrest of a migrant/foreigner.

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Detention of children on migration grounds is not formally prohibited in Spain. Migrant children in family units which are subjected to deportation proceedings may be sent to a detention center and kept together. In such cases, the center must provide specific facilities to accommodate the families in separate modules from other migrant detainees according to Article 7 of the Royal Decree 162/2014 of Aliens Detention centers. Also, the Supreme Court judgment of 10th February 2015 recognized the right to protect family life in migrant detention centers in application of the EU Return Directive 2008. Children falling under the category of unaccompanied foreign minors, however, are placed in foster care under the protection of the public authorities as established by the Organic Law on the Legal Protection of Minors with the regulations set forth in article 35 of the 4/2000 Immigration Act. This can be seen as an implicit prohibition of the detention of unaccompanied migrant children.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Being in Spain without a valid residence authorization is considered a serious infraction according to Article 53.1a of the LO 4/2000 Immigration Act. This may be grounds for detention and internment in a Detention Centre for Foreigners (CIE) according to Article 62 of the LO 4/2000 Immigration Act. CIEs are public establishments where the migrants may be held in detention for a maximum of 60 days.

### q21 · Right to appeal legality, conditions, and length of detention
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the LO 4/2000 Immigration Act, Articles 21, 23, 62.6, 65. In particular, Article 23 states that any breach of the legal grounds and conditions for detention and interment are subject to judicial control, and in extreme cases to Habeas Corpus proceedings.

### q23 · Right to an individualized detention decision.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in Athe Immigration Act 4/2000, Articles 53, 57, which require individualized determination of administrative detention based on grounds relating to immigration status.

### q25 · Right to be free from torture and inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Law, Articles 3 and 62bis b). Article 3 of the LO 4/2000 states "the norms related to the fundamental rights of foreigners will be interpreted in accordance with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights." This includes the right to be free from torture and cruel, inhumane, and degrading treatment. 2) The Spanish Constitution, Article 15. In order to provide protections of this right, Articles 3 and 62 bis b) of the Immigration Act 4/2000 must be read together with Articles 15 of the Spanish Constitution and with Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights, norms which clearly prohibit any torture, cruel, inhumane and degrading treatment of all human beings. Incorporation of EU law and international law is established by the Spanish Constitution in Articles 10.2 and 96 of the Constitution.


## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. In general, there is no express provision on the use of force. Instead, there are only general provisions and internal protocols on the proportionate use of force by police officers. In the case of Ceuta and Melilla (which are overseas territories located in the north of Morocco), the First Additional Clause of the Organic Law 4/2015 states that aliens trying to illegally enter Spanish territory can be detained at the border and immediately removed.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The Criminal Code provides general provisions on crimes like homicide, and explains the penalties for officials and citizens if the use of lethal force is found to be unnecessary or unjustified. However, neither the law nor the regulation cover the use of lethal force as a means of preventing entry into the territory. See: LO 4/2000 (articles 26 and 60); Royal Decree 557/2011, articles 15 and 23. There has been at least one criminal case against police officers (Tarajal case). In a recent case, he Melilla's case (ocurred on 24h June 2022 ) instead of providing aid to several migrants when they tried to enter Spanish territory by swimming from Morocco, instead tried to prevent the migrants from entering Spanish territory by using the threat of firing their weapons. However, despite some of the migrants drowning, the Courts have not convicted any member of the police for such actions.It is an open case.

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: Yes, the national law provides exemption
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) Ley Orgánica 10/1995, de 23 de noviembre, del Código Penal, article 177bis 11). 2) Article 59 bis of the 4/2000 Immigration Act. 3) Chapter IV of the 557/2011 Royal Decree. Illegal entry in Spain is considered a breach of administrative law, not criminal law. However, criminal law also exempts trafficked/smuggled migrants from criminal prosecution according to Article 59 bis of the 4/2000 Immigration Act. Provided said migrants are considered victims of human trafficking in accordance with Article 10 of the Council of Europe Convention on the Fight Against Trafficking in Human Beings, immigration officials are obliged to inform the potential victim of their rights and at the same time grant them a residence authorization for a short period of time in order for that person to recover and temporally sever their ties with traffickers. Additionally, Chapter IV of the 557/2011 Royal Decree regulates the temporary residence and work permits of victims of human trafficking (Articles 140 - 146). The chapter deals with many topics such as identification of potential non-European Union victims of trafficking, the procedures to obtain the above mentioned residence and work permits, the assisted return to the country of origin if so desired by the victim, and the protection of minors who are victims of human trafficking.


## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Convictions do not affect citizenship status.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the Civil Code Article 25.2. Article 25.2 of the Civil Code states that naturalization obtained using false statements, concealing information, or directly by fraud, can be nullified. Nullification proceedings can only be exercised during the next fifteen years from the date a migrant is naturalized by the Public Prosecutor’s Office before the civil court. Beyond this, no crime allows authorities to stripe any Spaniard of their nationality, be it nationality by birth or through naturalization.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the Civil Code, Article 22.1. The general rule to acquire Spanish citizenship is through ten years lawful and continued residence, irrespective of whether the migrant is on an economic, humanitarian, or family visa.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the Civil Code, Article 22.1. The general rule to acquire Spanish citizenship is through ten years lawful and continued residence, irrespective of whether the migrant is on an economic, humanitarian, or family visa.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in the Civil Code, Articles 22.1, 22.2.d. The general rule to acquire Spanish citizenship is through ten years lawful and continued residence, irrespective of whether the migrant is on an economic, humanitarian, or family visa. Additionally, acquisition of nationality through marriage to a Spaniard only requires one year residence after marriage (Article 22.2.d Spanish Civil Code). The situation is slightly different for children born in Spain from foreign parents; after one year since birth an application for Spanish citizenship can be submitted in this case. Similarly they could submit such application when they reach their majority.

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: Conditional, depending on the country of origin.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Spanish Constitution, Article 11.3. 2) The Civil Code, Article 24.1. In accordance with Article 24.1, those with citizenship of Latin American countries, Andorra, Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal, may obtain dual nationality. France has been added to this list by agreement between the Kingdom of Spain and the French Republic, done in Montauban, on 15 March, 2021 at the 26th Franco-Spanish Summit. Sephardic Jews may also obtain dual nationality according to LO 12/2015.

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to regularize after certain conditions are met.
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 the Immigration Act, Article 31.3. 2) Royal Decree 557/2011 Articles 123, 124. Irregular migrants can regularize in exceptional circumstances of special ties with the labour market or with family members regularly staying in Spain. This legal principal is called “arraigo social” (deep rooting), meaning that if a person without a legal permit residing in the country meets certain requirements by law, they can apply for a one year temporary residence authorization due to exceptional circumstances (set down in Article 123 of the Royal Decree 557/2011). Additionally, Article 124 of Royal Decree 557/2011 identifies three different types of “arraigo” related to work, social roots and family roots. The main conditions are that migrants have to prove a continued stay in Spain for a minimum period of two years, provide a lack of a criminal record in Spain and in their country of origin or in the country or countries in which they resided during the last five years, and demonstrate the existence of labor relations whose duration is not less than six months.

## Thought, conscience, and religion or belief

### q81 · Right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Spanish Constitution, Article 16.1. ## Victims of crime

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Spanish Constitution Article 24. Article 24 of the Spanish Constitution reads as follows, 'All persons have the right: 1) to obtain effective protection from the judges and the courts in the exercise of their rights and legitimate interests, and in no case may there be a lack of defense. 2) Likewise, all have the right to the ordinary judge predetermined by law; to defense and assistance by a lawyer; to be informed of the charges brought against them; to a public trial without undue delays and with full guarantees; to the use of evidence appropriate to their defense; not to make self-incriminating statements; not to plead themselves guilty; and to be presumed innocent. The law shall specify the cases in which, for reasons of family relationship or professional secrecy, it shall not be compulsory to make statements regarding allegedly criminal offences." 2) LO 4/2000, Article 20.1. which recognizes that migrants have the right to a fair trial and to lodge an appeal against administrative decisions.

### q45 · Right of victims of crime to immigration status or relief from expulsion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) LO 4/2000 Immigration Act, Articles 31bis, 59, 59bis. Article 59, among other provisions, states that "1. The foreigner who is irregularly in Spain and is a victim, injured or witness of an act of illicit trafficking of human beings, illegal migration, labor exploitation or illicit traffic of labor or exploitation in prostitution abusing their situation if necessary, it will be exempt from administrative responsibility and will not be expelled if it denounces the authors or cooperators of said traffic, or cooperates and collaborates with the competent authorities, essential data or testifying, where appropriate, in the corresponding process." 2) The Royal Decree 557/2011 (Reglamento de Extranjería), Article 126.1. However, exceptional residence permits for humanitarian reasons are only granted to victims of certain crimes like trafficking of human beings, racism, discrimination, xenophobia, etc., if victims collaborate with public authorities, police or the judiciary in their investigations against criminal networks.


## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

The suggested citation would be: National law in Spain protects this right in 1) Immigration Act 4/2000, (in spanish Ley de extranjería) in Articles 35, 59.5, 59 bis.5, 62.4. This act introduced a specific regime for unaccompanied migrant minors that, among other provisions, includes the duty to immediately protect the child following the "best interests of the child" principle. 2) Royal Decree 557/2011 (in spanish Reglamento de extranjería), Article 123. 3) Organic Act on Legal Minors' Protection Act 1/1996. 4) Royal Decree 629/2022 introduced modifications to reduce the financial means required for family reunification when the family member to be reunited is a child.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) Article 31 bis of the Immigration Act 4/2000. The Immigration Act 4/2000 allows for the temporary residence and work for foreign women victims of gender violence. It states that, regardless their administrative situation, women in such situations are guaranteed the rights recognized in Organic Law 1/2004 on the Integral Protection Measures against Gender Violence, as well as protection and safety measures established in current legislation. However, in order to access temporary protection status it is not enough to simply make an application to the authorities, there has to be a decision by the Prosecutor or the Judge acknowledging that harassment and/or violence or intimidation existed. This special regime included the authorization to legally and temporary stay in Spain. Women staying irregularly in Spain are protected from deportation procedures after reporting a situation of gender based violence. In such cases, deportation procedures will not be initiated, or if already initiated, will be halted.

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: Yes.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Spain protects this right in 1) the Royal Decree 557/2011, Article 41.2. 2) The Immigration Act 4/2000 Article 14.2, Article 17. Article 14.2 ensures a migrant's right to access and benefits from social services to all foreigners with disabilities (as well as minors) regardless of their status, including special care and medical treatment as required by their physical or psycological situation if they live in Spain (“domicilio habitual”). Additionally, Article 17 on Family Reunification, includes people with disabilities as beneficiaries of family reunification procedures as long as they are not able to provide for their own needs due to their health conditions; they shall also benefit from the same type of authorization as family member who are reuniting with them, as established in Article 41.2 of the Royal Decree 557/2011.
