# Dossier L — China


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Article 34 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018) gives the vote only to nationals: "All citizens of the People's Republic of China who have reached the age of 18, regardless of ethnicity, race, gender, occupation, family background, religious belief, level of education, property status or length of residence, shall have the right to vote and stand for election." Article 33 defines citizens as "all persons holding the nationality of the People's Republic of China". The Electoral Law of the National People's Congress and Local People's Congresses (中华人民共和国全国人民代表大会和地方各级人民代表大会选举法, 1979, seventh amendment 2020) repeats this rule in Article 4: "citizens of the People's Republic of China who have reached the age of 18 … all have the right to vote and to be elected." That law covers every level of people's congress. Deputies to township, town, county and district congresses are elected directly by voters, and higher congresses up to the National People's Congress are elected indirectly by the congress one level below (Article 3). No other route to the vote exists for foreigners. Article 7 extends voting only to Chinese citizens living abroad. Article 32 of the Constitution protects foreigners' "lawful rights and interests" but grants no political rights. The Exit and Entry Administration Law 2012 gives permanent residence holders (arts. 47-49) no electoral rights. A lawfully present migrant therefore has no right to vote in local, regional or national elections, whatever their permit or length of residence, including permanent residents.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Article 34 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018) gives the right "to vote and stand for election" only to "citizens of the People's Republic of China who have reached the age of 18". Article 33 defines citizens as "all persons holding the nationality of the People's Republic of China". Article 4 of the Electoral Law of the National People's Congress and Local People's Congresses (中华人民共和国全国人民代表大会和地方各级人民代表大会选举法, 1979, seventh amendment 2020) also limits the right "to be elected" (被选举权) to Chinese citizens aged 18 or over. This covers deputies to people's congresses at every level, from townships to the National People's Congress (Article 3). Higher state offices are also reserved for citizens. For example, Article 79 of the Constitution makes only "citizens of the People's Republic of China who have the right to vote and stand for election and who have reached the age of 45" eligible for president or vice president. Neither the Exit and Entry Administration Law 2012 nor the permanent residence rules give foreigners any eligibility for elected office. Article 32 of the Constitution protects foreigners' "lawful rights and interests" without conferring political rights. A lawfully present migrant, including a permanent resident, therefore has no right to stand for local, regional or national office.


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Law (中华人民共和国刑事诉讼法, as amended 2018), arts. 6 and 17; the Civil Procedure Law (中华人民共和国民事诉讼法, as amended 2023), art. 5; and the Administrative Litigation Law (中华人民共和国行政诉讼法), arts. 98-99. Article 17 of the Criminal Procedure Law provides that where a foreigner who commits a crime is to be held criminally responsible, "the provisions of this Law apply". Foreign defendants therefore receive the same procedural guarantees as citizens. Article 5 of the Civil Procedure Law gives foreigners and stateless persons who sue or are sued in the people's courts "the same litigation rights and obligations as citizens". Article 99 of the Administrative Litigation Law gives them "the same litigation rights and obligations as citizens" in administrative litigation. The protection has limits. Both procedural codes allow China to apply reciprocity to nationals of states that restrict the rights of Chinese litigants. Foreign parties may only instruct Chinese lawyers (Civil Procedure Law art. 274; Administrative Litigation Law art. 100). In civil cases, foreign parties pay for translation themselves (Civil Procedure Law art. 273). Most importantly, immigration enforcement is kept away from the courts. Under art. 64 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012), a foreigner may challenge continued questioning, detention for investigation, restriction of movement or repatriation only by administrative reconsideration, and "the reconsideration decision is final". Expulsion decisions of the Ministry of Public Security are also final (art. 81). Administrative Litigation Law art. 13(4) bars court review of acts that a statute makes finally decided by an administrative body. These exclusions concern measures that apply only to foreigners. In general civil, criminal and administrative proceedings the statutes give migrants equal standing, regardless of immigration status.

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Proceedings on a migrant's legal status are administrative. They include refusal of visas or residence permits, continued questioning, detention for investigation, restriction of movement, repatriation and expulsion, all governed by the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012). Under art. 64 they can be challenged only by administrative reconsideration, which is final. No statute provides counsel at state expense in these proceedings. The Legal Aid Law (中华人民共和国法律援助法, 2021) defines legal aid as a system for "citizens in economic difficulty and other parties meeting statutory conditions" (art. 2). Its mandatory appointment of counsel applies only to criminal suspects and defendants in listed categories (arts. 24-25). Its list of civil and administrative matters open to legal aid (art. 31) covers state compensation, social insurance, maintenance, labour remuneration and personal injury, but not immigration matters. For non-citizens, art. 69 provides that legal aid applies where Chinese law so provides, and otherwise "may" be provided by reference to the Law under a treaty or on the basis of reciprocity. This is a discretionary and conditional opening, not a right. A foreigner facing detention for investigation (up to 30 days, extendable to 60; art. 60) or repatriation (art. 62) therefore has no right to a state-funded lawyer. Asylum seekers have no such right either, because China has no refugee determination procedure; art. 46 only provides a temporary identity document. A migrant prosecuted for illegal border crossing as a crime (Criminal Law art. 322) can obtain criminal legal aid like any defendant. That right attaches to the criminal charge, not to the status proceedings.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Law (中华人民共和国刑事诉讼法, as amended 2018), arts. 9, 14 and 17, read with the Supreme People's Court Interpretation on the Application of the Criminal Procedure Law (2021), art. 484. Article 9 provides that "the people's courts, people's procuratorates and public security organs shall provide translation for participants in the proceedings who do not understand the language commonly used in the locality". The duty therefore covers investigation, prosecution and trial, and through art. 17 it applies to foreign suspects and defendants. Article 14 requires these authorities to protect the litigation rights of suspects, defendants and other participants. Article 484 of the 2021 SPC Interpretation requires courts trying foreign-related criminal cases to provide translation for the foreign party. Litigation documents must be accompanied by a foreign-language translation where the foreign party does not understand Chinese, and the foreign party may waive translation only by written statement. The cost is not placed on the defendant. Criminal proceedings carry no litigation fees, and official government legal guidance states that courts provide translation free of charge to foreigners and stateless persons who do not understand Chinese. It adds that "expenses for a translator to appear in court shall not be borne by the defendant". The Public Security Organs' procedural rules impose the same duty during investigation. This contrasts with civil proceedings, where the Civil Procedure Law (art. 273) makes foreign parties pay for translation. The right covers all migrants, regular and irregular, and victims and witnesses as participants in the proceedings.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Unauthorised entry is punished both administratively and criminally. Article 71 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012) sets a fine of 1,000 to 5,000 yuan for anyone who enters or exits with forged or fraudulently obtained documents, evades border inspection, or "illegally exits or enters by other means". In serious cases it adds five to ten days' detention and a fine of up to 10,000 yuan. Repatriation and a one-to-five-year re-entry ban may follow (art. 62), and detention for investigation of up to 60 days (art. 60). Criminal liability arises under art. 322 of the Criminal Law (中华人民共和国刑法), which punishes illegal crossing of the national border where the circumstances are serious. The Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate Interpretation on border-management crimes (法释〔2012〕17号) defines "serious" in art. 5. It covers crossing three or more times, crossing in a group of three or more, drawing others into crossing, colluding with foreign organisations, and crossing again within one year of an administrative penalty for illegal crossing. Article 6 defines illegal crossing to include entering without documents or with forged or another person's documents. The joint Opinions of 2022 (SPC, SPP, Ministry of Public Security, National Immigration Administration) add further aggravating circumstances, such as damaging border fences. No provision exempts refugees, asylum seekers or trafficking victims. Article 46 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law only allows refugee applicants to stay with a temporary identity document during screening and contains no non-penalisation clause.


## Education

### q127 · Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Education Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国教育法, 1995, amended 2021) frames the right to education and equal opportunity for nationals: "Citizens of the People's Republic of China have the right and duty to receive education ... citizens ... enjoy equal educational opportunities according to law" (art. 9). The right to vocational training and continuing education in art. 41 belongs to "employees" in general, but public higher education and degree courses are reached through the national (gaokao) system for citizens. Foreigners enter a separate international-student track governed by the Measures for the Administration of Schools Recruiting and Training International Students (学校招收和培养国际学生管理办法, Order No. 42 of 2017). Under these Measures, higher education institutions set quotas and admit international students at their own discretion (arts. 10-12). Applicants' "financial guarantee" documents are examined (art. 12), and fees for international students follow separate "relevant State provisions" (art. 14). In practice these are higher, internationally priced tuition schedules rather than domestic rates. Students must hold an X1/X2 study visa and a study residence permit (arts. 35-36) and must buy insurance (art. 40). Access to public adult and higher education is therefore available to foreigners, but on a separate, discretionary and fee-differentiated basis that is not commensurate with citizens. No statute gives refugees, temporary workers or irregular migrants equal access to public degree-level or vocational training institutions. Permanent residents receive no express education entitlement for adults in the 2012 permanent-residence treatment measures, which cover only children's compulsory schooling.


## Equal protection

### q131 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. No national law prohibits discrimination on the ground of nationality or migrant status. The equality clause of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018), Article 33, provides that "all citizens of the People's Republic of China are equal before the law" and so does not reach foreigners. Article 4's ban on discrimination concerns China's own ethnic groups (民族). The employment non-discrimination clauses list closed or illustrative grounds that omit nationality. Article 12 of the Labour Law (中华人民共和国劳动法) provides that "workers shall not be discriminated against in employment because of ethnicity, race, sex or religious belief". Article 3 of the Employment Promotion Law (中华人民共和国就业促进法, 2007, amended 2015) repeats this with "ethnicity, race, sex, religious belief, etc.". Article 3 of the Trade Union Law (中华人民共和国工会法, 2021) lists ethnicity, race, sex, occupation, religious belief and education. Courts have not read nationality or migrant status into these lists. The legal framework instead treats foreigners differently by design: employment requires a separate permit and labour-market test, and residence-based entitlements are tied to status. China has no general anti-discrimination statute or equality body. Article 32's protection of foreigners' "lawful rights and interests" is a general principle, not a ban on status discrimination.


## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: any conviction (even minor administrative offenses) can lead to loss of residence status
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012) lets the authorities end a foreigner's stay for any breach of Chinese law, criminal or administrative. Under art. 81(1), a foreigner who "engages in activities inconsistent with the purpose of stay or residence, or otherwise violates Chinese laws or regulations and is unsuitable to continue to stay or reside in China" may be ordered to leave within a time limit. Under art. 81(2), the Ministry of Public Security may expel a foreigner whose violation of the Law is "serious but does not constitute a crime". That decision is final, and it brings a ten-year re-entry ban. Art. 62(1)(iv) allows repatriation of any foreigner who "violates this Law or other laws or administrative regulations". Repatriation carries a re-entry ban of one to five years, and challenges go only to administrative reconsideration, which is final (art. 64). Art. 31(4) bars the issue of a residence permit to anyone who "violates relevant Chinese laws or administrative regulations and is unsuitable to reside in China". Refusals to renew are final (art. 36). Permanent residents get no stronger protection. Under art. 49, the Ministry cancels permanent residence for anyone who has been expelled, and also for any "other circumstances unsuitable for permanent residence". No provision limits these powers to serious crimes, weighs length of residence or family ties, or protects long-term residents. Minor public-security offences such as drug use, illegal stay or illegal work can therefore end lawful residence.

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in China protects this right only for extradition, in the Extradition Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国引渡法, adopted 28 December 2000), art. 8(7). Art. 8 lists cases in which a foreign request for extradition "shall be refused". One of them is where "the person sought has been or may be subjected to torture or other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment in the requesting State". The ban is mandatory, and its wording follows the Convention against Torture, which China ratified in 1988. It protects any person whose extradition is sought, not only nationals of particular states. The protection does not extend to immigration removal. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012) lets the police repatriate foreigners who stay or work illegally, have been refused entry, or have breached other laws (art. 62). It names no country-of-return risk as a bar. Challenges go only to administrative reconsideration, which is final (art. 64). Art. 46 gives asylum seekers a temporary identity document during refugee status screening. It contains no express ban on return, and no screening procedure has been put in place. The Constitution (art. 32) allows asylum for political reasons but says nothing about torture risk. In law, then, the torture-based non-refoulement ban covers only formal extradition requests. Deportation, repatriation and border returns are left to discretion and to treaty obligations that courts do not apply directly.

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in China protects this right only for extradition, in the Extradition Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国引渡法, 2000), art. 8(3)-(4). Extradition "shall be refused" if it is requested for a political offence, or if China "has granted the person sought the right of asylum" (art. 8(3)). It must also be refused if "the person sought may be prosecuted or punished on account of his or her race, religion, nationality, sex, political opinion or personal status", or may be treated unfairly in judicial proceedings for those reasons (art. 8(4)). These grounds largely track the Refugee Convention list and add sex. They are mandatory. The Constitution (art. 32) allows asylum to foreigners who request it for political reasons. Outside extradition the law sets no persecution-based bar. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012) gives asylum seekers a temporary identity document while their refugee status is screened, and gives recognised refugees a refugee identity document (art. 46). It does not prohibit their return, and no status determination procedure has been adopted, so UNHCR decides claims. Repatriation under art. 62 lists no persecution-risk exception, and challenges end in final administrative reconsideration (art. 64). China acceded to the 1951 Convention and 1967 Protocol in 1982, but art. 33 has not been incorporated and courts do not apply it directly. The statutory ban is therefore real but narrow: it binds extradition decisions and leaves deportation and border returns unregulated.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The only statutory non-refoulement rules sit in art. 8 of the Extradition Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国引渡法, 2000). That article requires refusal of extradition on grounds of nationality, political offence or asylum, discriminatory prosecution (race, religion, nationality, sex, political opinion or status), and torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment (art. 8(7)). It also covers military offences, lapse of time or pardon, and judgments in absentia. It does not bar extradition where the person faces the death penalty or another serious threat to life, liberty or fundamental rights. Art. 9 adds only an optional ground: extradition "may be refused" where the person's "age, health or other reasons" make it inappropriate on humanitarian grounds. That ground is discretionary and is not tied to conditions in the receiving state. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012) allows repatriation (art. 62) and expulsion (art. 81) with no exception for risks to life or other fundamental rights in the country of return. Challenges go only to final administrative reconsideration (art. 64). Art. 46 gives asylum seekers temporary documents but no complementary or humanitarian protection status. The Constitution's art. 32 asylum clause covers political reasons only. Chinese law therefore has no ban on returning people to serious deprivations of fundamental rights such as the right to life, beyond the torture and persecution grounds limited to extradition.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Neither the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012) nor its implementing rules require officials to tell a foreigner that asylum may be available before refusing entry or removing them. The only mention of asylum seekers is art. 46: "Foreigners applying for refugee status may, during the screening period, stay in China with a temporary identity certificate issued by the public security organ." The provision assumes an application already exists. It creates no procedure, no duty to inform and no duty to refer. The removal provisions point the other way. Art. 25 lets border inspection refuse entry and says the authority "may give no reasons". Art. 26 orders people refused entry to return, by force if needed. Art. 62 allows repatriation (遣送出境) of anyone in an irregular situation. None of these mentions asylum, a protection claim or a referral to UNHCR. The Ministry of Public Security's Provisions on Procedures for Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order No. 149, 2018) give foreigners an interpreter (art. 241) and consular notification (art. 254) in their chapter on foreign-related cases. They also contain no duty to give asylum information. Constitution art. 32, which allows asylum for political reasons, is a discretionary state power, not an individual's right to information. China has no refugee status determination procedure, so UNHCR's Beijing office handles the few claims that reach it.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. National law contains no non-refoulement rule for expulsion. Without a direct ban, it also has no ban on removal to a third country that might pass the person on. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012) allows repatriation (art. 62) and deportation (art. 81). It states the grounds but puts no limit on where a person may be sent. The Ministry of Public Security's Provisions on Procedures for Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order No. 149, 2018) say where a repatriated foreigner may be sent, in art. 247: the country of nationality, the country of residence before entry, the country of birth, the country of the port of departure before entry, or "other countries or regions that allow the repatriated foreigner to enter". The only test is whether the destination will admit the person. Nothing asks whether that state is safe or would send the person on to persecution or torture. Art. 46 of the Exit-Entry Law, which mentions refugee status, attaches no protection against removal to that status. China has no refugee law that would supply a safe-third-country or onward-refoulement rule.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012), arts. 58, 62 and 81. The Ministry of Public Security's Provisions on Procedures for Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order No. 149, 2018), arts. 246, 250 and 251, put these into practice. Under art. 58 the law, not officials' discretion, assigns removal: only county-level or higher public security organs or border inspection authorities may order repatriation. Art. 62 lists the individual grounds for repatriation (遣送出境): failing to leave by an order's deadline, being inadmissible, illegal stay or illegal employment, or other breaches of law that "require repatriation". Art. 81 makes an order to leave within a time limit (限期出境) and deportation (驱逐出境) administrative penalties tied to the person's own conduct. Deportation requires a "serious" breach and a decision of the Ministry of Public Security, which "is final". Art. 246 of the Provisions requires approval by the head of a county-level or higher organ before repatriation. Art. 251 requires the case to be referred up to the Ministry for deportation. Because the latter two are penalties, the Administrative Penalty Law (中华人民共和国行政处罚法, 2021) applies. Art. 40 requires the facts to be established, and art. 59 requires a written decision stating the facts, evidence and legal basis. The Criminal Law separately lets courts impose deportation on foreign offenders. These rules apply to all foreigners. They have limits: art. 62(4) and art. 81(1) contain broad catch-all grounds ("not suitable to continue staying"), and entry can be refused without reasons (art. 25).

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Administrative Penalty Law (中华人民共和国行政处罚法, 2021), arts. 44, 45 and 62, and in art. 64 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012). An order to leave within a time limit (限期出境) and deportation (驱逐出境) are administrative penalties under art. 81 of the Exit-Entry Law. So before deciding either, the authority must "inform the party of the content of the proposed penalty and the facts, reasons and basis, and of the rights to make statements, defend themselves and request a hearing" (art. 44). It "must fully listen to the party's opinions" and review the facts and evidence the party submits (art. 45). A penalty decided without these steps may not be made (art. 62). For repatriation (遣送出境), detention for investigation and restriction of movement, art. 64 of the Exit-Entry Law allows administrative reconsideration by the higher authority, and "the reconsideration decision is final". Art. 249 of the Ministry of Public Security's Provisions on Procedures for Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order No. 149, 2018) repeats this. Art. 241 of the Provisions guarantees an interpreter. The protection has clear limits. Repatriation is not a penalty, so the duty to inform before the decision does not apply to it, and the only review is reconsideration, which may happen after removal. Courts cannot review repatriation (art. 64) or the Ministry's deportation decision, which is "final" (art. 81). Refusal of entry needs no reasons (art. 25).


## Family

### q73 · Right of settled migrants to family reunification.
- Law: Conditional
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China conditionally protects this right in the Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (中华人民共和国外国人入境出境管理条例, State Council Order 637, revised by Order 814 of 2025), arts. 6, 7, 15 and 16, together with the Measures for the Administration of Examination and Approval of Foreigners' Permanent Residence in China (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, MPS/MFA Order 74 of 2004), art. 6. Under art. 6(9) of the Regulations, the Q1 visa is issued to "family members of foreigners with Chinese permanent residence who apply to enter and reside for family reunion". Under art. 6(11), the S1 visa is issued to "the spouse, parents, children under 18 and parents-in-law of foreigners residing in China for work, study or other reasons" who come for long-term family visits. Art. 15 creates matching reunion-type (团聚类) and private-affairs-type (私人事务类) residence permits, and art. 16 sets out the evidence required: proof of the family relationship and the sponsor's residence permit. Permanent residence itself is open to the spouse and unmarried minor children of investors, senior staff and people with major contributions (Measures art. 6(4)). It is also open to the spouse of a permanent resident after five years of marriage and five years' residence (art. 6(5)), to minor children joining their parents (art. 6(6)), and to dependants aged 60 or over with no direct relatives abroad (art. 6(7)). The right is therefore conditional. The family members who qualify depend on the sponsor's status: permanent residents can sponsor "family members", while work and study permit holders can sponsor only the listed close relatives. Adult children, siblings and other non-dependent relatives of work-permit holders have no reunion route. Every grant is discretionary: permits may be refused where it is "not suitable" to issue them (Exit-Entry Administration Law, 出境入境管理法, arts. 21 and 31), and such refusals are final (art. 36).

### q75 · Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57 of 2012), art. 36, states that decisions by public security exit-entry authorities "not to extend, renew or reissue ordinary visas, not to issue foreigners' stay or residence permits, or not to extend the period of residence are final decisions". This covers refusals of the reunion-type and private-affairs-type residence permits through which family reunification is carried out (Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners, arts. 15-16). The Administrative Litigation Law (中华人民共和国行政诉讼法), art. 13(4), bars people's courts from accepting suits against "administrative acts which the law provides shall be finally adjudicated by an administrative organ". This removes court review even though art. 99 gives foreigners equal litigation rights in principle. Refusals of the Q1 or S1 family visas abroad cannot be challenged effectively either: under the Exit-Entry Law, art. 21, "the visa authority may give no reasons", and under art. 25 border authorities may refuse entry without reasons. Detention and repatriation measures, which can break up families, are subject only to administrative reconsideration, "which shall be final" (art. 64). The one possible gap concerns refusals of permanent residence applications by spouses or children under the 2004 PR Measures. These are decided by the Ministry of Public Security and are not expressly covered by art. 36, so in theory they could be challenged as licensing decisions under the Administrative Litigation Law, art. 12(3). No reported case of such a challenge was found, and the main reunification decisions are excluded from court review.

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Civil Code (中华人民共和国民法典, 2020), Book V, defines marriage as a union between a man and a woman. Art. 1046 provides that "marriage shall be entered into by the man and the woman fully voluntarily". Art. 1049 requires that "the man and the woman intending to marry shall apply in person to the marriage registration authority". Same-sex marriages and civil partnerships cannot be registered, and no statute or regulation recognises same-sex marriages concluded abroad. The family reunification framework depends on proof of a recognised family relationship. Under the Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (中华人民共和国外国人入境出境管理条例, Order 637 as revised by Order 814 of 2025), Q1 and S1 visas and the reunion-type and private-affairs-type residence permits for a "spouse" require a "certificate of family-member relationship" (arts. 6, 7, 15, 16). Likewise, the 2004 Permanent Residence Measures require a marriage certificate from spouses (art. 13). A same-sex spouse therefore does not qualify as a "spouse" for any of these permits. The only gap is the residual category of private-affairs permits for "persons needing to reside in China for other private affairs" (art. 15(5)). Local exit-entry offices may use it at their discretion, but it is not a recognition of the relationship, and no published rule extends it to same-sex partners.

### q79 · Right to family reunification without resource conditions
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Family reunification depends on the family member meeting resource conditions. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57 of 2012), art. 21(4), applies to all visas, including the Q1 and S1 family visas. It provides that a visa shall not be issued to a foreigner who "practises fraud in the visa application or cannot guarantee the expenses required during the stay in China". The authority may also refuse in "other circumstances where it considers issuance unsuitable" (art. 21(6)) and need give no reasons. The Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (中华人民共和国外国人入境出境管理条例, revised by Order 814 of 2025), arts. 7 and 16, let visa and exit-entry authorities demand "other application materials" and "materials related to the purpose of the application". In practice these include proof of financial resources and accommodation. Permanent residence is stricter. Under the 2004 Permanent Residence Measures (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, Order 74), art. 6(5), spouses of Chinese citizens or permanent residents must have "a stable livelihood and residence". Elderly dependants must likewise have "stable livelihood guarantees and residence" (art. 6(7)). Under art. 14, spouses must submit a "notarised certificate of livelihood guarantee and a lease or property certificate". Under art. 16, dependants must submit a "notarised proof of the applicant's source of income or the sponsor's financial guarantee". These income, maintenance and housing requirements mean that no avenue for family reunification exists regardless of resources.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right for all migrants. National law in China grants citizenship to children who would otherwise be stateless only in one narrow case. Article 6 of the Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国籍法, 1980) provides: "Any person born in China whose parents are stateless or of uncertain nationality and have settled in China shall have Chinese nationality." The safeguard requires birth in China, parents who are stateless or of uncertain nationality, and parents who have "settled" (定居) in China, which in practice means lawful long-term residence. It does not cover a child born in China to foreign-national parents who cannot pass on their nationality, a child born abroad, or a child whose stateless parents are irregularly present and not settled. Chinese nationality otherwise passes by descent from a Chinese parent (arts. 4-5). Stateless adults have no right to citizenship. Under Article 7 they may only apply to naturalise, at the discretion of the Ministry of Public Security, if they are near relatives of Chinese nationals, have settled in China, or have other legitimate reasons. Article 8 adds the requirement to give up any foreign nationality. There is no statelessness determination procedure, and China is not party to the 1954 or 1961 statelessness conventions.


## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China protects this right in various degrees for different categories of migrants. The general health statute, the Law on Basic Medical and Health Care and the Promotion of Health (中华人民共和国基本医疗卫生与健康促进法, 2019), frames the entitlement for citizens. Article 5 says "citizens enjoy, in accordance with law, the right to obtain basic medical and health services from the State and society". Article 82 says "citizens have the right and obligation to participate in basic medical insurance". The one guarantee that does not depend on status is emergency care. Article 27 provides that emergency centres "shall not refuse or delay emergency services to critically ill patients on the ground of non-payment". Coverage on the same terms as citizens comes through social insurance, and only for lawfully employed foreigners. Article 97 of the Social Insurance Law (中华人民共和国社会保险法, 2010, amended 2018) provides that "foreigners employed within China shall participate in social insurance with reference to this Law". The Interim Measures for the Participation in Social Insurance of Foreigners Employed in China (在中国境内就业的外国人参加社会保险暂行办法, MOHRSS Order No. 16, amended by Order No. 54 of 2024) set out how this works. Art. 3 makes foreigners lawfully hired by employers participate in employee basic medical insurance and the other branches of social insurance. Under art. 2, this covers only holders of a work permit or resident-journalist card plus a residence permit, and permanent residence holders who are lawfully employed. The 2012 Measures on Treatment of Foreigners with Permanent Residence (人社部发〔2012〕53号, item 9) let permanent residents who are not employed join urban resident basic medical insurance "where they meet the requirements of the pooling region". There is no comparable right for students, dependants, short-term visitors, asylum seekers or irregular migrants. Outside emergencies, they must pay their own costs or buy private insurance.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. No national law or regulation gives foreign migrants access to publicly supported housing on the same terms as citizens. The main national rule is the Measures for the Administration of Public Rental Housing (公共租赁住房管理办法, MOHURD Order No. 11, 2012). Art. 3 defines public rental housing as housing let to "urban lower-middle-income families with housing difficulties, newly employed workers without housing, and migrant workers (外来务工人员) in stable urban employment". Here 外来务工人员 means internal migrants, not foreign nationals. Art. 7 requires no local housing, income and assets below set limits and, for migrant workers, stable local employment for a set number of years. It leaves the detailed conditions to municipal and county governments. The State Council General Office Opinion on Accelerating the Development of Affordable Rental Housing (国办发〔2021〕22号) is likewise aimed at "new urban residents and young people" and lets city governments set the entry conditions. Neither instrument mentions foreigners. The 2012 Measures on Treatment of Foreigners with Permanent Residence (人社部发〔2012〕53号) say permanent residents have "in principle the same rights as Chinese citizens" (item 1). On housing they grant only two specific rights: to pay into and use the housing provident fund (item 10), and to buy commercial housing for their own use without the one-year residence condition (item 11). They do not give access to public rental or affordable housing. Under the MRD rules, that general equality-in-principle clause does not amount to a specific guarantee. Temporary residents and irregular migrants have no entitlement at all. Any access foreigners get through local talent-housing or affordable-rental schemes rests on local discretion, not national law.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Compulsory Education Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国义务教育法, 2006, amended 2015 and 2018) makes the nine years of compulsory education free (art. 2: "compulsory education shall be implemented without charging tuition or miscellaneous fees"). However, the entitlement belongs only to nationals. Art. 4 provides that "all school-age children and adolescents who hold the nationality of the People's Republic of China ... enjoy the equal right to receive compulsory education according to law". Art. 12 ties enrolment to the hukou (household registration) locality, a system foreign children cannot enter. The Education Law (中华人民共和国教育法, amended 2021) likewise frames the right to education and equal educational opportunity for "citizens of the People's Republic of China" (art. 9). Foreign children are dealt with separately as "international students". The Measures for the Administration of Schools Recruiting and Training International Students (学校招收和培养国际学生管理办法, Order No. 42 of 2017), art. 2, leaves their admission, teaching and school management at primary and secondary level to provincial rules. The 1999 Ministry of Education measures on primary and secondary schools admitting foreign students let provincial education and price authorities set the fees they are charged. No statute or regulation guarantees free primary schooling to foreign, refugee or undocumented children on the same terms as citizens. A 2012 inter-ministerial policy document, the Measures on Treatment Enjoyed by Foreigners with Permanent Residence (外国人在中国永久居留享有相关待遇的办法, item 8), says that children of permanent-residence holders who "meet the conditions" may enrol in compulsory education near where they live, with no fees beyond those set by the state. That document is a conditional, administrative normative document covering only a tiny group, not a legal guarantee. Free schooling given to some Myanmar children in Yunnan border schools is local practice, not a legal entitlement.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Labour Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国劳动法, 1994, amended 2009 and 2018), Article 46, which provides that "wage distribution shall follow the principle of distribution according to work and implement equal pay for equal work" (实行同工同酬). Article 3 gives every worker the right to "obtain remuneration for labour", and Article 48 sets a minimum wage system binding on all employers. The Labour Law applies to all workers who have a labour relationship with an employer in China (Art. 2). The Provisions on the Administration of Employment of Foreigners in China (外国人在中国就业管理规定, 劳部发〔1996〕29号, amended 2010 and 2017) bring lawfully employed foreigners under this framework. Article 21 requires that a foreigner's wages "shall not be lower than the local minimum wage standard", and Article 25 sends labour disputes with foreign employees to the Labour Law and the Labour Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Law. No rule allows lower pay for foreign nationals. The protection is limited to foreigners working lawfully with a work permit and work-type residence permit (Exit and Entry Administration Law, art. 41). Article 33 of the Supreme People's Court Interpretation (I) on Labour Disputes (法释〔2020〕26号) provides that where a foreigner without an employment permit signs a labour contract, a claim that a labour relationship exists "shall not be supported". Irregular migrant workers therefore cannot enforce the equal-pay principle in labour arbitration or the courts. The equal-pay rule is general and does not name nationality as a prohibited ground.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Employment is tied to specific employer; change of employer possible, but requires new work permit application
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China protects this right only in a limited way. Under the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 主席令第57号, 2012), Art. 41, a foreigner may work only with a work permit and a work-type residence permit. Under Art. 43(2), working "beyond the scope limited by the work permit" is illegal employment. The Provisions on the Administration of Employment of Foreigners in China (外国人在中国就业管理规定, 1996, amended 2017), Art. 23, provide that "the employer of a foreigner employed in China must be the same as the unit indicated on the employment certificate". A change of employer within the same region and occupation needs the issuing authority's approval. A change of region, or a change of employer with a different occupation, needs a new employment permit. Art. 28 lets the labour authority withdraw the permit of a foreigner who changes employer or occupation without authorisation, and ask the police to cancel residence. Art. 20 requires the employer to report termination and arrange the foreigner's exit. The State Administration of Foreign Experts Affairs' Foreigner's Work Permit Service Guide (外国人来华工作许可服务指南（暂行）, 外专发〔2017〕36号) lets a foreigner who changes employer, in the same post and with a valid work-type residence permit, apply in China. However, the person "shall first cancel the existing work permit" and submit a full new application. Foreigners with permanent residence may work without a work permit (Exit and Entry Administration Law, Art. 48).

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The spouse of a principal migrant has no automatic right to work. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 主席令第57号, 2012), Art. 41, requires every foreigner working in China to "obtain a work permit and a work-type residence permit". It forbids any unit or individual to employ a foreigner without them. The Provisions on the Administration of Employment of Foreigners in China (外国人在中国就业管理规定, 1996, amended 2017), Art. 8, expressly provide that "accompanying family members of foreigners holding Z visas may not be employed in China". In special cases the employer must apply for a permit through the normal approval procedure, and the person must change status with the police before working. Family-reunion and private-affairs residence permits do not authorise work (Exit and Entry Administration Law, Arts. 37 and 43). The national Foreigner's Work Permit Service Guide (外专发〔2017〕36号) eases only the procedure: "spouses or children of foreigners residing permanently or working in China", and foreign spouses of Chinese citizens, may apply for a work permit from inside China. They still need a sponsoring employer and an individual work permit, which is subject to the general conditions and labour-market test (Employment Provisions, Arts. 6-7). Only spouses who themselves obtain permanent residence (Measures on Foreigners' Permanent Residence 2004, family categories) may work without a permit (Exit and Entry Administration Law, Art. 48).

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment automatically implies the loss of immigration status
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The work-type residence permit depends on a specific job. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 主席令第57号, 2012), art. 41, requires a foreigner working in China to hold both "a work permit and a work-type residence permit". The Provisions on the Administration of Employment of Foreigners in China (外国人在中国就业管理规定, 劳部发〔1996〕29号, amended 2017) tie the residence permit to the employment permit (art. 16). Art. 20 provides that once the labour contract is terminated, "the employer shall promptly report to the labour and public security departments, return the foreigner's employment permit and residence permit, and go through exit formalities at the public security organ." Changing employer requires prior approval or a new permit (arts. 19 and 23). The Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (国务院令第637号, revised by 国务院令第814号, 2025) require the employer to report when an employee leaves (art. 26). If a change in the reason for residence is not reported, the permit can be declared void (art. 34(3)). The Exit-Entry Law, art. 33, requires any change in registered particulars, including the reason for residence, to be reported within 10 days. No statute or regulation gives a grace period for finding new work. In practice, exit-entry offices issue a short transitional stay permit of up to about 30 days after cancelling the residence permit. This is an administrative practice, not a statutory right. The rule covers holders of work-type residence permits. Permanent residents (Exit-Entry Law art. 48) and holders of family-reunion or private-affairs permits do not depend on employment.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Migrants have a right to most of the employment conditions and protections for citizens
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in China protects this right for lawfully employed foreigners in the Labour Law (中华人民共和国劳动法, 1994, amended 2018) and the Provisions on the Administration of Employment of Foreigners in China (外国人在中国就业管理规定, 1996, amended 2017). Under Art. 22 of the Provisions, the "working hours, rest, leave, labour safety and hygiene and social insurance" of foreigners employed in China follow the relevant national rules. Art. 25 sends their labour disputes to the Labour Law and the Labour Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Law. The Labour Law therefore gives foreign permit holders an 8-hour day and 44-hour week (Art. 36), at least one rest day a week (Art. 38), limits on overtime (Art. 41) with overtime premiums of 150-300% (Art. 44), paid annual leave (Art. 45), occupational safety duties (Arts. 52-54) and protection against dismissal during sickness, injury or pregnancy (Art. 29). The Interim Measures on Social Insurance for Foreigners Employed in China (2011, amended 2024) bring them into the compulsory social insurance schemes. The Supreme People's Court confirms that foreigners holding a work permit can have a recognised labour relationship (Interpretation (I) on Labour Disputes, 法释〔2020〕26号, Art. 33(2)). Protections are fewer in several respects. Contracts with foreigners may last at most five years (Provisions, Art. 17), and the employment certificate lapses when the contract ends (Art. 18). This limits access to the open-ended contract that Labour Law Art. 20 offers after ten years' continuous service. The employer must end the contract when the police cancel the foreigner's residence (Art. 24), and termination triggers exit procedures (Art. 20). Foreigners working without a permit have no recognised labour relationship at all (Interpretation (I), Art. 33(1)).

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Access to some, but not all types of social security protections (e.g., no equal access under this program; or access depending on visa type)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China protects migrant workers' access to social insurance in Article 97 of the Social Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国社会保险法, 2010, amended 2018): "Foreigners employed within the territory of China shall participate in social insurance with reference to this Law." The Interim Measures for the Participation in Social Insurance of Foreigners Employed in China (在中国境内就业的外国人参加社会保险暂行办法, MOHRSS Order No. 16 of 2011, amended by Order No. 54 of 23 December 2024) put this into effect. Article 2 covers foreigners who hold a PRC Foreigner's Work Permit (or a resident journalist card) and a residence permit, as well as permanent-residence holders. Article 3 says such employees "shall participate in basic pension insurance, basic medical insurance, work-injury insurance, unemployment insurance and maternity insurance" in the same way as Chinese employees. Article 5 grants benefits to those who meet the conditions. It also lets a worker who leaves before pension age keep the personal account or have its balance paid out in a lump sum. Article 8 opens mediation, arbitration and litigation. Coverage starts on hiring: employers must register the worker within 30 days of the work permit being issued (art. 4). Benefits then depend on the same contribution periods that apply to citizens, such as 15 years for a monthly pension (SIL art. 16). The protection is incomplete in three ways. First, non-contributory social assistance is framed for citizens and tied to household registration. The Interim Measures on Social Assistance (社会救助暂行办法, State Council Order 649, 2014/2019) aim to "guarantee the basic livelihood of citizens" (art. 1), and claims for minimum living support go to the township of one's "place of household registration" (art. 11). Work-permit holders are therefore excluded. Second, under art. 9, nationals of the 12 countries with bilateral agreements in force (e.g. Germany, Korea, Japan) may be exempt from some branches. Third, only regular workers with permits are covered. Irregular migrants are excluded.

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: Access to some, but not all types of unemployment benefits; OR not to the same extent as citizens; OR it depends on their visa type (e.g., temporary vs. permanent, high-skill vs. low skill)
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in part. Article 97 of the Social Insurance Law (中华人民共和国社会保险法, 2010, amended 2018) and Article 3 of the Interim Measures for the Participation in Social Insurance of Foreigners Employed in China (在中国境内就业的外国人参加社会保险暂行办法, MOHRSS Order No. 16 of 2011, amended by Order No. 54 of 2024) require foreigners with a work permit and residence permit to pay into "unemployment insurance". Article 5 of the Measures says they "enjoy social insurance benefits according to law" when they meet the conditions. The conditions are those that apply to citizens under SIL Article 45: contributions for at least one year, involuntary loss of employment, and having "completed unemployment registration and [having] a job-seeking requirement". Benefits last 12 to 24 months depending on contribution years (art. 46), and claimants must register with the public employment service (art. 50). Foreigners cannot meet these conditions on the same terms as citizens. A Z-type work permit and the work-type residence permit depend on a specific job, and there is no job-seeker status for a foreigner who has lost work. Benefits stop when the claimant "emigrates abroad" (art. 51(3)). The interim Measures make no special provision for foreign claimants. Local rules fill the gap selectively. Shanghai's 2023 notice (沪人社就〔2023〕409号) lets holders of the Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance benefits. It does not extend this to ordinary work-permit holders. Under art. 9 of the Measures, nationals of countries with bilateral agreements covering unemployment insurance (e.g. Korea, Germany) may be exempt from this branch. In effect, access depends on residence status: permanent residents can claim, while temporary work-permit holders contribute but rarely qualify. Irregular workers are excluded altogether.

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in a limited way. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 主席令第57号, 2012), art. 47, provides that foreigners who have made outstanding contributions "or meet other conditions for permanent residence in China" obtain permanent residence on application, with approval by the Ministry of Public Security. Art. 48 lets permanent residents live and work in China on that basis. The Measures for the Administration of Examination and Approval of Foreigners' Permanent Residence in China (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, 公安部、外交部令第74号, 2004), art. 6(2), open an employment-based route. It covers foreigners who hold posts of "deputy general manager, deputy factory director or above, or a senior title of associate professor, associate researcher or above". They must have held the post continuously for four years, resided in China for at least three of those years, and have a good tax record. Art. 8 limits qualifying employers to central or provincial government bodies, key universities, entities running national key projects, and high-tech or encouraged foreign-invested enterprises. Art. 11 requires the Foreign Expert Certificate or Employment Permit. Other routes cover investors (arts. 6(1), 7), people with major contributions (art. 6(3)), and spouses and dependants (arts. 6(4)-(7)). All applicants must obey Chinese law, be healthy and have no criminal record. So a temporary worker can become a permanent resident, but only senior, highly qualified staff of qualifying employers can do so through work.

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The route from work to permanent residence depends on job title and employer type, not on lawful residence alone. Under the Measures for the Administration of Examination and Approval of Foreigners' Permanent Residence in China (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, 公安部、外交部令第74号, 2004), art. 6(2), only foreigners who hold "deputy general manager, deputy factory director or above" posts or senior academic titles (associate professor, associate researcher or above) qualify. They need four continuous years in post and three years' residence. Art. 8 further limits the route to staff of government bodies, key universities, entities running national key projects, and high-tech or encouraged foreign-invested enterprises. Ordinary Z-visa workers, language teachers, staff of other private firms, interns and students working part-time therefore have no work-based path. So do the irregular or seasonal cross-border workers in border provinces, however long they have worked lawfully. The other routes in art. 6 depend on investment of at least USD 500,000 to 2 million (art. 7), major contributions certified by a government body (art. 12), or family ties to a Chinese citizen or permanent resident (art. 6(5)-(7)). None of these is open to temporary workers as such. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (主席令第57号), art. 47, leaves the conditions to these Measures.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, but limited
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China protects this right only for lawfully employed migrant workers. The Provisions on the Administration of Employment of Foreigners in China (外国人在中国就业管理规定, 劳部发〔1996〕29号, amended 2017) say that labour disputes between an employer and an employed foreigner "shall be handled in accordance with the Labour Law and the Labour Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Law" (art. 25). They also guarantee at least the local minimum wage (art. 21) and apply national rules on hours, rest, safety and social insurance (art. 22). Lawfully employed foreigners can therefore bring claims for unpaid wages, unlawful dismissal, compensation and damages through mediation, arbitration and the people's courts, as citizens can. Several limits apply. Labour contracts may last at most five years and end automatically at expiry (art. 17). Remedies depend on holding a valid work permit. The Supreme People's Court Interpretation (I) on Labour Dispute Cases (最高人民法院关于审理劳动争议案件适用法律问题的解释（一）, 法释〔2020〕26号), art. 33, provides that where foreigners or stateless persons sign a labour contract "without having obtained employment documents in accordance with the law", a claim that a labour relationship exists "shall not be supported". The Exit-Entry Law (主席令第57号), art. 41, bars employment without a work permit. Irregular or undocumented workers, and workers whose permits have lapsed, thus fall outside the Labour Contract Law. They can claim only payment for work done and fault-based civil damages, not statutory severance, compensation or reinstatement.

### q135 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of race or ethnicity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in China prohibits discrimination on the ground of race or ethnicity in employment, in the Labour Law (中华人民共和国劳动法, 1994, amended 2018), Article 12, which provides that "workers shall not be discriminated against in employment on the basis of ethnicity, race, sex or religious belief" (不因民族、种族、性别、宗教信仰不同而受歧视). Article 3(2) of the Employment Promotion Law (中华人民共和国就业促进法, 2007, amended 2015) repeats the ban for "ethnicity, race, sex, religious belief, etc.". Its Article 26 requires employers and employment agencies to provide "equal employment opportunities and fair employment conditions" and not to engage in employment discrimination. These provisions cover all workers in a labour relationship in China (Labour Law, Art. 2), including lawfully employed foreigners, whose disputes go to the Labour Law (Employment Provisions 1996/2017, Art. 25). The protection is narrow. It applies to employment only. No general statute bans racial discrimination against foreigners in housing, services or treatment by public authorities. The Constitution's equality and nationalities clauses are framed for citizens and China's own ethnic groups. Foreigners working without a permit cannot invoke Labour Law protections (SPC Interpretation (I) on Labour Disputes, Art. 33). The UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination found in 2018 that domestic law contains no definition of racial discrimination in full conformity with article 1 of the Convention and that China has no comprehensive anti-discrimination law.


## Legal personhood

### q27 · Right not to have identity documents confiscated.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right for lawfully employed migrant workers in the Labour Contract Law (中华人民共和国劳动合同法, 2007, amended 2012), arts. 9 and 84. Article 9 provides that "when recruiting workers, the employer shall not detain the worker's resident identity card or other certificates, and shall not require the worker to provide a guarantee or collect property from the worker under any other name." Article 84(1) sets the sanction: where an employer "detains a worker's resident identity card or other certificates, the labour administration department shall order it to return them to the worker within a time limit and impose penalties in accordance with relevant laws." The words "other certificates" (其他证件) cover a foreign worker's passport, work permit and residence permit. The protection is limited in three ways. First, it binds only employers (用人单位) in a labour relationship. No general rule forbids other private actors, such as recruiters, landlords, brokers or the buyers of trafficked brides, from holding a foreigner's passport. The Resident Identity Card Law's ban on detaining ID cards covers only Chinese citizens' cards. Second, it reaches only foreigners in a recognised labour relationship. Foreigners working without the work permit and work-type residence permit required by Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012) arts. 41 and 43 are in illegal employment, and in practice they fall outside labour-contract protection. Third, the article 84 sanction is only an order to return the documents plus penalties under other laws; the Labour Contract Law itself sets no fine. Public security organs may lawfully inspect, cancel or confiscate exit-entry documents (Exit-Entry Law arts. 38 and 67).

### q29 · Right to birth registration.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China requires registration of foreign children born in China in the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012), art. 40(1): "For a foreign infant born in China, the parents or agent shall, within sixty days of the birth, take the infant's birth certificate to the exit-entry administration of the public security organ at or above county level where the parents stay or reside and complete stay or residence registration for the infant." The duty is attached to the fact of birth in China. It is not conditioned on the parents' immigration status, so on its face it applies to children of regular and irregular migrants alike. It is, however, an immigration (stay or residence) registration with the police, not civil-status registration. China has no separate civil birth register for foreigners. Children with at least one Chinese parent are Chinese nationals under the Nationality Law and are registered in the household registration (hukou) system instead. Because the registration is made with the police exit-entry authority, an irregular parent who registers a child exposes themselves to detection, detention and repatriation under arts. 59-62 of the same Law. Children born to undocumented mothers, including trafficked brides and North Korean women, often cannot complete either the art. 40 registration or hukou registration. No provision separates birth registration from immigration enforcement.

### q31 · Right to government document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China mandates documents recognising migrants' residence, work status and identity in the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012). Under art. 30, a foreigner whose visa requires a residence document must apply within 30 days of entry, and the police exit-entry authority "shall examine and decide within 15 days ... and issue a foreigner's residence certificate of the corresponding category and period according to the purpose of residence." Work-type residence permits last 90 days to five years; other types last 180 days to five years. Art. 33 lists the permit's entries: name, sex, date of birth, purpose and period of residence, and passport number. Art. 34 provides for stay certificates (up to 180 days). Art. 41 makes a work permit and a work-type residence certificate the legal basis for any employment. Under art. 48, permanent residents live and work on a permanent residence certificate. Under art. 46, asylum seekers "may stay in China with a temporary identity certificate issued by the public security organ" during status determination, and recognised refugees receive a refugee identity certificate. Refusals to issue or extend residence documents are final (art. 36), and issuance is discretionary where the authority "considers it inappropriate" (art. 31(5)). Irregular migrants receive no document, and art. 38 requires foreigners over 16 to carry their passport or residence document.

### q33 · Right against destruction of document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China makes it a crime to destroy official documents in the Criminal Law (中华人民共和国刑法, 1997, as amended), art. 280(1): "Whoever forges, alters, buys or sells, or steals, seizes or destroys official documents, certificates or seals of state organs shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years, criminal detention, public surveillance or deprivation of political rights, and a fine; where the circumstances are serious, to fixed-term imprisonment of three to ten years and a fine." Foreigners' residence and stay permits, work permits and permanent residence cards are issued by Chinese public security and labour authorities, so they are "certificates of state organs" covered by the offence. The provision protects these documents whoever holds them and whoever destroys them; it is not limited to employers. A migrant's foreign passport is issued by a foreign state, not a Chinese state organ, so art. 280(1) does not clearly reach it. Destroying a passport would then fall only under general property-damage rules. Article 280(3) separately punishes forging, altering or trading identity documents such as passports, but not destroying them. The Supreme People's Court's 2024 guidance (Fadawang answers) states that art. 280 offences in practice need a threshold of seriousness (by analogy, about three documents), so destroying a single permit may be dealt with administratively rather than prosecuted. Public security organs may lawfully cancel or confiscate invalid exit-entry documents (Exit and Entry Administration Law art. 67).


## Liberty

### q15 · Right to communicate with consular officials.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China protects this right in the Provisions on Procedures for Public Security Organs Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order 149 of 2018, as amended), arts. 254 and 256, and the Provisions on Procedures for Public Security Organs Handling Criminal Cases (公安机关办理刑事案件程序规定, MPS Order 159 of 2020), arts. 368 and 370. Art. 254 covers foreigners placed in administrative detention, detention for investigation (拘留审查, the main immigration-detention measure) or restriction of movement. The deciding organ must report the case to the provincial public security organ within 48 hours. The provincial organ must then 'notify the embassy or consulate in China of the country to which the foreigner belongs'. Art. 256 provides that where the foreigner's 'diplomatic or consular officials in China request a visit, the deciding organ shall promptly arrange it.' Arts. 368 and 370 of the criminal provisions impose the same duties for foreign suspects under bail, residential surveillance, criminal detention or arrest, and for prisoners serving sentences in detention centres. Both texts let the detainee refuse notification or a visit, but only by a personal written statement. Notification may be dropped at the detainee's request only where no bilateral consular agreement requires it. The duty covers foreign nationals only. China does not recognise dual nationality (Nationality Law art. 3), so dual nationals treated as Chinese citizens fall outside it. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (出境入境管理法) does not itself mention consular contact. China acceded to the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations in 1979, but the treaty is applied through these implementing rules and bilateral consular agreements rather than directly by the courts.

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012) gives only a partial age-based exception. Art. 61 provides that detention for investigation 'does not apply' to foreigners 'under sixteen or over seventy', or who are seriously ill or pregnant. These people may instead have their 'scope of activities restricted' for up to 60 days, and that period runs only once nationality or identity is established. This is a confinement measure in its own right. Under it the person may not leave the designated area without police approval. The exception does not cover 16- and 17-year-olds. Art. 63 has no age exception at all. It provides that persons detained for investigation 'or decided to be repatriated but whose repatriation cannot be executed immediately, shall be held in a detention house or repatriation facility'. That allows children awaiting removal to be held in custody. The Provisions on Procedures for Public Security Organs Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order 149) repeat the same scheme (arts. 244 and 248). No statute or regulation sets a general ban on detaining migrant children, a best-interests test, or a last-resort rule for immigration detention. China ratified the Convention on the Rights of the Child in 1992, but courts do not apply it directly.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Under the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012), migration status is itself a ground for detention. Art. 59 allows questioning and continued questioning of persons suspected of illegal entry or exit, or of foreigners suspected of 'illegal residence or illegal employment'. Art. 60 allows a foreigner to be 'detained for investigation' (拘留审查) where suspicion cannot be dispelled after questioning. Detention lasts up to 30 days, extendable to 60 for complex cases. For a foreigner whose nationality or identity is unclear, the period 'is calculated from the date on which nationality and identity are ascertained', so the total length of detention has no effective cap. Art. 62 makes illegal residence and illegal employment grounds for repatriation. Art. 63 requires persons detained for investigation, or awaiting a repatriation that cannot be carried out at once, to be 'held in a detention house or repatriation facility'. Art. 64 limits challenges to administrative reconsideration, whose decision is 'final', so a court never reviews the detention. The Provisions on Procedures for Public Security Organs Handling Administrative Cases (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定, MPS Order 149, arts. 242-249) implement the same scheme. Neither text requires a necessity, proportionality or last-resort test. Art. 61 offers non-custodial restriction of movement only to the seriously ill, pregnant or breastfeeding women, people under 16 or over 70, and other unsuitable cases.

### q21 · Right to appeal legality, conditions, and length of detention
- Law: Yes, one of the three (legality, conditions, length)
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in China protects this right only in part, in the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57 of 2012), art. 64, read with the Administrative Reconsideration Law (中华人民共和国行政复议法, revised 2023), arts. 10, 11 and 44. Under art. 64, a foreigner who "objects to continued questioning, detention for investigation, restriction of the scope of activity or repatriation" imposed under the Law "may apply for administrative reconsideration in accordance with the law, and the reconsideration decision is final." The Reconsideration Law lets people challenge administrative compulsory measures (art. 11(2)). It puts the burden of proving "legality and appropriateness" on the authority that made the decision (art. 44). Because art. 64 makes reconsideration final, the usual route to court is closed (art. 10). Reconsideration does not suspend the measure unless the authorities decide it should (art. 42). The review therefore covers whether detention is lawful, and it is an internal administrative review rather than an appeal to a court. Length is not separately reviewable. Art. 60 caps detention for investigation at 30 days, extendable to 60 with the approval of the higher-level authority. But for foreigners whose nationality or identity is unclear, the period only starts once that is established. No provision allows a detainee to challenge the duration or seek periodic review. Nor is there any procedure in the immigration law for complaining about conditions in the detention houses or repatriation centres where detainees are held (art. 63). Administrative detention imposed as a penalty for illegal stay or work (arts. 78, 80) can be challenged through ordinary reconsideration and administrative litigation, but that again concerns legality. The protection applies to all foreigners, including irregular migrants. Only legality is covered.

### q23 · Right to an individualized detention decision.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57 of 2012), arts. 59-61. Detention is not mandatory for irregular stay or work. A person suspected of illegal entry, illegal stay or illegal work may first be questioned on the spot and then subjected to continued questioning (art. 59). Detention for investigation is allowed only where, "after on-the-spot or continued questioning, the suspicion still cannot be excluded and further investigation is needed" (art. 60). Even then, the Law says the foreigner "may" be detained, which leaves it to the authority's discretion. The detaining authority must produce a written detention-for-investigation decision and question the person within 24 hours. "Where it is found that detention should not apply, the detention shall be lifted immediately" (art. 60). Art. 61 requires a further assessment of the individual's circumstances. Detention "does not apply" to foreigners who are seriously ill, pregnant or nursing a child under one, under 16 or over 70, or in "other circumstances where detention is not suitable". Such people may instead have their scope of activity restricted, for up to 60 days. The Law thus ties detention to the facts of the individual case, allows an alternative and requires a reasoned written decision. It sets no necessity or proportionality test beyond the need for further investigation. Repatriation (art. 62) and holding people pending removal in detention houses or repatriation centres (art. 63) are also discretionary ("may"). The provisions apply to all foreigners and other persons from outside China, including irregular migrants.

### q25 · Right to be free from torture and inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Law (中华人民共和国刑事诉讼法, amended 2018), arts. 17, 52 and 56; the Criminal Law (中华人民共和国刑法, as amended), arts. 247-248; and the People's Police Law (中华人民共和国人民警察法, 1995, amended 2012), art. 22. The Criminal Procedure Law applies to foreigners (art. 17) and provides that "extorting confessions by torture and collecting evidence by threats, enticement, deceit or other illegal means is strictly prohibited" (art. 52). Statements obtained this way must be excluded (art. 56). Under Criminal Law art. 247, judicial officers who extort confessions by torture or extract witness testimony by violence face up to three years' imprisonment, or heavier punishment as intentional injury or homicide if the victim is injured or dies. Art. 248 punishes supervisory staff of prisons, detention houses and other custodial institutions who "beat or inflict corporal punishment or maltreatment" on detainees, or who instruct other detainees to do so. Police Law art. 22 forbids police from "extorting confessions by torture or inflicting corporal punishment or maltreatment on persons in custody" and from beating anyone. Together these provisions prohibit torture and also maltreatment and corporal punishment of detainees, which covers the substance of inhuman or degrading treatment in custody. They apply regardless of nationality, so they cover foreigners held in immigration detention houses and repatriation centres (Exit-Entry Law art. 63). There are limits. The torture offence is defined narrowly, by reference to obtaining confessions or testimony. Chinese law contains no general prohibition using the words "cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment". The constitutional guarantees of personal freedom and dignity (arts. 37-38) are framed for citizens. And no statute bars return to a country where a person faces torture.


## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. National law authorises, rather than prohibits, the use of force by officers to stop unauthorised entry. The Land Borders Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国陆地国界法, Presidential Order No. 99, 2021), art. 38, provides that 'no individual may illegally cross the border'; non-violent crossers who are 'brought under control' are handed to the public security organs. If they 'commit violence, resist arrest or engage in other violent acts endangering the personal and property safety of others, law-enforcement personnel may lawfully use police equipment and weapons.' Art. 7 also tasks the PLA and the People's Armed Police with preventing, stopping and striking at illegal border crossing. At sea, the Coast Guard Law (中华人民共和国海警法, 2021), art. 46, allows officers to use police equipment 'when boarding, inspecting, intercepting or hot-pursuing vessels and it is necessary to force the vessel to stop', and 'when forcibly expelling or towing away vessels'. Art. 47 allows hand-held weapons, after a warning, against vessels carrying criminal suspects that refuse to stop. The general Regulations on the Use of Police Equipment and Weapons by the People's Police (人民警察使用警械和武器条例, State Council Order No. 191, 1996), art. 2, let police 'adopt coercive means' to stop unlawful acts. Under art. 7, police may use batons, tear gas and water cannon against anyone who forcibly breaches a police cordon or violently resists officers. Art. 16 extends these rules to the People's Armed Police. These provisions limit force by proportionality, but none bars force as a means of stopping migrants from entering. They apply to all foreigners, regular or irregular, who seek to cross outside authorised ports.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in China restricts lethal force at the land border in the Land Borders Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国陆地国界法, Presidential Order No. 99, 2021), art. 38. The article provides that illegal border-crossers, once brought under control, 'are dealt with by the public security organs'. Weapons may be used only where crossers 'commit violence, resist arrest or commit other violent acts endangering the personal and property safety of others.' Firearms are therefore not authorised merely to prevent a non-violent person from crossing. The Regulations on the Use of Police Equipment and Weapons by the People's Police (人民警察使用警械和武器条例, State Council Order No. 191, 1996), art. 9, allow 'lethal' weapons (art. 3) only in listed 'emergency situations of violent crime'. These include violent resistance endangering officers' lives; illegal border crossing as such is not listed. Art. 10 bars weapons against pregnant women and children, and art. 16 applies the Regulations to the People's Armed Police. There is an important exception at sea. The Coast Guard Law (中华人民共和国海警法, 2021), art. 47, allows hand-held weapons, after an ineffective warning, where 'there is evidence that a vessel carries criminal suspects' and it 'refuses to obey an order to stop'. Art. 49 permits use without warning where there is no time. Art. 50 requires officers to judge 'the necessary limit' and avoid unnecessary casualties. Smuggling vessels could fall within art. 47, so the protection is complete on land but conditional at sea. It applies to all migrants.

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: No, government may punish trafficked or smuggled migrants
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. No statute exempts trafficked or smuggled persons from criminal or administrative punishment for illegal entry or related offences. Under the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012), art. 71, anyone who evades border inspection or 'exits or enters illegally by other means' is fined 1,000-5,000 yuan. In serious cases the penalty is 5-10 days' detention plus a fine. Arts. 60 and 62 allow detention for investigation and deportation of foreigners who enter illegally. Criminal Law art. 322 makes illegal border crossing a crime in 'serious circumstances'. The SPC/SPP Interpretation on cases obstructing border administration (法释〔2012〕17号), art. 5, defines these to include crossing 'three or more times or three or more persons crossing together'. Smuggled groups can therefore meet the threshold. Art. 6 treats entry 'without entry-exit documents or evading border inspection' as illegal crossing, with no carve-out for persons who were smuggled or trafficked. The 2022 joint Opinion of the SPC, SPP, MPS and NIA (法发〔2022〕18号), para. 11, allows minor guides of illegal crossings to go unprosecuted, but this benefits smugglers, not the migrants themselves. Para. 10 adds aggravating circumstances for crossers. The Criminal Law punishes traffickers and buyers (arts. 240-241), but neither it nor the anti-trafficking rules contain a non-punishment clause for victims. Trafficked and smuggled foreigners can therefore be fined, detained, prosecuted and deported.


## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Convictions do not affect citizenship status
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in China protects this right, because the Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国籍法, 1980, NPC Standing Committee Chairman's Order No. 8) has no provision that lets the State revoke or deprive a person of nationality, whether acquired by birth or by naturalisation. The law lists only a few ways to lose Chinese nationality. Under Article 9, nationality is lost automatically when a citizen settled abroad 'voluntarily joins or acquires foreign nationality'. Under Articles 10-11, a person may renounce nationality by application and approval. Article 14 states that, apart from the Article 9 case, 'the acquisition, loss and restoration of Chinese nationality must go through application procedures'. Loss therefore depends on the person's own act. No article ties loss of nationality to a criminal conviction, an administrative penalty or conduct against the State. Article 7 does require applicants for naturalisation to be 'willing to abide by China's Constitution and laws', but this is a condition at the time of application. It is not a ground for withdrawing nationality later. The Act also has no specific clause on revoking naturalisation obtained by fraud. A naturalised Chinese citizen who is convicted of a crime keeps Chinese nationality and faces the ordinary criminal penalties applied to citizens. Deportation under Criminal Law art. 35 applies only to foreigners.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in China provides a pathway to citizenship for economic migrants in Article 7 of the Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国籍法, 1980). Article 7 provides that 'foreigners or stateless persons who are willing to abide by China's Constitution and laws and who meet one of the following conditions may, upon application and approval, be naturalised as Chinese nationals: (1) close relatives of Chinese nationals; (2) persons who have settled in China; (3) persons with other legitimate reasons.' Economic migrants qualify under the 'settled in China' limb. In practice that means permanent residence. Under Article 47 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012), the Ministry of Public Security grants permanent residence to foreigners who have made 'outstanding contributions' or meet other conditions. Article 6 of the Measures for the Administration of Examination and Approval of Foreigners' Permanent Residence in China (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, MPS/MFA Order No. 74, 2004) sets those conditions. They include stable direct investment for three consecutive years with a good tax record. They also include four consecutive years as a deputy general manager or associate professor (or above), with at least three years' residence. The Measures further require 'no criminal record'. Under Articles 8 and 16 of the Nationality Law, an approved person must give up any foreign nationality, and the Ministry of Public Security decides the application. The law sets no minimum residence period, no language or knowledge test and no deadline for a decision, so approval is entirely discretionary.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Never; Rarely

National law in China provides a pathway to citizenship for humanitarian migrants only through the general naturalisation clause in Article 7 of the Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国籍法, 1980). Article 7 opens naturalisation to 'foreigners or stateless persons' who are willing to abide by the Constitution and laws. They must also be close relatives of Chinese nationals, be 'settled in China', or have 'other legitimate reasons'. Refugees and stateless persons are therefore not excluded. They could qualify as settled persons or under the undefined 'other legitimate reasons' limb. The law gives refugees no special or simplified route, however. The only provision on refugee status is Article 46 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012). It provides that 'foreigners recognised as refugees may stay or reside in China with refugee identity certificates issued by public security organs'. No implementing law or procedure for determining refugee status exists, and UNHCR still decides claims in Beijing. Refugee status is also not among the grounds for permanent residence listed in Article 6 of the 2004 Measures on Foreigners' Permanent Residence (公安部、外交部令第74号). Approval of any naturalisation application is entirely at the discretion of the Ministry of Public Security (Nationality Law, art. 16), and applicants must give up their former nationality (art. 8).

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in China provides a pathway to citizenship for family migrants in Article 7(1) of the Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国籍法, 1980). It allows foreigners or stateless persons 'willing to abide by China's Constitution and laws' who are 'close relatives of Chinese nationals' (中国人的近亲属) to be naturalised on application and approval. The Act sets no minimum period of residence or marriage for this ground. Family members can also qualify under the 'settled in China' limb. Article 6(1)(5) of the Measures for the Administration of Examination and Approval of Foreigners' Permanent Residence in China (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, MPS/MFA Order No. 74, 2004) gives permanent residence to the spouse of a Chinese citizen or of a foreign permanent resident. The marriage must have lasted five years, and the spouse must have lived in China continuously for five years, at least nine months each year, with stable means of support and housing. Minor children joining their parents (art. 6(1)(6)) and dependants aged 60 or over (art. 6(1)(7)) may also obtain permanent residence. The approved applicant must give up any foreign nationality (Nationality Law, art. 8). The Ministry of Public Security decides the application (art. 16), and the law sets no criteria for its discretion and no deadline. Parents or guardians may apply on behalf of children under 18 (art. 14).

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: No, no migrants may obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国籍法, adopted 10 September 1980, promulgated by Order No. 8 of the Chairman of the NPC Standing Committee) rules out dual nationality on principle. Article 3 provides that "The People's Republic of China does not recognise dual nationality for any Chinese national." Article 7 lets foreigners and stateless persons apply to naturalise if they are willing to abide by the Constitution and laws and meet one of three conditions: they are near relatives of Chinese nationals, they have settled in China, or they have other legitimate reasons. Article 8 then states that a person whose application is approved acquires Chinese nationality, but "a person whose application for naturalisation as a Chinese national has been approved shall not retain foreign nationality." Article 13 applies the same rule to former Chinese nationals whose nationality is restored. Article 9 adds that a Chinese national settled abroad who voluntarily acquires a foreign nationality automatically loses Chinese nationality. The law makes no exception by country of origin, for bilateral agreements, or for people who cannot renounce their original nationality. Every migrant who naturalises must therefore give up their nationality of origin. Approval rests with the Ministry of Public Security (art. 16).

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: No, there is no opportunity for irregular migrant workers to regularize
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012) contains no regularisation procedure for foreigners who are irregularly present or irregularly employed. Article 41 requires every foreigner working in China to obtain a work permit and a work-type residence permit, and bars any employer from hiring a foreigner without them. Article 43 defines working without these documents, or outside their scope, as "illegal employment" (非法就业). The law answers irregularity only with sanctions and removal. Article 78 punishes illegal stay with a warning, a fine of 500 yuan per day (up to 10,000 yuan) or 5 to 15 days' detention. Article 80 fines illegally employed foreigners 5,000 to 20,000 yuan, with possible detention. Article 62 provides that foreigners who stay or work illegally "may be repatriated", with a re-entry ban of one to five years. Article 36 makes refusals to grant residence permits or extend stay final. No provision lets an irregular worker apply in-country for a status on grounds of length of stay, family ties or employment. The State Council Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (Order 637, revised by Order 814 of 2025) and the 2004 Permanent Residence Measures contain no such route either. Undocumented foreign spouses in border provinces have no statutory path to status, and national political advisers have publicly called for one to be created.


## Opinion and expression

### q83 · Right to freedom to hold opinion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018) contains no guarantee of freedom of thought or of holding opinions as such. Its rights chapter is titled "Fundamental Rights and Obligations of Citizens", and Article 33 defines citizens as "all persons holding the nationality of the People's Republic of China". The nearest provision, Article 35, grants freedom of speech, the press, assembly, association, procession and demonstration only to "citizens of the People's Republic of China". Article 41's right to criticise state organs is likewise reserved to citizens. The only constitutional provision addressed to foreigners, Article 32, states that China "shall protect the lawful rights and interests of foreigners in the territory of China" and that foreigners "must abide by the law". It names no freedom of opinion and leaves the content of foreigners' rights to ordinary law. No statute fills the gap. The Provisions on the Administration of Religious Activities of Aliens (State Council Order No. 144) protect religious belief only, not opinions generally. Article 33's clause that "the state shall respect and protect human rights" is a general principle and has not been applied by courts to give foreigners a freedom of opinion. The position is the same for all categories of migrants: lawful residents, temporary workers, students, asylum seekers and irregular migrants.

### q85 · Right to freedom of expression.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Article 35 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018) provides that "citizens of the People's Republic of China shall enjoy freedom of speech, the press, assembly, association, procession and demonstration". It sits in Chapter II, "Fundamental Rights and Obligations of Citizens", and Article 33 confines citizenship to holders of Chinese nationality. Article 41's right to criticise and make suggestions to state organs is also granted only to citizens. Article 32 protects only foreigners' "lawful rights and interests" and requires them to "abide by the law of the People's Republic of China". It does not extend freedom of expression to them, and no statute does so. Foreigners' expressive activity is instead treated as a matter of control. Article 34 of the Law on Assemblies, Processions and Demonstrations (中华人民共和国集会游行示威法, 1989, amended 2009) bars foreigners from joining citizens' demonstrations, the main public form of expressing views, without approval. The Provisions on Religious Activities of Aliens (State Council Order No. 144, art 8) forbid religious preaching to citizens. The general clause that "the state shall respect and protect human rights" (art 33) has not been applied by courts to give foreigners a right of free expression. This applies to all migrants, including lawful residents and journalists, whose stay can be refused or ended without a reviewable decision.


## Peaceful assembly and association

### q87 · Right to freedom of association.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Article 35 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018) grants freedom of association only to "citizens of the People's Republic of China". The Regulations on the Registration and Administration of Social Organizations (社会团体登记管理条例, State Council Order No. 250 of 1998, revised 2016) state their aim as safeguarding "citizens' freedom of association" (art 1). They define a social organization as a non-profit body "voluntarily formed by Chinese citizens" (art 2), so foreigners cannot found or register a domestic association. Trade union rights are framed more broadly. Article 3 of the Trade Union Law (中华人民共和国工会法, 1992, amended 2021) gives "workers whose main source of livelihood is wages" in employers in China, "regardless of ethnicity, race, sex, occupation, religious belief or educational level", the right to join and organise unions. Article 7 of the Labour Law (中华人民共和国劳动法) gives "workers" the right to join and organise unions. Neither provision mentions nationality, so lawfully employed foreigners are not textually excluded. However, unions must belong to the single All-China Federation of Trade Unions "led by the Communist Party of China" (Trade Union Law art 2). Irregular workers have no lawful employment relationship. With the general freedom of association reserved to citizens and foreigners barred from forming associations, migrants do not enjoy this right commensurate with citizens.

### q89 · Right to freedom of assembly.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. Article 35 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国宪法, 1982, as amended 2018) grants freedom of assembly, procession and demonstration only to "citizens of the People's Republic of China". Article 32 protects foreigners' "lawful rights and interests" without naming any freedom. The Law on Assemblies, Processions and Demonstrations (中华人民共和国集会游行示威法, 1989, amended 2009) is enacted "to safeguard citizens' exercise of the rights of assembly, procession and demonstration" (art 1). It subjects all open-air gatherings to prior application and police permission (art 7). Its supplementary provisions treat foreigners differently from citizens. Article 34 states that "foreigners holding assemblies, processions or demonstrations within China are subject to this Law". It adds that "foreigners in China may not, without the approval of the competent authority, participate in assemblies, processions or demonstrations held by Chinese citizens". Foreigners thus face an additional, foreigner-specific approval requirement on top of the general permit regime, and the purpose clause frames the protected right as one of citizens. Participation in unauthorised gatherings can also lead to penalties, curtailment of stay or deportation under the Exit and Entry Administration Law. The same applies to lawful residents, temporary workers, students, asylum seekers and irregular migrants.


## Thought, conscience, and religion or belief

### q81 · Right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right for foreigners in the Provisions on the Administration of Religious Activities of Aliens within the Territory of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国境内外国人宗教活动管理规定, State Council Order No. 144 of 1994) and its Implementing Rules (中华人民共和国境内外国人宗教活动管理规定实施细则, National Religious Affairs Administration Order No. 23, in force 1 May 2025). Article 1 of the Provisions states that they are made "to ensure the freedom of religious belief of aliens within the territory". Article 2 provides that China "respects the freedom of religious belief of aliens within Chinese territory". Article 4 of the 2025 Rules adds that China "respects the freedom of religious belief of foreigners within its territory and protects their religious activities according to law". Foreigners may take part in religious activities at registered temples, mosques and churches. They may hold foreigner-only activities at recognised venues and invite Chinese clergy for baptisms, weddings and funerals (Provisions arts 3-5). The protection is a separate regime and narrower than the citizens' guarantee in Article 36 of the Constitution (中华人民共和国宪法), which is framed for "citizens". Article 8 of the Provisions forbids foreigners to set up religious organisations, offices, venues or schools, to recruit believers among Chinese citizens, to appoint clergy or to proselytise. Under the 2025 Rules, collective worship must be held in registered venues providing a "special service" or at temporary sites approved at provincial level (art 6). It must be convened by at least three lawfully resident foreigners with "no words or deeds hostile to China" (art 7) and is led by Chinese clergy unless a foreign leader is filed with the authorities (art 10). Freedom of thought and conscience as such has no counterpart for foreigners. The Provisions cover all foreigners, but only lawful residents may convene collective worship.


## Victims of crime

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Law (中华人民共和国刑事诉讼法, as amended 2018), arts. 17, 46, 101, 108, 110, 112-114. Article 17 applies the Law to cases involving foreigners. The Law's victim rights are not limited by nationality. Article 110 provides that "a victim has the right to report or file a complaint with the public security organs, people's procuratorates or people's courts" about crimes against his or her person or property, and the authorities "shall accept" reports and complaints. Article 108 makes the victim a "party" (当事人) to the proceedings. If the authorities decline to open a case, they must notify the complainant, who may apply for reconsideration (art. 112). The victim may also ask the procuratorate to require the police to explain the refusal (art. 113). Victims may bring private prosecutions directly in court in private-prosecution cases (art. 114). They may appoint a litigation agent from the time a public-prosecution case is transferred for review (art. 46) and may file an incidental civil action for material loss (art. 101). Article 9 guarantees translation for participants who do not understand the local language. Under the Legal Aid Law (中华人民共和国法律援助法, 2021), art. 29, victims in economic difficulty may apply for legal aid. For foreigners, however, art. 69 makes legal aid depend on Chinese law, a treaty or reciprocity. Access to proceedings is therefore formally the same as for citizens, for regular and irregular migrants alike. The main practical limit is that an irregular victim who comes forward may also face detention or repatriation under the Exit and Entry Administration Law, and no rule protects a complainant from removal.

### q45 · Right of victims of crime to immigration status or relief from expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Exit and Entry Administration Law (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, 2012) and the Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (中华人民共和国外国人入境出境管理条例, State Council Order 637, revised by Order 814 of 2025) create no visa, residence permit or bar to removal for victims of crime or trafficking. Article 62 of the Law allows the repatriation of any foreigner who is staying or working illegally, with a re-entry ban of one to five years. Article 61 lists only illness, pregnancy or nursing, and age (under 16 or over 70) as grounds for using restriction of movement instead of detention, and victimisation is not among them. The residence permit classes in art. 15 of the Regulations are work, study, journalism, family reunion and "private affairs" (私人事务). The private-affairs class is open to persons who "need to reside in China for other private affairs", but it is a general discretionary category with no reference to crime victims. It also presupposes lawful status and is subject to the refusal grounds in art. 21. Article 46 of the Law, which allows refugee applicants to stay pending screening, concerns asylum, not victimisation, and no screening procedure exists. Neither the Criminal Procedure Law's victim provisions nor the Criminal Law's anti-trafficking provisions (arts. 240-241) are linked to immigration relief. Any decision to let a foreign victim remain, for example to assist an investigation, is therefore an administrative discretion with no legal basis specific to victims.


## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in China protects this right in the Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012), articles 61 and 78, and in the Measures for the Administration of Examination and Approval of Foreigners' Permanent Residence in China (外国人在中国永久居留审批管理办法, Ministry of Public Security and Ministry of Foreign Affairs Order No. 74, 2004), articles 6, 13, 15, 17 and 21. Article 61 says that a foreigner "under the age of sixteen" shall "not be subject to detention for investigation". The authorities may instead restrict where the child goes, for no more than 60 days. Article 78, para. 2 deals with a foreigner under sixteen who stays illegally because a guardian failed in their duty of guardianship. In that case the guardian, not the child, receives a warning and a fine of up to 1,000 yuan. Adults who overstay face fines or 5-15 days' detention. On derivative status, the 2004 Measures make PR available to the "unmarried children under 18" of investors, senior staff and people with major contributions (art. 6(4)). They also cover "unmarried children under 18 joining their parents" (art. 6(6)). Children do not have to meet the five-year residence and stable-income conditions that apply to spouses and elderly dependants. Parents can apply on their behalf (art. 17), and children receive a five-year PR card (art. 21). The protections are limited. There is no best-interests-of-the-child clause in immigration law. Nothing bars the removal of children or of their parents. Children under 16 can still have their movement restricted, and repatriation (art. 62) applies to them. There is also no refugee or asylum procedure that would protect asylum-seeking children.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in China protects this right in the Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012), article 61(2). The provision says a foreign woman who is "pregnant or breastfeeding her own infant under one year of age" is "not subject to detention for investigation". The authorities may instead restrict where she goes, for no more than 60 days (art. 61, para. 2). This takes such women out of the detention regime that applies to other migrants suspected of illegal entry, stay or work under arts. 59-60. Adults there can be held for up to 30 days, extendable to 60, and the clock only starts once nationality or identity is established. The protection is narrow. It covers only pregnancy and nursing during immigration investigation. Pregnancy and nursing do not stop repatriation under art. 62 or detention pending removal under art. 63. Immigration law makes no provision for residence or protection for foreign victims of trafficking, forced marriage or gender-based violence. The Criminal Law (arts. 240-241) punishes trafficking in women, but it gives victims no immigration status. North Korean women in particular are treated as illegal migrants. There is also no refugee procedure through which women could claim gender-related persecution.

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in China. The Exit and Entry Administration Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国出境入境管理法, Presidential Order No. 57, 2012) does not mention disability. Its closest provision is article 61(1). That article exempts a foreigner "suffering from a serious illness" from detention for investigation and allows only restriction of movement instead. It is framed around illness, not disability, and it does not cover people with disabilities as such. Where the Law addresses mental health, it restricts rather than protects. Article 21(2) lists anyone "suffering from a serious mental disorder" among those who shall not be issued a visa, and article 25(2) applies the same ground to refuse entry. Article 61(4) lets the authorities withhold detention in "other circumstances unsuitable for detention". This is a discretionary catch-all, not a protection for people with disabilities. The Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreigners (Order 637, revised by Order 814 in 2025) and the 2004 Permanent Residence Measures contain no disability-specific accommodation, status or procedural safeguard either. China's general disability legislation is not tied to migration status, and no court has applied it to foreigners in immigration proceedings.
