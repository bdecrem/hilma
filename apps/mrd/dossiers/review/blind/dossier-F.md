# Dossier F — Portugal


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right for migrants generally in Portugal. Being lawfully present does not by itself give a foreigner the vote. The Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), art. 15(2), excludes "political rights" from the general equality of foreigners with citizens. Art. 15(4) only permits the law to grant foreigners resident in Portugal, "under conditions of reciprocity", the right to vote and stand in local (autarquias locais) elections. Art. 15(5) allows EU citizens to vote in European Parliament elections. The Local Elections Act (Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2001, de 14 de agosto), art. 2(1), extends local voting only to the following groups: (b) EU citizens, where Portuguese citizens enjoy the same right in their state; (c) nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries "with legal residence for more than two years", on reciprocity; and (d) "other citizens with legal residence in Portugal for more than three years" whose countries grant reciprocal voting rights to Portuguese residents. Under art. 2(2), the list of qualifying countries is published in the Diário da República. In practice this means Brazil and Cape Verde, plus Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Iceland, Norway, New Zealand, Peru, Uruguay, Venezuela and the UK (pre-Brexit residents or under bilateral agreement). Nationals of most large migrant communities, such as South Asians, Chinese and Ukrainians, cannot vote at any level, however long their lawful residence. National elections are reserved to citizens. The one exception is Brazilians holding the special statute of equality of political rights, which is a nationality-specific treaty regime and not a right that flows from lawful presence. As with Spain, the franchise depends on nationality and reciprocity, not on lawful residence as such.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right for migrants generally in Portugal. Being lawfully present does not give foreigners the right to stand for office. The Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), art. 15(2), excludes political rights and non-technical public functions from foreigners' general equality with citizens. Art. 15(4) only authorises the law to confer "passive electoral capacity" in local elections on resident foreigners "under conditions of reciprocity". Art. 15(3) allows nationals of Portuguese-speaking states with permanent residence to receive further rights by law and on reciprocity, but never access to the offices of President of the Republic, President of the Assembly, Prime Minister or presiding judge of a supreme court. The Local Elections Act (Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2001, de 14 de agosto), art. 5(1), makes the following people eligible for local bodies: (b) EU citizen voters, on reciprocity; (c) voters from Portuguese-speaking countries "with residence in Portugal for more than four years", on reciprocity; and (d) "other citizen voters with legal residence in Portugal for more than five years" whose countries grant Portuguese residents the right to stand. Under art. 5(2), the qualifying countries are listed in the Diário da República. Fewer states offer reciprocal candidacy rights than voting rights, so the group able to stand is narrower than the group able to vote. Third-country nationals from non-reciprocal states cannot stand for any office, whatever their period of lawful residence. Candidacy for the Assembly of the Republic is open only to Portuguese citizens and to Brazilians with the statute of equality of political rights, a nationality-specific treaty regime. As with Spain, eligibility depends on nationality and reciprocity, not on lawful presence.


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), articles 15(1) and 20, and in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros), articles 39, 87-B and 150. Article 15(1) CRP provides that foreigners and stateless persons 'who are present or reside in Portugal enjoy the rights and are subject to the duties of Portuguese citizens'. The exceptions in art. 15(2) are political rights, non-technical public functions and rights reserved to citizens, and none of them concerns judicial protection. Article 20(1) assures 'everyone' access to law and the courts, which 'may not be denied for lack of economic means'. Article 20(4) guarantees a decision within a reasonable time through fair process. Because these guarantees attach to presence rather than legal status, they reach irregular migrants and asylum seekers before civil, criminal and administrative courts alike. The Foreigners Act also opens the administrative courts against migration decisions. Refusals of entry can be challenged under art. 39. Coercive removal orders can be challenged under art. 150(1)-(2), which keeps the urgent and suspensive remedies of administrative procedure available. Under art. 87-B(1), claims against decisions or omissions of the migration agency AIMA take the form of an ordinary administrative action, with interim relief available. There are limits. In its current wording, art. 87-B(2)-(3) admits the urgent 'intimation for protection of rights, freedoms and guarantees' against AIMA only where AIMA's conduct compromises personal rights 'in a demonstrably serious and direct way' that interim measures cannot protect. It also directs the judge to weigh AIMA's caseload and resources. This narrows, for migrants, a remedy citizens can use without those conditions. In addition, several migration challenges (refusal of entry, removal) have only devolutive (non-suspensive) effect.

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 40(2), 112(4) and 150(3), in the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), arts. 25(4) and 35-B(2), and in the Legal Aid Act (Lei n.º 34/2004, de 29 de julho, regime de acesso ao direito e aos tribunais), art. 7. Article 20(1)-(2) of the Constitution (CRP) gives everyone the right to legal consultation and representation, and art. 15(1) extends that right to foreigners. Under the general legal aid regime, art. 7(1) of Law 34/2004 covers foreigners 'with a valid residence permit in an EU Member State' who show economic need. Art. 7(2) grants it to other foreigners only to the extent their own state grants it to Portuguese citizens (reciprocity). The migration laws override this for status proceedings. A foreigner refused entry is guaranteed timely access to a lawyer 'at his own expense or, on request, legal protection' under the urgent-defender regime (art. 40(2) Foreigners Act). A foreigner challenging a coercive removal order 'enjoys, on request, legal protection' under the same regime (art. 150(3)). Asylum applicants in the border procedure enjoy 'the benefit of legal protection', with fast appointment of counsel (art. 25(4) Asylum Act). Detained applicants must be told they may obtain legal aid (art. 35-B(2)). Presumed trafficking victims receive legal protection with the reciprocity rule of art. 7(2) expressly disapplied (art. 112(4) Foreigners Act). Outside these routes, for example in permit refusals by AIMA, irregular non-EU residents depend on the reciprocity clause. Legal aid is generally means-tested by the Social Security Institute (ISS). In asylum appeals the appointed lawyer applies a merits test.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Code of Criminal Procedure (Código de Processo Penal, Decreto-Lei n.º 78/87, de 17 de fevereiro), arts. 92 and 120(2)(c). Under art. 92(1), procedural acts are conducted in Portuguese. Article 92(2) provides that 'when a person who does not know or does not master the Portuguese language has to take part in the proceedings, a suitable interpreter is appointed, at no cost to that person', even if the presiding authority or other participants know the language used. Article 92(3)-(6) adds a right of the accused to written translation, within a reasonable time, of essential documents and of others essential for the defence. Only exceptionally may an oral translation or summary replace it, and the accused may make a reasoned request for further translations. Failure to appoint an interpreter where the law requires one is a nullity that can be invoked under art. 120(2)(c), so evidence obtained without interpretation can be excluded. The guarantee is framed around language, not nationality or residence status. It therefore covers all migrants, including irregular migrants and asylum seekers, whether they are accused, victims or witnesses. It is reinforced by the constitutional guarantees of defence in criminal proceedings and by the equality of foreigners present in Portugal (CRP art. 15(1)). Code art. 92 also reflects Portugal's obligations under EU Directive 2010/64/EU on interpretation and translation in criminal proceedings.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 181, 192 and 134, read with art. 187. Article 181(1), in the chapter on penal provisions, defines what counts as illegal entry: entry in breach of arts. 6, 9, 10 and 32(1)-(2) or the Schengen Borders Code. It attaches no penalty, and no provision of the Act makes unauthorised entry or stay as such a crime. Irregular presence is handled as an administrative offence (contraordenação) and through removal. Under art. 192, staying beyond the authorised period is punished only by fines of €80 to €700, scaled to the length of the overstay, and art. 134 makes illegal entry and stay grounds for coercive removal. Art. 134(2) adds that removal does not prejudice any criminal liability the foreigner has otherwise incurred, for example through forged documents under the Penal Code, and art. 134(3) applies the more favourable regime of law or treaty to refugees. The exception is art. 187: a foreigner who enters 'during the period for which that entry was prohibited' is punished with up to 2 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 100 days, and may be expelled as an accessory penalty. So first-time unauthorised entry is exempt from criminal sanction and handled administratively, but re-entry in breach of an entry ban is criminalised. The criminal offence of assisting illegal immigration (art. 183) targets smugglers, not the migrants smuggled.


## Education

### q127 · Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right, for certain statuses, in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 83(1)(a) and (d) and 56-D(2). Art. 83(1) gives every residence-permit holder, "without need of special authorisation relating to his status as a foreigner", the right "a) to education, teaching and vocational training, including grants and scholarships" and "d) to vocational guidance, training, improvement and retraining". Art. 83(2) guarantees provisions on equal treatment of foreigners. Seasonal workers with a short-stay or temporary-stay visa are guaranteed equal treatment with national workers under art. 56-D(2), including in "education and vocational training". The Constitution (Constituição da República Portuguesa) adds general rules: art. 74(1) and (2)(c)-(d) on everyone's right to education, permanent education and access to the highest levels of education, read with art. 15(1). Access to public higher education is not equal for all regular migrants, however. The Statute of the International Student (Decreto-Lei n.º 36/2014, de 10 de março, as amended by DL 62/2018 and DL 20/2025) treats every non-national as an "international student" under art. 3, with separate quotas and higher fees. Exempt are EU nationals and non-EU nationals who have resided legally in Portugal "for more than two years, uninterruptedly", together with their children; since 2018 the cut-off date is 1 January of the year of entry. Time spent on a study residence permit was originally excluded (art. 3(4)), and the 2025 amendment counts it only in some cases. No provision guarantees irregular adult migrants access to public higher education or vocational training. Equal access therefore depends on holding a residence permit and, for degree courses, on two years of continuous lawful residence.


## Equal protection

### q131 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP, 1976), articles 13(2) and 15(1); Law 93/2017 on the prevention, prohibition and combating of discrimination (Lei n.º 93/2017, de 23 de agosto), articles 1-4; and the Labour Code (Código do Trabalho, Lei n.º 7/2009), article 24. Article 15(1) CRP grants foreigners and stateless persons present or residing in Portugal the rights of citizens. Article 13(2) bars anyone from being prejudiced or deprived of any right by reason of, among other grounds, "territory of origin". Law 93/2017 prohibits "any form of discrimination" based on "racial and ethnic origin, colour, nationality, ancestry and territory of origin". It applies to all natural and legal persons, public and private, in social protection, health, education, housing, goods and services and culture (arts. 1, 2 and 4). Article 4(2)(i) covers any practice by a state body that "conditions or limits the exercise of any right". The law shifts the burden of proof (art. 14) and provides for compensation (art. 15). Article 24 of the Labour Code guarantees equal access to employment and working conditions irrespective of "nationality" and "territory of origin". These grounds capture discrimination against migrants as foreigners. However, "migrant" or residence status is not a named ground. Distinctions between regular and irregular migrants remain lawful: Labour Code article 4 grants equal treatment only to foreign workers "authorised" to work, and article 15(2) CRP permits citizen-only political rights.


## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: only major convictions (e.g., criminal offenses) can lead to loss of residence status
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros), arts. 81, 85, 135, 136 and 151, read with art. 30(4) of the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP). Only criminal convictions above set thresholds affect residence status. A resident can be given the accessory penalty of expulsion only if 'convicted of an intentional crime with a sentence of more than one year of imprisonment'. The court must weigh 'the seriousness of the facts…, his personality, any reoffending, the degree of social integration… and the length of residence in Portugal' (art. 151(2)). Permanent residents may be expelled only where their conduct is a 'serious danger or threat to public order, security or national defence' (art. 151(3)). For non-residents the threshold is an intentional crime punished by more than six months of effective imprisonment (art. 151(1)). An application for a permit or its renewal is refused where the applicant was 'convicted in Portugal by final judgment to a prison sentence of more than 1 year', or more than once to such a sentence even if suspended (art. 81(3)(b)). The permit is cancelled after a removal or judicial expulsion decision (art. 85(1)(a)). Long-term residents may be expelled only if they are a 'real and sufficiently serious threat to public order or public security' (art. 136(1)). Art. 135(1) bars expelling people born in Portugal, people who arrived as children, and parents of resident minors. Under art. 135(2) these bars give way after a sentence of 5 years or more for serious offences, or in cases of terrorism or state-security crimes. Administrative offences (contraordenações) carry fines and do not by themselves end lawful residence. CRP art. 30(4) forbids any penalty from automatically entailing the loss of rights. On that basis the Constitutional Court struck down automatic expulsion of parents of Portuguese children (Acórdão n.º 232/2004). One caveat: permits can also be cancelled on vaguer grounds without any conviction, namely 'public order or security' (art. 85(1)(c)-(d)) and threats to public order (art. 134(1)(b)).

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), arts. 7 and 47(2), and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 40(4) and 143(1). These rest on the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), art. 25(2), which provides that 'no one may be subjected to torture or to cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment or punishment'. Asylum Act art. 47(2) is absolute: 'No one shall be returned, removed, extradited or expelled to a country where they would be subjected to torture or to cruel or degrading treatment.' Foreigners Act art. 143(1) bars removal and expulsion 'to any country where the foreign citizen may suffer torture, inhuman or degrading treatment within the meaning of Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights'. Art. 40(4) extends this guarantee to people refused entry at the border. People facing such a risk qualify for subsidiary protection, because 'torture or inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment' counts as serious harm (Asylum Act art. 7(1)-(2)(b)). The safe-third-country concept requires an individual assessment in which the applicant may argue they would face torture or ill-treatment (art. 2(1)(r)(iii)). The ban covers all foreigners, including irregular migrants and rejected applicants. One procedural limit: to benefit from art. 143, the person must raise the fear and produce evidence within a set deadline (art. 143(2)). If removal to the country of origin is barred, the person is sent to another country that accepts them (art. 143(3)).

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), art. 33(8)-(9), the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), arts. 2(1)(aa), 3 and 47(1), and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), art. 143(1). The Asylum Act defines non-refoulement as the principle 'enshrined in Article 33 of the Geneva Convention, under which asylum seekers must be protected against direct or indirect expulsion or return to a place where their life or freedom would be threatened on account of their race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion' (art. 2(1)(aa)). When loss of international protection leads to removal proceedings, those proceedings must obey this principle (art. 47(1)). For all foreigners, the Foreigners Act forbids removal or expulsion 'to any country where the foreign citizen may be persecuted for the reasons which, under the law, justify the grant of asylum' (art. 143(1)). Art. 40(4) applies the same guarantee to people refused entry at the border. Refugee status covers people with a well-founded fear of persecution on the Convention grounds (art. 2(1)(ac)), and CRP art. 33(8) adds a constitutional right of asylum. Following Geneva Convention art. 33(2), the art. 2(1)(aa) guarantee does not apply to anyone who is a danger to national security or has a final conviction for a particularly serious crime. Even then the absolute ban on return to torture in art. 47(2) remains. Art. 143(2) requires the person to invoke the fear of persecution and prove it within a set deadline.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), arts. 24 and 33(4) and (6), the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), art. 7, and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), art. 143. Art. 24 CRP declares that 'human life is inviolable' and that 'in no case shall there be the death penalty'. Under art. 33(6) CRP, 'extradition, or surrender on any basis, is not permitted for political reasons or for crimes punishable, under the law of the requesting State, by death or any other penalty resulting in irreversible injury to physical integrity'. Under art. 33(4), extradition for crimes carrying life or indefinite sentences is allowed only if the requesting State gives guarantees that such a sentence will not be applied or carried out. Subsidiary protection, with a residence permit, goes to anyone who cannot return because of 'a risk of suffering serious harm', which 'includes, in particular: a) the death penalty or execution; b) torture or inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment...; or c) a serious and individual threat to the life or physical integrity of a civilian resulting from indiscriminate violence in situations of international or internal armed conflict' (Asylum Act art. 7(1)-(2)). The word 'nomeadamente' (in particular) leaves the list open. Art. 143(1) of the Foreigners Act and art. 47(2) of the Asylum Act forbid removal to persecution, torture or inhuman treatment. The protection reaches all foreigners. Its limits: the art. 9 exclusion clauses apply to subsidiary protection, and wider risks to social and economic rights are covered only through humanitarian or exceptional residence permits.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right through the directly applicable EU Pact regulations, as executed by the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho – Lei de Estrangeiros), as amended by Law 62/2026 (Lei n.º 62/2026). Under Article 30(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1348 (Asylum Procedure Regulation), where there are indications that third-country nationals held in detention facilities or present at border crossing points, including transit zones, 'may wish to make an application for international protection', the competent authorities 'shall provide them with information on the possibility to do so'. Article 11(1)(b) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1356 (Screening Regulation) requires that persons subject to screening be informed of 'the right to apply for international protection and the applicable rules on making an application'. EU regulations apply directly in Portugal under Article 8(4) of the Constitution. Law 62/2026 implements them. New Article 40.º-I(2) of Law 23/2007 obliges screening authorities to give 'adequate information … on the rights and duties' of the person. Article 40(4) guarantees people refused entry the non-refoulement regime of Article 143 'without prejudice to the protection conferred by the asylum law'. Article 38 requires a hearing before refusal of entry and written notice of the grounds 'in a language he can presumably understand'. Scope and limits: the express duty covers the border, transit zones, screening and detention facilities, which is where refusal of entry and most removals of irregular migrants start. The domestic coercive-removal procedure (Articles 145-149) contains no separate duty to advise on asylum. Article 146(6) only requires AIMA to inform people of their rights once they have applied for protection.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), Article 2(1)(aa), (r) and (z) and Article 47, and in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), Articles 40(4), 131(10) and 143. Article 2(1)(aa) defines non-refoulement as the principle under which asylum seekers 'must be protected against expulsion or return, direct or indirect', to a place where their life or freedom is threatened. The words 'direct or indirect' expressly cover onward (chain) refoulement. A country may be treated as a 'safe third country' (Article 2(1)(r)) or 'first country of asylum' (Article 2(1)(z)) only if 'the principle of non-refoulement and the right not to be subjected to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment are respected' there. The applicant must be able to obtain effective protection there and must be individually allowed to contest the designation. If that country refuses readmission, the applicant must have access to the Portuguese procedure. Article 47(2) provides that 'no one shall be returned, removed, extradited or expelled to a country where he would be subjected to torture or cruel or degrading treatment'. For all foreigners facing coercive removal or judicial expulsion, Article 143(1) of Law 23/2007 bars removal to any country where the person may be persecuted or face treatment contrary to Article 3 ECHR. Article 143(3) then routes the person to another country that accepts them. Article 131(10) requires removal of a long-term resident to a third country to observe 'the principle of non-refoulement'. Article 40(4) extends Article 143 to people refused entry. Under Article 143(2) the person must invoke the fear and offer proof. The courts have reportedly applied the principle to cases of indirect exposure to refoulement.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa), Article 33(2), and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), Articles 134, 135, 145, 149 and 152-157. Article 33(2) provides that expelling anyone who entered or remains lawfully, holds a residence permit, or has an asylum application that has not been refused 'may only be ordered by a judicial authority'. Article 134 lists the grounds for coercive removal or judicial expulsion exhaustively, each tied to the person's own conduct or situation. Examples are illegal entry or stay, a threat to national security or public order, and serious reasons to believe the person has committed serious crimes. Article 135 bars removal of protected categories, such as people born and resident in Portugal, parents caring for Portuguese or resident minor children, and unaccompanied minors. Under Article 145, the police (PSP) may order administrative coercive removal 'only on the ground of illegal entry or stay'. Any other expulsion requires a court decision (Articles 152-157). Article 149(2)-(3) requires a written decision, notified with 'its grounds', the person's legal obligations, the entry ban, and the country to which the person must not be sent under Article 143. Under Article 146(1), an irregular migrant who is detained must be brought before a judge within 48 hours for validation. The law therefore requires individual, reasoned decisions taken under and authorised by law, for regular and irregular migrants alike. Refusal of entry at the border follows a separate individual procedure (Articles 32-40), with a hearing and reasoned notice.

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), Articles 38-40, 148, 150, 152-158 and 165. In coercive-removal proceedings, Article 148(1) provides that 'the hearing of the person against whom [the proceedings] were brought is ensured, and he enjoys all guarantees of defence'. Under Article 148(2), that hearing counts as the formal hearing of the interested party under administrative procedure. Article 148(3)-(4) requires the investigator to take the steps needed to establish the truth. The investigator may refuse evidence the person requests only by reasoned order, and must write a report before the competent authority decides. Article 150 allows the PSP removal decision to be challenged before the administrative courts, without prejudice to urgent or suspensive proceedings under administrative procedural law. On request, the person receives legal aid (Article 150(3)) and translation and interpretation (Article 150(4)). Judicial expulsion is decided by a criminal court under summary-trial rules, with appeal to the Court of Appeal (Articles 152-158). Refusal of entry is decided only after a hearing of the person (Article 38(1)) and may be challenged in the administrative courts (Article 39). People refused entry are guaranteed timely access to a lawyer or legal aid (Article 40(2)). Readmission proceedings also require a prior hearing (Article 165(2)). Limits: challenges under Articles 39 and 150 have merely devolutive effect. Removal is therefore suspended only if the person obtains urgent or interim relief, or applies for international protection (Article 146(8)).


## Family

### q73 · Right of settled migrants to family reunification.
- Law: Conditional
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Portugal conditionally protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros), arts. 98-108, as amended by Law 61/2025 (Lei n.º 61/2025, de 22 de outubro). Under art. 98(1), the holder of a residence permit "valid for at least two years has the right to family reunification with the family members covered by articles 99 and 100", provided they have lived with the holder or depend on the holder. This applies whether the family ties arose before or after the holder entered Portugal. The waiting period is 15 months for a spouse or partner who cohabited with the holder for 18 months before entry (art. 98(2)). The waiting period does not apply to dependent minors or incapacitated persons, or to a spouse or partner who is the co-parent of such a child. It also does not apply to the families of highly qualified, researcher or similar permit holders (arts. 90, 90-A, 121-A) (art. 98(3)). The Minister may waive or shorten it in exceptional cases (art. 98(4)). Refugees may reunify without the waiting period (art. 98(5)). Article 99 lists the eligible family members: the spouse; minor or incapacitated children; adopted minors; dependent adult unmarried children who are studying; dependent first-degree ascendants; and minor siblings under the resident's guardianship. Article 100 adds de facto partners and their children. The right is also subject to several conditions. Art. 101(1) requires adequate housing and means of subsistence "without recourse to social support". Art. 101(3)-(4) adds integration measures (Portuguese language and constitutional values courses) that condition renewal. Refusal is possible on public-order, security or health grounds (art. 106). The right is reserved to lawful residence-permit holders; irregular migrants and short-stay visa holders are excluded. Law 61/2025 applies only to procedures started after 23 October 2025. Constitutional Court Ruling 785/2025 struck down an earlier, stricter version of these rules.

### q75 · Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), art. 106(6)-(7), read with the Code of Administrative Court Procedure (Código de Processo nos Tribunais Administrativos, Lei n.º 15/2002) and CRP art. 268(4) (effective judicial protection against administrative acts). Article 106(6) provides that a refusal decision "is notified to the applicant with indication of its grounds, and must state the right to judicial challenge and the respective time limit". Article 106(7) provides that "the decision refusing the application for family reunification may be challenged in court, with devolutive effect, before the administrative courts". Before refusing, the authority must weigh the nature and strength of the family ties, the length of residence and ties with the country of origin (art. 106(3)). A refugee's application cannot be refused solely for lack of documents proving the family relationship (art. 106(4)). The challenge has devolutive effect, so it does not suspend the refusal; interim relief is available under the general CPTA rules. Applicants also use CPTA actions to compel AIMA to decide when it fails to act within the legal deadline. The remedy is open to every applicant for reunification under arts. 98-103. In Ruling 785/2025 the Constitutional Court struck down a proposed art. 87-B, which would have narrowed urgent judicial remedies (intimação) against AIMA. The general administrative-court route therefore remains intact.

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Civil Code (Código Civil), art. 1577 as amended by Law 9/2010 (Lei n.º 9/2010, de 31 de maio). It also protects it in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 99(1)(a), 99(6) and 100, and in the De Facto Unions Act (Lei n.º 7/2001, de 11 de maio, as amended by Lei n.º 23/2010), art. 1(2) and art. 3(3). Article 1577 of the Civil Code defines marriage as "the contract concluded between two persons who intend to found a family through a full communion of life", so it is gender-neutral. The Foreigners Act names "the spouse" as a family member for reunification without distinction of sex (art. 99(1)(a)). The right to reunify with a spouse or partner depends only on "the marriage or de facto union being valid and recognised under Portuguese law" and on both being at least 18 (art. 99(6)). Same-sex marriages concluded in Portugal or abroad, if recognisable under Portuguese law, therefore qualify. Article 100 also allows reunification with a de facto partner. Law 7/2001 defines a de facto union as two persons who, "regardless of sex", have lived in conditions analogous to spouses for more than two years. Under its art. 3(3), any provision granting rights based on a de facto union applies "regardless of the sex of its members". Same-sex spouses and partners face the same conditions as other families: waiting period, housing, means and integration measures (arts. 98, 101).

### q79 · Right to family reunification without resource conditions
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Portugal. The Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), art. 101(1), makes reunification conditional on resources. The applicant must have "a) accommodation, demonstrably owned or rented, considered normal for a comparable family in the same region ... and meeting general safety and health standards" and "b) sufficient means of subsistence to support all members of the family group, without recourse to social support". Ministerial ordinances set the thresholds. Under art. 106(1)(a), an application may be refused when these conditions are not met. Under art. 101(4), renewal of the family member's permit depends on proof that the conditions continue to be met, "including as regards the non-counting of social support". The only general exemption is for refugees: art. 101(2) states that the resource conditions "do not apply to the family reunification of refugees". Art. 98(5) gives recognised refugees reunification with family members inside or outside Portugal. Some highly qualified permit holders (arts. 90, 90-A, 121-A) are exempt from the waiting period under art. 98(3)(c), but not from the resource conditions. Because the law requires housing and income from all migrants except refugees, it does not give migrants a right to family reunification regardless of resources.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Nationality Act (Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro – Lei da Nacionalidade, as amended up to Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026), Articles 1(1)(g), 1(2) and 6(3). Under Article 1(1)(g), "individuals born in Portuguese territory who do not possess another nationality" are Portuguese by origin. The rule applies at birth and does not depend on the parents' residence status, length of stay or nationality, so it covers children of irregular migrants. Under Article 1(2), newborns found abandoned in Portugal are presumed, unless proven otherwise, to have been born there; this protects foundlings. Adult stateless migrants have a facilitated naturalisation route under Article 6(3): "The Government grants Portuguese nationality to stateless persons who have resided legally in Portugal for at least four years and who meet the requirements of points c) to h)" of Article 6(1). The wording 'grants' (concede) makes naturalisation an entitlement once the conditions are met. The conditions are language and civic knowledge, adherence to democratic principles, no serious criminal record and no security concerns; stateless applicants are exempt from the self-subsistence requirement. The protection for adults depends on lawful residence and is not automatic. Statelessness status itself has been recognised in the Asylum and Foreigners Acts since Law n.º 41/2023, but the law still sets no determination procedure.


## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, for all visa types (including irregular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Health Framework Law (Lei n.º 95/2019, de 4 de setembro – Lei de Bases da Saúde), Bases 20 and 21, and in the Health Minister's Order on foreigners' access to the National Health Service (Despacho n.º 25360/2001, 2.ª série). Base 20(2)(a) makes the National Health Service (SNS) universal, 'guaranteeing the provision of health care to all persons without discrimination, in conditions of dignity and equality'. Base 21(2) names as SNS beneficiaries, alongside Portuguese citizens, nationals of third countries, stateless persons, 'applicants for international protection and migrants with or without their situation regularised', who are permanently resident or staying or residing temporarily in Portugal, 'under the applicable legal regime'. That regime is set by the Despacho. Under point 1, foreigners lawfully resident in Portugal get access to SNS care and medicines 'with equal treatment to that of SNS beneficiaries'. Under point 2, they register by showing a residence or stay permit or a work visa. Under point 4, foreigners without these documents may still use SNS services if their parish council (junta de freguesia) certifies that they have been in Portugal for more than 90 days. Under point 5, these migrants may be charged for care according to their economic and social situation, except for care where public health is at risk. A 2009 circular from the Directorate-General of Health, as described by the health regulator ERS, adds further free care: urgent and vital care, maternal and child health, vaccination and communicable diseases. So irregular migrants are SNS beneficiaries by statute, but their access can be charged and depends on 90 days' certified presence. Lawful residents, refugees and asylum seekers get the same treatment as citizens. Lei n.º 23/2007, art. 83(1)(e) also guarantees residence-permit holders 'access to health'.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right for regular migrants in the Supported Rental Housing Act (Lei n.º 81/2014, de 19 de dezembro – regime do arrendamento apoiado para habitação), article 5. Article 5(1) provides that 'national citizens and foreign citizens holding valid titles of stay in national territory' may be allocated supported-rent (social) housing, if they meet the law's other conditions and are not caught by the impediments in article 6. Under this test, any lawfully present foreigner with a valid residence or stay title competes on the same terms as nationals. The statutory preference criteria (single-parent families, families with minors, persons with disabilities or aged 65 or over, domestic-violence victims) do not include nationality. The Housing Framework Law (Lei n.º 83/2019, de 3 de setembro – Lei de Bases da Habitação), article 2(1), adds a general guarantee that 'everyone has the right to housing, for themselves and their family, regardless of ... territory of origin, nationality' and other grounds. That is a framework principle, though; the access rule for public housing is the residence-title condition in Lei 81/2014. Irregular migrants hold no valid title, so they cannot be allocated supported-rent housing. Asylum seekers are housed instead through reception arrangements under the asylum law. The Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007), art. 83(2), also guarantees residence-permit holders equal treatment in 'access to goods and services available to the public'.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes (including irregular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), arts. 15(1) and 74, and in Decree-Law No. 67/2004 creating a national register of foreign minors in an irregular situation (Decreto-Lei n.º 67/2004, de 25 de março), arts. 2 and 3. Under CRP art. 15(1), foreigners and stateless persons "who are present or reside in Portugal" have the same rights as Portuguese citizens. Because the rule turns on presence and not only on residence, it reaches children without status. Art. 74(1) gives "everyone" the right to education with equal opportunity of access and success. Art. 74(2)(a) requires the State to "ensure universal, compulsory and free basic education". Decree-Law 67/2004 deals specifically with irregular children. Art. 2(1) creates a register "exclusively to ensure the minors' access to health care and to pre-school and school education". Art. 3(3) charges AIMA, together with the other public services, with ensuring that registered minors "access the same rights that the law grants to minors in a regular situation". Art. 2(3)-(4) adds a firewall: register data can never ground or prove any administrative or judicial proceeding against the parents, and cannot serve as a basis for regularisation. Children with residence permits are also covered by the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007), art. 83(1)(a): the right to "education, teaching and vocational training". Free primary education therefore extends to all migrant children, whatever their status, on the same terms as nationals. The Ministry of Education confirms that they follow the same placement rules as Portuguese pupils.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), article 59(1)(a), the Labour Code (Código do Trabalho, Lei n.º 7/2009), articles 4, 24, 25 and 270, and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007), articles 56-D(2) and 198-A(10). CRP art. 59(1) gives "all workers, without distinction of age, sex, race, citizenship, territory of origin, religion, political or ideological convictions" the right to pay according to the quantity, nature and quality of their work, "observing the principle that for equal work, equal pay", so as to guarantee a decent existence. CRP art. 15(1) extends citizens' rights to foreigners who are present or resident in Portugal. Labour Code art. 270 requires pay to be set on the principle "for equal work or work of equal value, equal pay". Art. 4 gives a foreign or stateless worker authorised to do subordinate work in Portugal "the same rights" as a Portuguese worker. Arts. 24-25 bar employer discrimination on nationality, ethnic origin or race, including in working conditions, and a breach is a very serious administrative offence. Seasonal workers are guaranteed equal treatment with nationals in labour rights, including payment of wage arrears (Lei 23/2007, art. 56-D(2)). For workers employed without authorisation, art. 198-A(10) makes the official assessment of wage credits for work actually performed directly enforceable against the employer. Art. 198-B lets unions and migrant associations file complaints over unpaid wages. The equal-pay rule therefore covers migrant workers generally. The Labour Code's express equal-treatment clause (art. 4) is limited to authorised workers, but the constitutional rule and arts. 198-A/198-B also protect irregular workers' pay for work already done.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Migrant workers have completely free choice of employment
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), as amended up to Lei n.º 62/2026, articles 83(1), 88(8) and 56-D(1). Under art. 83(1), any holder of a residence permit is entitled "without need of special authorisation relating to his/her status as a foreigner" to subordinate employment (b) and to independent professional activity (c), as well as to vocational guidance and training. Work authorisation is therefore not a separate, employer-specific permit. A residence permit for subordinate work is first granted on the basis of an employment contract and social security registration (art. 88(1)). After that, art. 88(8) provides that the holder "may change employer by communication to AIMA, IP, without need for the issue of a new title". Under art. 88(5) the holder may also switch to self-employment by replacing the residence title. Seasonal workers on short-stay or temporary-stay visas may do the activity named in the visa "or others, with one or successive employers" throughout the national territory (art. 56-D(1)). The Constitution (CRP) art. 15(1) gives foreigners the rights of citizens. Art. 15(2) excludes non-technical public functions, so access to public posts that involve sovereign authority is reserved to citizens. Remaining limits are procedural: the employer change must be notified, and permit renewal depends on continued lawful means of subsistence or employment. Neither ties the worker to one employer or sector.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: Yes, unrestricted right to work
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), as amended up to Lei n.º 62/2026, articles 107 and 83(1). Under art. 107(1), a family member who holds a family-reunification visa, or whose reunification request has been approved while in Portugal, "is granted a residence permit of duration identical to that of the resident". Family members of permanent residents receive a two-year renewable permit (art. 107(2)). A spouse married or in a de facto union for more than five years receives an autonomous permit from the start (art. 107(5)). Once this residence permit is issued, art. 83(1) applies: every residence-permit holder is entitled "without need of special authorisation relating to his status as a foreigner" to subordinate employment (b), independent professional activity (c), and vocational training and education (a, d). No separate work permit is required, and no sector, occupation or labour-market test limits the reunified spouse or partner. Equal treatment in social security, union membership and recognition of qualifications is guaranteed by art. 83(2). The right depends on the reunification permit actually being issued. Since Lei n.º 61/2025, the sponsor must generally have two years' lawful residence before applying, with exceptions, which delays access to the territory and thus to work, but this does not limit the right once the permit is held.

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment implies loss of immigration status unless a new job is found within a specified time period
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal conditionally protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros), articles 78, 85 and 88, read with the implementing Regulatory Decree (Decreto Regulamentar n.º 84/2007), article 63. Loss of a job is not a ground for cancelling a residence permit: article 85(1) limits cancellation to removal or expulsion decisions, fraud, serious criminality, public order or security and EU restrictive measures, and article 85(2) adds prolonged absence. A worker who loses a job therefore keeps the permit until it expires (two years for a first temporary permit, three for each renewal under article 75). Under article 88(8), the holder may also change employer simply by notifying AIMA. Renewal is the point where employment matters. Article 78(2) requires the holder to show 'means of subsistence', accommodation and compliance with tax and social security obligations. Article 63(2)(c) of the Regulatory Decree adds that a renewal application must include 'information proving the maintenance of an employment relationship or the exercise of an independent professional activity'. A temporary migrant who loses a job must therefore find new employment, or show other means, before the permit expires. That remaining validity is the time window. Article 78(7) gives the renewal receipt the effect of a permit for 60 days, renewable. EU Blue Card holders who become unemployed may look for work and sign a new contract (article 121-G(3)). Permanent residents (article 76) and EU long-term residents (articles 125 ff.) are not affected by job loss.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Labour Code (Código do Trabalho, Lei n.º 7/2009), articles 4, 24 and 25, the Constitution (CRP), articles 15(1) and 59(1), and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007), articles 83(2) and 56-D(2). Labour Code art. 4 provides that "the foreign or stateless worker who is authorised to exercise a subordinate professional activity in Portuguese territory enjoys the same rights and is subject to the same duties as the worker of Portuguese nationality". The only carve-out is the law applicable to posted workers. All Labour Code protections therefore apply on equal terms, including working time and overtime, weekly rest, paid holidays, health and safety, and dismissal protection. Art. 24(1) guarantees every worker equal opportunity and treatment in working conditions regardless of nationality, ethnic origin or race, and art. 25 prohibits direct and indirect discrimination by employers. Art. 5 adds a protective formality: contracts with foreign workers must be in writing and state pay, hours and place of work. The constitutional rights in CRP art. 59(1)(b)-(f) apply to all workers "without distinction of... citizenship": decent organisation of work, health and safety, rest, a maximum working day, weekly rest and paid holidays, unemployment assistance, and compensation for workplace accidents. Under Lei 23/2007, art. 83(2) guarantees residence-permit holders equal treatment in social security and trade-union membership. Art. 56-D(2) extends equal treatment in labour rights under law and collective agreements to seasonal workers.

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Equal access to all types of social security protections after some period of time
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho – Lei de Estrangeiros), article 83(2), and in the Social Security Framework Law (Lei n.º 4/2007, de 16 de janeiro – Lei de Bases da Segurança Social), articles 2, 7, 37, 47 and 51. Article 83(2) of the Foreigners Act guarantees permit holders "the application of the provisions that ensure equal treatment of foreign citizens, namely in matters of social security". Articles 56-D(2), 97-A and 121-H extend the same art. 83(2) equal treatment to seasonal workers, researchers/students and EU Blue Card holders. Under Lei 4/2007, "everyone has the right to social security" (art. 2). The equality principle bars discrimination by nationality "without prejudice … to conditions of residence and reciprocity" (art. 7). Everyone who works for an employer or is self-employed is compulsorily covered by the contributory (previdencial) system (art. 51). As a result, migrant workers have immediate equal access to the contributory benefits: sickness, parenthood, unemployment, invalidity, old age and death. Non-contributory protection is more limited. The solidarity subsystem covers nationals and "may be extended, under conditions established by law, to non-nationals" (art. 37(1)). Family benefits may depend on "minimum periods of legal residence" (art. 47(2)). The social integration income requires "legal residence in Portugal" (Lei n.º 13/2003, art. 6(1)(a)). Decree-Law n.º 166/2026 (Decreto-Lei n.º 166/2026, de 13 de agosto) merges 13 non-contributory benefits into a single social benefit (PSU) from 31 December 2026. Under its art. 9(c), third-country nationals count as residents only if they have stayed "with a valid residence title for at least one year". Lawfully resident migrant workers therefore have immediate equal access to contributory protection and gain equal access to means-tested protection after about one year of lawful residence.

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: Yes, immediate equal access to all types of unemployment benefits
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in Decree-Law n.º 220/2006 (Decreto-Lei n.º 220/2006, de 3 de novembro – regime jurídico de proteção social na eventualidade de desemprego), article 8. It is read with the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), article 83(2), and the Social Security Framework Law (Lei n.º 4/2007), articles 7 and 51. Article 8(1) gives the right to unemployment benefit to insured persons whose employment contract has ended involuntarily, who meet the conditions for the benefit and who reside in Portugal. Article 8(2) adds that foreign citizens "must also hold a valid residence title or the respective receipt of the renewal application, or other titles that authorise the exercise of subordinate professional activity and respective extensions". Refugees and stateless persons must hold a valid protection title. No nationality-based contribution period or residence period applies. Foreign workers meet the same qualifying periods, benefit amounts and durations as Portuguese workers, and the rules cover both the contributory unemployment benefit and the social unemployment benefit. Article 83(2) of the Foreigners Act separately guarantees permit holders equal treatment "in matters of social security". Lei 4/2007 compulsorily covers all employees in the contributory system (art. 51) and bars nationality discrimination (art. 7). The protection depends on holding a valid residence or work-authorising title, or a renewal receipt. Undocumented workers are excluded even if they paid contributions. From 31 December 2026, Decree-Law n.º 166/2026 folds the means-tested social unemployment benefit into the new single social benefit (PSU). For third-country nationals that benefit requires one year of residence with a valid title (art. 9(c)). The contributory benefit stays equal.

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros), articles 74, 76, 80 and 126. Article 74 distinguishes temporary from permanent residence permits, and under article 76(1) a permanent permit 'has no limit of validity'. Under article 80(1), a foreigner who has 'held a temporary residence permit for at least five years' obtains a permanent residence permit if, cumulatively, they have no prison sentence over one year in the last five years, have means of subsistence, have accommodation and can show knowledge of basic Portuguese. Separately, article 126(1) grants EU long-term resident status to a third-country national with 'legal and uninterrupted residence in national territory during the five years immediately preceding the application'. That status also requires stable and regular resources without recourse to the solidarity subsystem, health insurance, accommodation and basic Portuguese. It 'has a permanent character' (article 129(9)), and if AIMA does not decide within nine months the application is deemed granted (article 129(6)). Temporary migrant workers holding residence permits for employed or self-employed work (articles 88-89), Blue Card holders and other temporary permit holders can therefore move to permanent status once they meet the residence and integration conditions. Time spent under seasonal-work visas does not count (see q115).

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right for all temporary migrants in Portugal. The Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros) opens permanent status only to holders of a residence permit, and it excludes certain temporary categories. Article 80(1)(a) requires the applicant to 'have held a temporary residence permit for at least five years'. Seasonal workers do not hold residence permits: they are admitted on a short-stay visa or temporary-stay visa for seasonal work (articles 56 ff.), and article 56-D gives them equal treatment in working conditions but no residence permit. Time spent under those visas therefore does not count toward permanent residence. For EU long-term status, article 125(2)(e) excludes those who 'remain in Portugal exclusively for reasons of a temporary nature, such as seasonal workers, workers posted by a service provider for the purpose of cross-border provision of services, or cross-border service providers'. Article 126(2) adds that residence periods under article 125(2)(e) and (f) 'are not taken into account' when calculating the five years. Other temporary workers with residence permits for employed or self-employed work, Blue Card holders, researchers and similar categories can access permanent status after five years. Access is therefore conditional on visa or permit type, and seasonal and posted workers are excluded.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, same as citizens
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Labour Code (Código do Trabalho, Lei n.º 7/2009, de 12 de fevereiro), articles 4 and 5, and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, Lei de Estrangeiros), articles 83, 198-A and 198-B, read with the Constitution's equality clause for foreigners (art. 15). Under article 4 of the Labour Code, a foreign or stateless worker 'authorised to exercise a subordinate professional activity in Portuguese territory enjoys the same rights and is subject to the same duties as the worker of Portuguese nationality'. Contractual claims are therefore enforceable through the Labour Conditions Authority (ACT) and the labour courts on the same terms as for nationals. Under article 5, contracts with foreign workers must be in writing and state pay, place and working hours. Article 83(1)(f) of the Foreigners Act guarantees residence-permit holders 'access to law and the courts', and seasonal workers have equal treatment 'including payment of outstanding remuneration' (article 56-D(2)). Irregularly employed workers are also protected. Under article 198-A(5), employers are liable for outstanding wage credits. Under article 198-A(8), a minimum-wage level and a three-month employment relationship are presumed unless proved otherwise. Under article 198-A(10), the liquidation note for unpaid wage credits is an enforceable title. Article 198-B allows trade unions and immigrant organisations to file complaints for unpaid wages on the worker's behalf. One gap is that the Legal Aid Act (Lei n.º 34/2004, art. 7) restricts free legal aid for foreigners without a valid EU residence title.

### q135 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of race or ethnicity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution (CRP), articles 13(2), 15(1) and 59(1), the Anti-Discrimination Act (Lei n.º 93/2017, de 23 de agosto), articles 1, 3 and 4, and the Labour Code (Código do Trabalho, Lei n.º 7/2009), articles 24 and 25. CRP art. 13(2) provides that no one may be "privileged, favoured, prejudiced, deprived of any right or exempted from any duty by reason of ancestry, sex, race, language, territory of origin". Art. 15(1) extends constitutional rights to foreigners present or resident in Portugal, and art. 59(1) protects all workers "without distinction of... race, citizenship, territory of origin". Lei 93/2017 establishes the legal regime for preventing, prohibiting and combating "any form of discrimination on grounds of racial and ethnic origin, colour, nationality, ancestry and territory of origin" (art. 1). It applies to all natural and legal persons, public and private, in social protection, health, education, housing and access to goods and services (art. 2). It defines direct, indirect and multiple discrimination, discrimination by association, and harassment (art. 3), and prohibits all such discrimination (art. 4). It also shifts the burden of proof (art. 14), provides for damages (art. 15) and imposes administrative fines (art. 16), enforced by the Commission for Equality and against Racial Discrimination (CICDR), made independent under the Parliament by Lei 3/2024. In employment, Labour Code art. 24 guarantees equal treatment regardless of "nationality, ethnic origin or race", and art. 25 prohibits employer discrimination, with damages under art. 28. The Penal Code separately criminalises incitement to discrimination and hatred. These protections cover migrants regardless of status.


## Legal personhood

### q27 · Right not to have identity documents confiscated.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Penal Code (Código Penal, Decreto-Lei n.º 400/82 as republished by Decreto-Lei n.º 48/95), articles 160(7) and 259, and in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), article 34. Article 160(7) of the Penal Code, as renumbered by Lei n.º 53/2026, makes it a crime to hold on to a trafficking victim's papers: "whoever retains, conceals, damages or destroys identity or travel documents of a victim of a crime provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2" faces up to three years' imprisonment. Paragraph 1 covers trafficking for labour exploitation. Article 259 reaches anyone, not only traffickers. It punishes, with up to three years' imprisonment or a fine, whoever, to cause harm or obtain an illegitimate benefit, "conceals or takes away a document … of which he may not dispose, or which another person may lawfully demand to be handed over". An employer or recruiter who takes a worker's passport to keep that worker in place therefore commits a crime. Where the victim is a private person, prosecution requires a complaint (art. 259(4)). The Foreigners Act gives the power to seize travel documents to public authorities only. Article 34 allows the border authority to seize a false, falsified or fraudulently obtained travel document when it refuses entry. Articles 85(5) and 131(7) provide that a residence title is seized when the permit is cancelled. No statute contains a stand-alone labour-law ban on employers keeping documents, as Spain's LO 4/2000 does. The protection rests on these criminal provisions, which apply to all migrants, regular or irregular. Read with art. 15 of the Constitution, which gives equal rights to foreigners present in Portugal, the effect is that only an authorised official may lawfully take a migrant's identity document.

### q29 · Right to birth registration.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Portugal requires birth registration in the Civil Registry Code (Código do Registo Civil, approved by Decreto-Lei n.º 131/95, de 6 de junho, as amended up to DL 126/2023 and Lei 39/2025), articles 1 and 96. Article 1(1)(a) provides that "civil registration is compulsory" and that births are among the facts it covers. Article 96(1) provides that the birth "must compulsorily be declared" by the parents or other legal representatives, or by the nearest capable relative. Article 96(2) provides that "a birth occurring in Portuguese territory" must be declared in one of three ways: electronically; in person at any civil registry office within 20 days of the birth; or at the health unit where the birth took place, before the mother is discharged (the 'Nascer Cidadão' scheme, art. 96-A). These obligations attach to the fact of a birth in Portugal. The Code does not make them depend on the parents' nationality or residence status. Read with art. 15 of the Constitution, which gives equal rights to foreigners present in Portugal, they therefore apply to children of irregular migrants, asylum seekers and other foreigners on the same terms as to Portuguese children. Late registration is also possible. Registration is separate from nationality: under the Nationality Act (Lei 37/81, as amended by Lei Orgânica 1/2026), a child born to foreign parents acquires Portuguese nationality only on conditions, but the birth must be registered in any case.

### q31 · Right to government document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Portugal mandates these documents in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, as amended up to Lei 62/2026), articles 74(2), 83 and 84, together with article 2(1)(x) and article 212. Article 74(2) provides: "A residence title is issued to the foreign citizen authorised to reside in Portuguese territory." Article 2(1)(x) defines the residence title as the document issued "in accordance with the rules and the uniform model in force in the European Union". Article 84 makes it an identity document: "The residence title replaces, for all legal purposes, the identity document". The Brazilian equal-status regime under the 2000 Porto Seguro Treaty is preserved. The title also shows the holder's right to work. Article 83(1) entitles holders to exercise employed and self-employed activity. Special titles show their category on their face, such as the 'EU Blue Card', which art. 121-B(3) requires to be marked with that designation in the 'type of permit' field. Article 212 provides the legal basis for biometric identification and the standard permit card, and art. 217(8) requires holders of older permits to exchange them for that card. Under art. 75(4), the title must be renewed whenever the identification details on it change. The duty covers lawfully resident migrants: temporary and permanent residents, long-term residents and Blue Card holders. Asylum seekers receive separate proof of their application under Lei 27/2008. Irregular migrants receive no status document until they are regularised.

### q33 · Right against destruction of document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal makes it illegal to destroy these documents in the Penal Code (Código Penal, Decreto-Lei n.º 400/82 as republished by Decreto-Lei n.º 48/95), articles 259 and 160(7). Article 259(1) punishes, with up to three years' imprisonment or a fine, "whoever, with intent to cause harm to another person or to the State, or to obtain an illegitimate benefit, destroys, damages, renders unusable, makes disappear, conceals or takes away a document … of which he may not dispose". Attempt is punishable (art. 259(2)). Where the offended party is a private person, prosecution requires a complaint (art. 259(4)). A residence title is a document of this kind: the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007) says it "replaces, for all legal purposes, the identity document" (art. 84). So does a foreigner's passport. Destroying either with harmful intent is therefore a crime, whoever commits it and whatever the migrant's status. Article 160(7) adds a specific offence for trafficking: "whoever retains, conceals, damages or destroys identity or travel documents" of a trafficking victim faces up to three years' imprisonment, unless a heavier penalty applies. The general crime of damage to property (art. 212) also covers the physical object. The protection covers all documents recognising identity, residence or work status, whether issued by AIMA or by the country of origin, for regular and irregular migrants alike.


## Liberty

### q15 · Right to communicate with consular officials.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho – regime jurídico de entrada, permanência, saída e afastamento de estrangeiros do território nacional), arts. 38(1), 40(1) and 146-A(1) and (5), and in the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho). Under art. 38(1), a decision refusing entry is made after hearing the foreigner and "is immediately communicated to the diplomatic or consular representation of his or her country of origin". Under art. 40(1), a foreigner refused entry who is held in the port or airport international zone, a temporary installation centre (CIT) or an equivalent space "may communicate with the diplomatic or consular representation of his or her country or with any person of his or her choice". For removal detention, art. 146-A(1) provides that a foreigner detained in a CIT or equivalent space "is authorised, on request, to contact his or her legal representatives, family members and the competent consular authorities". Art. 146-A(5) requires detainees to be given a document setting out the centre's rules and their rights, "namely the right to contact the entities referred to in paragraph 1". The protection therefore covers the main points where migrants lose their liberty: refusal of entry at the border (with automatic notice to the consulate) and administrative detention pending removal (contact on request). Asylum seekers held in CITs under Lei 27/2008, art. 35-B(3), may contact their legal representatives, family and organisations working in the field, and the Foreigners Act rules apply alongside. The duty is framed as allowing communication. Outside refusal of entry, the statute does not require the authorities to notify the consulate on their own initiative.

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Portugal. Under the general removal regime of the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), detention is not prohibited for children; the Act assumes they will be detained. Art. 146-A(6) provides that "detained families must be accommodated in separate places that guarantee due privacy". Art. 146-A(7) provides that "detained accompanied minors must have the possibility to take part in leisure activities ... and, depending on the length of stay, must have access to education". Art. 146-A(3) lists minors and unaccompanied minors among the vulnerable detainees who need special attention. Nothing in arts. 142 or 146 exempts children from placement in a temporary installation centre (CIT). The Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), art. 35-C, added by Lei n.º 62/2026, comes closest. Art. 35-C(3) says "save where the conditions in paragraph 6 are met, minors may not be detained". Art. 35-C(6) then allows detention "in exceptional circumstances, as a measure of last resort" where it is in the child's best interests: accompanied children may be detained if a parent or primary carer is detained, and unaccompanied children if detention is necessary to protect them. Art. 35-C(5) and (7) call for alternatives for families and for family-adapted detention centres. This qualified rule covers only children who apply for international protection. It does not reach children in removal or refusal-of-entry proceedings, and even for asylum seekers it permits detention.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Portugal. Under the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), irregular status is itself a ground for detention. Art. 146(1) provides that "the foreign citizen who enters or stays illegally in national territory is detained by a police authority". The person must be brought before a criminal court judge within 48 hours for validation and possible coercive measures. Art. 146(3) allows placement in a temporary installation centre (CIT) for as long as strictly necessary for the removal decision, "with the limit of 180 days, extendable for an equal period" where the foreigner does not cooperate or travel documents are delayed. Art. 142(1) lists the coercive measures a judge may impose "where there is a risk of absconding" in removal or expulsion proceedings. They include placement in a CIT or equivalent space, alongside non-custodial alternatives such as reporting, electronic monitoring, bail and surrender of documents. Foreigners refused entry may be kept in a CIT or equivalent airport space by court order if they are not re-embarked within 48 hours (art. 38(4)). The Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho), art. 35-A(1), bars detaining asylum seekers "solely for having applied". But art. 35-A(2) expressly allows detention "in the case of illegal entry or stay in national territory", after an individual assessment and where less coercive alternatives cannot be applied effectively. Detention is judicially supervised and time-limited, but national law does not prohibit detaining migrants because of their migration status.

### q21 · Right to appeal legality, conditions, and length of detention
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP), arts. 27(3)(c) and 31; the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 142, 146 and 146-A; Law 34/94 on temporary installation centres (Lei n.º 34/94, de 14 de setembro), arts. 3 and 7, as amended by Lei n.º 62/2026; and the Code of Criminal Procedure (Código de Processo Penal), art. 219. The CRP allows a migrant who entered or stays irregularly to be detained only by a measure "subject to judicial control" (art. 27(3)(c)). Habeas corpus lies against "illegal imprisonment or detention", and the judge must decide within eight days (art. 31). Legality: a detained foreigner must be brought before a criminal judge within 48 hours "for validation and possible application of coercive measures" (Lei 23/2007, art. 146(1)). Placement in a centre is one of the coercive measures in art. 142(1)(c), and under CPP art. 219 an appeal lies against any decision that "applies, replaces or maintains" such measures. Length: detention may not exceed "what is strictly necessary", up to 180 days, extendable once by the same period (art. 146(3)). Since September 2026 it "must be judicially reassessed at the end of each 30-day period" (Lei 34/94, art. 3(2)). Conditions: art. 146-A sets out detainees' rights (contact with lawyers, family and consuls; health care; special attention to vulnerable people; separate family quarters). Lei 34/94, art. 7 applies the Code on the Execution of Sentences (Lei 115/2009) to detention in these centres. Under art. 116 of that Code, detainees may file complaints "relating to the execution of measures depriving them of liberty" with the director, the inspection services, the Ombudsman, the ECtHR, the CPT and the UN Committee against Torture. Coverage: all foreigners held in temporary installation centres or equivalent spaces, including people refused entry at the border (arts. 38(4) and 40). Asylum seekers have parallel safeguards under the Asylum Act.

### q23 · Right to an individualized detention decision.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 142 and 146, and in Law 34/94 on temporary installation centres (Lei n.º 34/94, de 14 de setembro), arts. 3 and 5-A, as amended by Lei n.º 62/2026. It is read with the Constitution (CRP), art. 27(3)(c). Detention of a migrant for removal is a judicial coercive measure. A criminal judge may order placement in a temporary installation centre only "if there is a risk of absconding" (art. 142(1)). Placement is one option on a graded list that also includes periodic reporting, house arrest with electronic monitoring, a bond, surrender of travel documents and an open-regime centre. The risk of absconding must be "assessed in light of the personal, family, social and economic or professional situation of the foreign citizen". The relevant factors include an unknown address, the absence of family ties in Portugal, doubts about identity and known preparations to flee (art. 142(3)). The judge must validate the detention within 48 hours (art. 146(1)). Detention may not exceed "what is strictly necessary" (art. 146(3)) and must be judicially reassessed every 30 days (Lei 34/94, art. 3(2)). An open regime must be used where it suits the person's "personal and procedural circumstances" and there is no risk of absconding (Lei 34/94, art. 5-A(2)). Art. 146-A(3) requires special attention to vulnerable detainees. Scope: these rules govern detention of irregularly present foreigners for removal. Holding people refused entry at the border (art. 38(4)) also needs a judicial decision, but the text does not set out individual criteria; asylum seekers fall under the Asylum Act.

### q25 · Right to be free from torture and inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (CRP), arts. 15(1) and 25; the Penal Code (Código Penal, DL 400/82 as republished by DL 48/95), arts. 243-245; and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007), arts. 40(4), 143 and 146-A. Art. 25 of the CRP provides that "the moral and physical integrity of persons is inviolable" and that "no one may be subjected to torture, nor to cruel, degrading or inhuman treatment or punishment". Under art. 15(1), foreigners and stateless persons "who are present or reside in Portugal" enjoy the same rights as citizens. This covers every migrant, whatever their status. Art. 16(2) requires fundamental rights to be interpreted in harmony with the UDHR. The Penal Code makes it a crime, punishable by 1 to 5 years, for any official responsible for criminal investigation or for the "protection, guard or surveillance of a detained person" to torture or treat a person "in a cruel, degrading or inhuman manner" (art. 243). Aggravated forms carry 3 to 12 years, and 8 to 16 if the victim dies (art. 244). A superior who fails to report such acts commits a crime (art. 245). The Foreigners Act forbids removal "to any country where the foreign citizen may suffer torture, inhuman or degrading treatment within the meaning of Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights" (art. 143(1)). The same guarantee extends to people refused entry at the border (art. 40(4)). Art. 146-A guarantees detainees in temporary installation centres health care and special attention to victims of torture. ECHR art. 3 and the UN Convention against Torture also apply directly under CRP art. 8(2).


## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Portugal. No national law expressly prohibits officers from using force as a means of preventing migrants from entering the territory. The Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Regime jurídico de entrada, permanência, saída e afastamento de estrangeiros) sets out a procedure for refusal of entry. Art. 32 lists the grounds. Under art. 38(1)-(2) the refusal decision "is issued after hearing the foreign citizen" and is notified "in a language he or she can presumably understand, stating its grounds" and the right of judicial challenge. Art. 39 allows an appeal to the administrative courts, but "with merely devolutive effect", so the appeal does not suspend removal. Art. 40 gives a person who is refused entry the right to contact consular authorities, an interpreter, health care, material support and a lawyer. Under art. 38(4), if re-embarkation cannot take place within 48 hours, a judge must be informed. These safeguards govern refusal and removal. Unlike the French rule of a clear day before repatriation, they include no waiting period before enforcement, and none of them forbids physical force to stop entry. The only limits on force are the general rules of police law. Under Decree-Law 457/99 (Decreto-Lei n.º 457/99, de 5 de novembro), art. 2, firearms may be used only "in case of absolute necessity, as an extreme measure" and proportionately. No statute or regulation addresses non-firearm force at the border, or force used specifically to prevent entry. Portugal's external Schengen borders are almost entirely its airports and seaports.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in Decree-Law 457/99 on the use of firearms in police action (Decreto-Lei n.º 457/99, de 5 de novembro), arts. 1-4. The decree binds every police body defined as a criminal-police authority, which covers the PSP, the GNR and the PJ; they took over border policing after SEF was abolished, with the PSP's UNEF created by Lei n.º 55-C/2025. Art. 2(1) allows firearms only "in case of absolute necessity, as an extreme measure, when other less dangerous means prove ineffective". Art. 2(2) requires officers to "respect and preserve human life". Under art. 3(2), firearms may be used against persons only in three exhaustively listed situations. These are: to repel a current unlawful attack "if there is imminent danger of death or serious injury to physical integrity"; to prevent a particularly serious crime that threatens human lives; or to detain or stop the escape of a person who poses that threat. Art. 3(3) forbids intimidating anyone by gunfire where firearm use is not allowed. Art. 4 requires a prior warning. Irregular entry is defined in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007), art. 181. Overstaying is only an administrative offence under art. 192, punished by fines of €80-700. Neither is a ground on which lethal force may be used. Lethal force is therefore lawful only where a person threatens life, and never as a means of stopping migrants from entering. The decree does not mention migrants or borders expressly; the protection follows from its closed list of permitted uses.

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: No, government may punish trafficked or smuggled migrants
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right in Portugal. The Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho) protects victims of trafficking and of migrant smuggling ("auxílio à imigração ilegal") through residence and support measures, but it grants no exemption from criminal prosecution. Under art. 109(1), a residence permit is granted to a foreigner who "is or has been a victim of criminal offences linked to trafficking in persons or to aiding illegal immigration, even if he or she entered the country illegally". Except for identified trafficking victims (art. 109(4)), the permit normally depends on cooperating with the authorities. Art. 111 provides a 30-60 day reflection period during which "no removal measure may be enforced". Art. 112 guarantees subsistence, urgent medical care, protection and legal aid. These measures shield victims from removal, not from punishment. Irregular entry and stay are not crimes as such: art. 181 defines them, and overstaying is an administrative offence fined under art. 192. Some offences a smuggled or trafficked migrant may commit remain crimes, however. Art. 187(1) punishes a foreigner who enters during an entry ban "with imprisonment of up to two years or a fine of up to 100 days", and document fraud is punishable under the Penal Code. Art. 183 punishes those who facilitate illegal entry, not the migrant, but no provision exempts the migrant from other offences. Portuguese law has no specific non-punishment clause for trafficking victims who commit offences as a direct result of being trafficked. The Council of Europe's anti-trafficking monitoring body, GRETA, confirmed this gap in its 2022 report and again in 2026. Non-prosecution depends on general criminal-law defences and on prosecutorial discretion.


## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Convictions do not affect citizenship status
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Nationality Act (Lei da Nacionalidade, Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro, as amended up to Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026), arts. 8, 12-A and 12-B, read with Constitutional Court Rulings 1134/2025 and 409/2026. The Act's chapter on loss of nationality contains one ground only, a voluntary declaration: those who, "being nationals of another State, declare that they do not want to be Portuguese" lose Portuguese nationality (art. 8). An acquisition can also be declared null where it rested on "false documents ... or false declarations" (art. 12-A(1)). Nullity cannot be declared if it would leave the person stateless (art. 12-A(2)). Good-faith holding of nationality for 10 years consolidates it, except where it was obtained fraudulently (art. 12-B). Both rules concern fraud in the acquisition itself, which this indicator excludes. No provision allows a naturalised citizen to be stripped of nationality because of a later criminal or administrative conviction. Parliament tried twice to add an accessory penalty of loss of nationality to the Penal Code (new art. 69-D). It would have applied to dual nationals sentenced to 5 or more years for listed crimes committed within 15 years of acquiring nationality. The Constitutional Court struck down both versions (Ruling 1134/2025; Ruling 409/2026, unanimous) for breaching equality and proportionality, and the President vetoed the decree. On 3 July 2026 Parliament refused to override the ruling and rejected a narrower third version. Convictions matter only before acquisition: under art. 6(1)(f), a final prison sentence of more than 3 years for terrorism, violent or organised crime, crimes against State security or aiding illegal immigration bars naturalisation. Under arts. 9-10, the Public Prosecutor may oppose acquisition by declaration within two years of its registration on those grounds.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Nationality Act (Lei da Nacionalidade, Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro, as amended by Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, in force 19 May 2026), arts. 6, 7 and 15. The Government "grants Portuguese nationality" to adults who have resided legally in Portugal "for at least seven years" if they are nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries or EU citizens, or "10 years" if they are nationals of other countries (art. 6(1)(b)). Further requirements are knowledge of Portuguese language, culture, history and national symbols, shown by test or certificate (c); knowledge of the rights and duties of citizenship and of the political organisation of the State (d); a solemn declaration of adherence to democratic rule-of-law principles (e); no final conviction to more than 3 years' effective imprisonment for listed serious crimes (f); no threat to security (g); not being subject to UN/EU restrictive measures (h); and the means to support themselves (i). The Minister of Justice decides on application (art. 7). Under art. 15(1), legal residence means presence with a regularised status under any title, visa or permit of the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007). Work, job-seeker, highly qualified, CPLP-mobility and other economic permits therefore count. Interrupted periods are added up if they fall within 9 or 12 years (art. 15(3)). The 2026 reform lengthened the earlier 5-year requirement. Applications pending on 19 May 2026 are decided under the previous text (Organic Law 1/2026, art. 7). Irregular residence does not count, and the abolished 'manifestação de interesse' route only counts once a permit is issued.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Nationality Act (Lei da Nacionalidade, Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro, as amended by Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026), arts. 6(1), 6(3) and 15. The general naturalisation route requires legal residence of 7 years for nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries and EU citizens, or 10 years for other nationals (art. 6(1)(b)). Applicants must also meet the language, civic-knowledge, democratic-adherence, security, criminal-record and self-sufficiency requirements (art. 6(1)(c)-(i)). Refugees and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection are expressly covered. Art. 15(1) defines legal residence as presence with a regularised status under any title "provided for ... in the asylum regime" (Lei n.º 27/2008), as well as under the Foreigners Act. Holders of humanitarian or exceptional residence permits under Lei n.º 23/2007 also count. Stateless persons get a preferential route: the Government "grants Portuguese nationality to stateless persons who have resided legally in Portugal for at least four years" and meet requirements (c) to (h). They are exempt from the self-sufficiency condition (art. 6(3)), and their interrupted residence periods are added up within a 6-year window (art. 15(3)). Unlike stateless persons, refugees have no shorter period. Most refugees, who are not CPLP or EU nationals, therefore need 10 years of legal residence. Asylum seekers with pending claims hold a provisional title under the asylum regime. Whether that time counts as "legal residence" is not settled in the text I read. Applications filed before 19 May 2026 are decided under the former, shorter rules.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Nationality Act (Lei da Nacionalidade, Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro, as amended by Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026), arts. 1(1)(f), 2, 3, 5, 6 and 15. Spouses: "a foreigner married for more than three years to a Portuguese national may acquire Portuguese nationality by declaration made during the marriage" (art. 3(1)). A foreign partner in a de facto union of more than three years may do so after court recognition of the union (art. 3(3)). These routes depend on the absence of the conviction, security and sanctions grounds in art. 6(1)(f)-(h) (art. 3(4)). The Public Prosecutor may oppose within two years of registration for lack of effective ties (arts. 9-10). There is no opposition where the marriage or union has lasted more than six years or the couple has Portuguese children, save on those grounds (art. 9(2)). Children: minor children of a parent who acquires nationality may acquire it by declaration (art. 2), and adopted children of a Portuguese national acquire it by declaration (art. 5). Children born in Portugal to foreign parents are Portuguese by origin if they so declare and one parent had resided legally for at least five years at the birth (art. 1(1)(f)). Otherwise they can be naturalised as minors where a parent has five years' legal residence and the child attends compulsory schooling (art. 6(2)). Family-reunification permit holders who are neither spouses of Portuguese nationals nor minors may naturalise under art. 6(1) after 7 years (CPLP/EU nationals) or 10 years (others). Their reunification permit counts as legal residence under art. 15(1).

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: Yes, all migrants can obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Nationality Act (Lei n.º 37/81, de 3 de outubro – Lei da Nacionalidade, as amended up to Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026), Articles 6, 8 and 27. The naturalisation requirements in Article 6(1) cover age, lawful residence (7 years for nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries and EU citizens, 10 years for others), language, culture and civic knowledge, adherence to the rule of law, no criminal record and self-subsistence. None of them requires the applicant to give up a prior nationality. Article 27 accepts plural nationality outright: "If a person has two or more nationalities and one of them is Portuguese, only the latter is relevant under Portuguese law." Under Article 8, Portuguese nationality is lost only by those who, "being nationals of another State, declare that they do not want to be Portuguese". Loss is therefore voluntary, and holding another nationality is not a ground for it. The same applies to every other way of acquiring nationality: marriage or de facto union (Article 3), adoption (Article 5), stateless persons (Article 6(3)) and minors born in Portugal (Article 6(2)). No route depends on the applicant's country of origin or on reciprocity. Any loss of the original nationality depends only on the law of the country of origin, not on Portuguese law. The 2026 reform (Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, in force 19 May 2026) lengthened residence periods and added tests, but it brought in no renunciation requirement.

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to regularize after certain conditions are met
- Implementation: Rarely; Sometimes

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho – Lei de Estrangeiros, as amended up to Lei n.º 62/2026), Articles 109, 122 and 123. The general regularisation route for irregular workers was abolished by Decree-Law n.º 37-A/2024 (Decreto-Lei n.º 37-A/2024). That route was the 'manifestação de interesse' (expression of interest) under former Articles 88(2) and 89(2), which now read "[Revogado]" (repealed). Article 88(1) now requires a work contract and social security registration on top of the general entry requirements. Narrower in-country routes remain. Article 122(1) lists groups who "do not need a visa to obtain a temporary residence permit". They include persons born in Portugal who have stayed there (points b and d), parents of Portuguese minor children (point k), persons with a serious illness that prevents return (point g), and persons who "are or have been victims of a serious or very serious criminal or administrative offence relating to the employment relationship". That last group must have reported the offence and cooperate with the authorities, with evidence confirmed by the labour inspectorate (point m). Article 109 grants permits to victims of trafficking or of aiding illegal immigration "even if they entered the country illegally". Article 123 is an exceptional regime. The AIMA board may propose, or the minister may decide on their own initiative, a temporary permit for foreigners "who do not meet the requirements", on grounds of national interest, humanitarian reasons (including the best interests of the child) or relevant public-interest activity. Those decisions must give reasons. These routes are specific to certain situations and partly discretionary. Since June 2024 there is no general way for an irregular migrant to regularise through employment alone.


## Opinion and expression

### q83 · Right to freedom to hold opinion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP, 1976), articles 37(1)-(2), 41(3) and 15(1). Article 37(1) CRP provides that "everyone has the right to express and disseminate their thought freely" by word, image or any other means, "without impediment or discrimination". Article 37(2) bars any form of censorship. Holding opinions is also protected negatively. Article 41(3) provides that no authority may question anyone about their convictions or religious practice, except for anonymised statistics, and that no one may be prejudiced for refusing to answer. Article 13(2) bars anyone from being prejudiced or deprived of a right because of their "political or ideological convictions". Article 15(1) extends to foreigners and stateless persons "who are present or reside in Portugal" the rights of Portuguese citizens, so the guarantee reaches regular and irregular migrants and asylum seekers alike. The exceptions in article 15(2) concern political rights and public functions, not freedom of opinion. Article 18(1) makes these rights directly applicable against public and private actors. No provision of the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007) makes holding an opinion a ground for refusal or removal. Migrants therefore enjoy freedom of opinion commensurate with citizens.

### q85 · Right to freedom of expression.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Effectively Always

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP, 1976), articles 37 and 15(1). Article 37(1) CRP guarantees to "everyone" (todos) the right to express and disseminate their thought freely by word, image or any other means, and the right to inform, to inform oneself and to be informed, "without impediments or discrimination". Article 37(2) states that these rights cannot be prevented or limited by "any type or form of censorship". Article 37(3) channels any abuses into the general principles of criminal law or administrative offences, decided by the courts or an independent administrative body. Article 37(4) gives all persons the right of reply and correction on equal terms. The guarantee is framed for "everyone", and article 15(1) gives foreigners and stateless persons "who are present or reside in Portugal" the rights of citizens. Freedom of expression therefore covers regular and irregular migrants and asylum seekers. The citizen-only exceptions in article 15(2) concern political rights such as voting and standing for office, not expression as such. Article 18(1) makes the right directly applicable, and any restriction must meet the necessity and proportionality requirements of article 18(2)-(3). The Religious Freedom Act (Lei n.º 16/2001), article 8(d), separately protects the right of everyone to express and disseminate their thought on religious matters.


## Peaceful assembly and association

### q87 · Right to freedom of association.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP, 1976), articles 46, 55 and 15(1); the Labour Code (Código do Trabalho, Lei n.º 7/2009), articles 4 and 440; and the Immigrant Associations Act (Lei n.º 115/99, de 3 de agosto). Article 46(1) CRP gives the right to form associations freely "without dependence on any authorisation". Article 46(2) bars the state from dissolving associations or suspending their activities except by court decision. Article 55 recognises trade-union freedom for "workers", guaranteed "without any discrimination", including the freedom to form unions and to join them. Articles 45 and 46 refer to "citizens", but article 15(1) extends to foreigners present or residing in Portugal the rights of citizens. The only exclusion, in article 15(2), concerns political rights, which affects political-party membership. Under article 440 of the Labour Code, "workers have the right to form trade union associations at all levels". Article 4 gives foreign or stateless workers "authorised to exercise" employment the same rights as Portuguese workers. Law 115/99, articles 1-4, regulates associations representing immigrants and their descendants. It guarantees their independence from the state and their rights to take part in immigration policy and to intervene with authorities in defence of immigrants' rights. Union freedom under article 55 CRP attaches to workers as such. However, the Labour Code's explicit equal-treatment clause covers only authorised workers.

### q89 · Right to freedom of assembly.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP, 1976), articles 45 and 15(1). Article 45(1) CRP provides that "citizens have the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, even in places open to the public, without need of any authorisation". Article 45(2) recognises the right of demonstration. Article 45 refers to "citizens", but article 15(1) provides that foreigners and stateless persons "who are present or reside in Portugal enjoy the rights... of Portuguese citizens". Assembly is not among the article 15(2) exceptions (political rights, non-technical public functions, rights reserved to citizens), so the right extends to regular and irregular migrants and asylum seekers. Article 18(1) makes the right directly applicable, and article 18(2)-(3) limits restrictions to what is necessary and proportionate. The Religious Freedom Act (Lei n.º 16/2001), article 8(f), also guarantees the right to assemble and demonstrate according to one's religious convictions, within the limits of articles 45 and 46 CRP. The implementing statute, Decree-Law 406/74 on the right of assembly, is likewise worded in terms of citizens. Amnesty International Portugal has recommended amending it to extend the right expressly to non-nationals. The protection for migrants therefore rests on the constitutional equality clause, not on express wording in the statute.


## Thought, conscience, and religion or belief

### q81 · Right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa, CRP, 1976), articles 41 and 15(1), and in the Religious Freedom Act (Lei n.º 16/2001, de 22 de junho – Lei da Liberdade Religiosa), articles 1, 2 and 8. Article 41(1) CRP provides that "freedom of conscience, religion and worship is inviolable". Article 41(2) adds that no one may be persecuted or deprived of rights because of their convictions or religious practice. Article 15(1) CRP gives foreigners and stateless persons "who are present or reside in Portugal" the rights of Portuguese citizens. Because the wording covers presence as well as residence, irregular migrants and asylum seekers are included. The exceptions in article 15(2) (political rights, non-technical public functions, rights reserved to citizens) do not touch religious freedom. Article 1 of Law 16/2001 states that this freedom "is inviolable and guaranteed to everyone" in accordance with the Constitution, the UDHR and international law. Article 2 bars anyone from being privileged, prejudiced or deprived of any right because of religious belief or practice. Article 8 covers the right to have, change or leave a religion, to worship, to proselytise, and to assemble and associate on religious grounds. Under article 18(1) CRP, these rights, freedoms and guarantees are directly applicable and bind public and private entities. The protection therefore covers all migrants on the same terms as citizens.


## Victims of crime

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (CRP), arts. 15(1) and 20(1); in the Code of Criminal Procedure (Código de Processo Penal, Decreto-Lei n.º 78/87), arts. 49, 67-A and 68; and in the Victims' Statute (Estatuto da Vítima, approved by Lei n.º 130/2015, de 4 de setembro), art. 3. Article 15(1) CRP gives foreigners present or resident in Portugal the rights of citizens, and art. 20(1) guarantees everyone access to the courts. Under the Code of Criminal Procedure, a 'victim' is any natural person who suffered harm directly caused by a crime (art. 67-A(1)(a)), with no nationality or residence condition. Victims have 'rights of information, assistance, protection and active participation in the criminal proceedings' (art. 67-A(4)). A complaint (queixa) may be lodged by the holder of the right, by a lawyer or by a specially empowered agent (art. 49(3)). Offended persons over 16 may join the proceedings as assistente (art. 68(1)(a)), which lets them offer evidence, bring their own accusation and appeal (art. 69). Article 3 of the Victims' Statute guarantees that 'every victim, regardless of ancestry, nationality, social condition... language' enjoys these fundamental rights. Its interpretation and protection rights apply in the same way. The right therefore covers all migrants, including irregular migrants. Trafficking and smuggling victims also have reinforced participation rights, including a reflection period, interpretation and legal protection (Foreigners Act, Lei n.º 23/2007, arts. 111-112). A practical limit is that police contact can expose irregular victims to removal proceedings; there is no general 'firewall' rule.

### q45 · Right of victims of crime to immigration status or relief from expulsion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho), arts. 107(4), 109-113 and 122(1)(m). Article 109(1) grants a residence permit to a foreigner 'who is or has been a victim of criminal offences linked to trafficking in persons or to assistance to illegal immigration, even if he entered the country illegally or does not meet the conditions' for a permit. The permit follows a reflection period of 30 to 60 days. It requires that the victim's presence be needed for investigations or proceedings, that the victim be willing to cooperate, and that the victim have broken ties with the offenders. During the reflection period 'no removal measure may be enforced' against the person (art. 111(3)), who receives subsistence, urgent medical care, protection, interpretation and legal protection (art. 112). Article 122(1)(m) exempts from the visa requirement, and so opens a temporary residence permit to, foreigners who are or were victims of a serious or very serious criminal or administrative offence 'relating to the employment relationship'. This applies where the labour inspectorate has evidence and the worker has reported the offence and cooperates. Article 122(1)(n) allows former art. 109 permit holders to move to an ordinary temporary permit. Article 107(4) allows an autonomous permit for family members, including when the Public Prosecutor has charged the sponsor with domestic violence. Holders of an art. 109 permit may later obtain an ordinary temporary permit without a visa (art. 122(1)(n)). The avenue is limited to these crimes and mostly conditional on cooperation. There is no general status route for victims of other crimes, who must rely on the discretionary humanitarian regime (art. 123).


## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho, Lei de Estrangeiros), the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho) and Decree-Law 67/2004 (Decreto-Lei n.º 67/2004, de 25 de março). Under the Foreigners Act, art. 40-J (added by Lei n.º 62/2026) requires authorities screening minors to act "always taking into consideration the best interests of the child" and to give unaccompanied minors an independent representative "as quickly as possible". Art. 135(1)(e) bars the expulsion of unaccompanied minors below the age of criminal responsibility. Art. 122(1)(a), (b) and (e) let children born in Portugal, children attending school and children under compulsory guardianship obtain residence without a visa. Art. 123(1)(b) allows exceptional humanitarian permits, "namely considering the best interests of the child". Art. 114 requires child-adapted procedures for child victims of trafficking. Art. 146-A(3) and (7) give detained minors special attention, leisure and, depending on how long they are held, education. Art. 31 sets specific entry and exit controls for unaccompanied minors. Under the Asylum Act, art. 2(1)(y) and (ag) list minors and unaccompanied minors as particularly vulnerable people with special reception needs. Art. 79 requires the tribunal to be notified and a representative to be appointed within 15 working days. For irregular children, Decree-Law 67/2004, art. 2, creates a register "exclusively to ensure minors' access to health care and pre-school and school education". Data in the register cannot be used against the parents. The protections cover all migrant children, including irregular ones; the asylum-specific safeguards apply to applicants and beneficiaries of international protection. The law still allows accompanied children to be detained in temporary installation centres, subject to these safeguards.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho) and the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho). For family members admitted through reunification, Foreigners Act art. 107(4) allows an autonomous residence permit before the normal two-year period in exceptional cases. The listed cases include "indictment by the Public Prosecutor for the crime of domestic violence", even while the reunification application is still pending. This lets migrant spouses, mostly women, leave an abusive sponsor without losing their status. Arts. 109-113 grant residence to victims of trafficking or of facilitated illegal immigration "even if they entered the country illegally". They also provide a reflection period of 30-60 days during which no removal may be enforced (art. 111), plus subsistence, security, interpretation and legal aid (art. 112). Under art. 113(2), permit holders with specific needs, "such as minors or pregnant women, disabled persons, victims of sexual violence or other forms of violence", receive medical and social assistance. Art. 146-A(3) requires special attention to pregnant women in immigration detention. The Asylum Act lists pregnant women, single-parent families and victims of rape or serious sexual violence as particularly vulnerable (art. 2(1)(y)). It names victims of domestic violence and of female genital mutilation as applicants with special reception needs (art. 2(1)(ag)). Art. 17-A requires the asylum procedure, reception and health care to take account of an applicant's "sex, gender identity" and experience of violence. Art. 73(3) guarantees adequate health care to pregnant refugees and to survivors of domestic violence and FGM. Most of these provisions are written in gender-neutral terms. The trafficking permit is generally tied to cooperation with the authorities or to the victim's personal situation. The asylum safeguards cover applicants and beneficiaries of international protection only.

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Portugal protects this right in the Asylum Act (Lei n.º 27/2008, de 30 de junho) and the Foreigners Act (Lei n.º 23/2007, de 4 de julho). The Asylum Act lists "disabled persons" among particularly vulnerable people (art. 2(1)(y)). They are also named among applicants with special reception needs, along with people with serious illnesses and mental disorders (art. 2(1)(ag)). Art. 17-A requires the asylum procedure, material reception conditions and health care to take into account applicants whose capacity is limited "by virtue of their ... disability or serious illness, mental disorder". These applicants must get the support they need, and deadlines for interviews and evidence may be extended, with expert support at interviews. Art. 73(2)-(3) guarantees disabled refugees and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection adequate health care, including mental-health treatment, on the same terms as nationals. In the Foreigners Act, art. 146-A(3) requires detention centres to give special attention to "persons with disabilities". Art. 113(2) gives disabled holders of trafficking-victim permits the necessary medical and social assistance. Art. 135(1)(f) bars the expulsion of foreigners whose adult children depend on them because of "disability, serious illness or incapacity". Art. 122(1)(g) grants residence without a visa to people with an illness requiring prolonged medical care that prevents their return. The detailed safeguards apply mainly to asylum seekers, refugees, detainees and trafficking victims. Outside those regimes, no specific disability rule covers ordinary migrants, who rely on general equality and health rules.
