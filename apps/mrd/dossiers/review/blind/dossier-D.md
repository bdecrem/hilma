# Dossier D — Colombia


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right for all migrants. National law in Colombia protects this right for migrants with a residence status at the district and municipal level in National Law 1070/2006. This law allows migrants with residency visa and who have been registered to vote according to the national regulation, to vote in local elections.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National Constitution, article 99 prohibits foreigners the possibility to stand for elections.


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes (applies to administrative, civil, and criminal courts and tribunals)
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 28, 29, 30, 100, 229. Additionally, National Law 1564/2012 in Colombia protects citizens and migrants due process right in any type of judicial procedure. The access to justice is a Constitutional right to all the habitants of Colombia. This right is extended to foreigners by virtue of the Constitution of Colombia in article 100 which says: “foreigners in Colombia will enjoy the same civil rights that are granted to Colombians." This makes article 29 which contains due process and defense rights as fully applicable to foreign people.

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in: 1) The Constitution of Colombia, article 29. 2) Law 941 of 2005, article 2 and 43.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in: 1) The Criminal Procedure Code, article 144. 2) Agreement 1518 of the 28th of August of 2002 of the Administrative Chamber of the Superior Council of the Judiciary, modified by Agreement PSAA10-7339 of the 6th of October of 2010. Additionally, National Law 905/2004, article 144 protects the rights of migrants to have an interpreter under criminal proceedings and national judicial decision in Colombia protects the rights of migrants to have interpretation during a criminal proceeding with decisions T-956/2013, T-295/2018, T-530/2019, and SU-397/21.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects migrants from civil or criminal sanctions for unauthorized entry in: - Decree 4062 of 2011, article 3, 23 - Decree 2357 of 2020, article 14, 15.


## Education

### q127 · Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants).
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for regular migrants in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 67 and 13. - Decree 860 of 2003, articles 1-3 - Law 30 of 92, article 5, 14 - Constitutional Court, T-068 of 2012 in Colombia. Even though the Constitution and the Constitutional Court has established that both citizens and foreigns should have the right to access to higher education, regular migration status is required in order to file for admission.


## Equal protection

### q131 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

Pursuant to the Constitution of Colombia, article 13 “all people are born free and equal before the law, they will receive the same protection and treatment by the authorities and they will enjoy the same rights, freedoms and opportunities without any discrimination for reasons of sex, race, national or family origin, language, religion, opinion political or philosophical.” National origin is interpreted to include migrant status. Likewise, pursuant to article 100, “foreigners in Colombia will enjoy the same civil rights that are granted to Colombians. However, the law may, for reasons of public order, subordinate to special conditions or deny the exercise of certain civil rights to foreigners. Likewise, foreigners will enjoy, in the territory of the Republic, the guarantees granted to nationals, except for the limitations established by the Constitution or the law. Political rights are reserved for nationals, but the law may grant foreigners residing in Colombia the right to vote in municipal or district elections and referendums.”


## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: Any conviction (even minor administrative offenses) can lead to loss of residence status
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

National law in Colombia regulates the ability of migrant's criminal and other convictions affecting residence status in Resolution 2357 of 2020, article 15. Additionally, National Decree 1067/2015, article 2.2.1.13.2.1 also mentions the causes to expel a migrant. According to Decree 1067/2015 and Resolution 2357/2020 being condemned in Colombia is considered as a valid reason to sanction a migrant with an order of expulsion. Decree 1067/2015, article 2.2.1.13.2.1 and Resolution 2357/2020, article 15 highlight that being condemned, regardless of the type of crime committed, is enough reason to be considered a serious infringement which motivates the initiation of an expulsion process.

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Colombia bans refoulement on the basis of a risk of return to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment in: - Law 2136 of 2021, article 4. - The Criminal Procedure Code (Law 0906 of 2004), article 494. Additionally, National Decree 1067/2015, article 2.2.3.1.6.20 protects the non-refoulement principle.

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Colombia bans refoulement on the basis of a risk of return to persecution based on race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion in: - Decree 1067, article 2.2.3.1.6.20. - Law 2136 of 2021.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Colombia bans refoulement on the basis of a risk of serius deprivations of fundamental human rights in: - Decree 1067, article 2.2.3.1.6.20. - Law 2136 of 2021.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Colombia ratified the Convention of Refugees of 1951 by means of Law 35 of 1951 and therefore is bound to the non-refoulement principle. This principle is also implemented by the local regulations about refugees in Decree 2840 of 2013, section 32. Pursuant to both regulations, a refugee applicant will not be returned to another country, whether of origin or not, where their life, freedom and personal integrity are in danger due to their race, religion, nationality, belonging to a certain social group, or their political opinions. Nevertheless, there are no prohibitios of expulsion to a country where the migrant could be subject to refoulement through a subsequent expulsion.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in: 1) The Constitution of Colombia article 29; 2) Resolution 2357 of 2020, articles 1, 13, 18, 19, 20; 3) The Code of the Administrative Procedure (Law 1437 of 2011), articles 47 and 49. Specifically, Law 1437 of 2011, article 49 indicates the administrative act that confirms the decision must (among others), individualize the person who commits the conduct. According to immigration regulations, the assessment of the sanction must attend to the principles of proportionality, objectivity, and reasonableness, arguing in the administrative act that decides, the description of the facts attributable to the subject of verification, as well as those that are favorable to him/her. The motivation of the decision must integrate the analysis of the duties imposed by the immigration norm, the facts constituting the infraction, the classification of the offense and if there are criteria to commute, mitigate, aggravate, or exonerate the subject of verification.

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in: - The Constitution of Colombia, article 29 - Resolution 2357 of 2020 article 1 - The Code of the Administrative Procedure (Law 1437 of 2011), articles 47 and 74.


## Family

### q73 · Right of settled migrants to family reunification.
- Law: Conditional
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia provides settled migrants with legal avenues to facilitate reunification only with immediate and dependent family members in Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 93. Pursuant to this law, the beneficiary visa may be granted to the spouse, permanent partner and children under 25 years of age who are economically dependent on the main holder of a visa, or who, being older than 25, present some type of disability duly qualification that prevents them from having economic independence. Non-dependent family members are not otherwise eligible for family reunification. Reunification is only possible for regular migrants based on visa categories.

### q75 · Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.
- Law: Conditional
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in Colombia protects this right, conditional on visa category, in the Constitution of Colombia, article 86. Via “tutela” it could be possible to appeal the decision whenever it could be proved there has been a violation of any fundamental right. The tutela action is a legal resource designed within the Constitution, article 86, and Decree 2591/1991, where any citizen or foreigner can use it as a strategy to obtain immediate protection whenever their fundamental rights are threatened or violated by the action or omission of any public authority or individuals. So far the tutela has been used to claim the protection of family reunification in those cases where a migrant or refugee has been deported or expelled.

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Colombia recognizes same sex relationships under the family reunification framework in: 1) Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 93; 2) Constitutional Court, C-075 of 2007; 3) Constitutional Court, C-131 of 2018; 4) Constitutional Court, C-577 of 2011. This right is also protected by the National Constitution, article 42 and 100 in Colombia. Additionally, with the national decision SU-216/2016 Constitutional Court in Colombia recognizes same sex marriages.

### q79 · Right to family reunification without resource conditions
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National Resolution 5477/2022, article 93 in Colombia specifies when family migrants can apply to a beneficiary visa. Pursuant to Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 93, one of the requirements for the beneficiary visa is a written communication from the main holder requesting the beneficiary's visa and declaring the dependency and economic responsibility for the expenses inherent to the trip and/or stay of the beneficiary in Colombia, including health coverage services. The main holder must demonstrate through averages in bank statements of the last 6 months, or labor certification, that s/he has sufficient income to assume economic responsibility for the beneficiaries.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right given no guarantees. However, pursuant to Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 80, individuals who have been recognized as stateless may apply for a Migrant Stateless Visa and may sponsor beneficiaries. After accumulating 2 years with this visa continuously and uninterrupted, they may apply for a residence visa and then to citizenship.


## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses
- Implementation: Sometimes

The national law in Colombia protects the right of access to emergency health care services in Decree 780/2016, article 2.5.3.2.2 for all migrants regardless of status. Access to other health care services, however, is more limited especially for irregular migrants. Despite the limitations, there are some levels of protections in place for irregular migrants usually via judicial decisions. The Constitutional Court has said that migrant women in a state of pregnancy and in an irregular situation have the right to receive necessary medical care free of charge, according to the diagnosis made by the doctor, before, during and after childbirth if they do not have the financial resources to pay for them directly. This care includes prenatal checkups, medical exams, medicines and other services that are ordered by the doctor (Caselaw SU-677 of 2017 and T-298 of 2019). Migrants in an irregular situation who have terminal illnesses or chronic diseases, such as cancer or HIV, have the right to receive treatment as part of emergency care when this treatment is requested by the doctor and the life of the person is at risk (case law T-705 of 2017; T-210 of 2018; T-197 of 2010; T-246 of 2020 and T-496 of 2020). Additionally, in the case of Venezuelan migrants, Decree 780 of 2016, Resolution 3015 of 2017, Decree 064 of 2020, Resolution 1178 of 2021 were issued in order to protect the right to health of Venezuelan migrants and refugees.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for regular migrants in: - The Constitution of Colombia, article 51. - Decree 1077 of 2015, article 2.1.1.6.9.1., modified by Decree 57 of 2021, article 10. - Constitutional Court, SU 016 of 2021. Furthermore, National Decree 057/2021, article 2.1.1.6.9.1 guarantees access to Venezuelan migrants who are interested in the allocation of the family housing subsidy in the form of rentals. Irregular migrants, no matter what their category is, have no chance to apply to public housing.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrant children in: - The Constitution of Colombia, articles 13, 44, 45, and 67. -Law 146 of 1994 (this law ratifies the International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families), article 30; Unique Reglamentary Decree (Law 1075 of 2015), articles 2.3.3.1.2.1., 2.3.3.1.2.3. Furthermore, National guidelines "Circulares Conjuntas1/2017, 16/2018" guarantee access to free primary educational institutions and services to migrant children commensurate with citizens.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Colombia protects this right for migrants in regular status who have been hired formally in: - The Constitution of Colombia, article 13, 100;. - The Labour Code, article 138 - Law 146 of 2004 (the law that approves the International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families), articles 7 and 10. The specific provisions regarding the right to fair wages and equal remuneration for regular migrants is outlined in the Labour Code, article 138 in the following terms: "1. For equal work carried out in a position, working day and efficiency conditions that are also equal, an equal salary must correspond, including all the elements referred to in article 127. 2. Differences in salary cannot be established for reasons of age, gender, nationality, race, religion, political opinion or union activities. 3. Any differential treatment in terms of salary or remuneration will be presumed unjustified until the employer demonstrates objective factors of differentiation."

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Employment is tied to specific employer; change of employer possible, but requires new work permit application
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right in: - The Constitution of Colombia, articles 26 and 13 - Law 74 of 1968 (law that approves the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights), article 6 - The Labour Code, article 11. Colombia protects the right of free choice of employment or occupation. Nevertheless, foreigners are under the obligation to comply with immigration laws to work in Colombia. Pursuant to the Colombian immigration system, visas may have an open work permit, a close work permit or may not have a work permit at all. Visas with an open work permit allow the exercise of any legal activity in the country including work. On the other hand, visas with a so-called closed work permit only allows working activities for the sponsoring employer/contractor. The Colombian immigration system includes 3 types of visas, each of them with several subcategories. Resolution 5477 of 2022 defines each subcategory of visa indicating the eligibility criteria, characteristics and if they have a work permit (open or close) or not. Pursuant to Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 109, the validity of a visa ends early when the circumstances for which it was granted change. This means that if a visa was issued due to work (sponsored by an employer/contractor) the termination of the employment contract/services agreement implies a change of circumstances and as result an early termination of the visa. Nevertheless, whenever there is an early termination of a visa, pursuant to Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 111, the foreigner has 30 calendar days of regular stay to remain in the country and apply for a new visa that is in line with the new activities in the country. Finally, if the foreigner has a visa with an open work permit, a change of employer would not translate into a change or loss of the immigration status.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National Resolution 5477/2022, article 93 in Colombia explains the beneficiary visa and the requirements to access it. The spouse/partner of the principal migrant can apply to the so-called “beneficiary visa” in order to enter the country, but they won’t be able to work. If the spouse/partner wants to work, they will have to apply for a specific type of visa.

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment implies loss of immigration status unless a new job is found within a specified time period.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for migrants whose immigration status is tied to employment via Resolution 5477 of 2022. For these group of migrants, loss of employment implies loss of immigration status unless a new job is found within a specified time period. In these circumstances, the foreigner has 30 calendar days to remain in the country and apply for a new visa if necessary. During this period the foreigner may not work. If the visa was granted due to an employment contract or services agreement, the termination of the contractual relationship will cause the early termination of the visa. On the other hand, immigration status is protected despite the loss of employment for those migrants who entered the country for reasons which differ from work purposes, such as humanitarian grounds, or family.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections.
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right in: 1) The Constitution of Colombia, article 13, 25, and 100. 2) The Labour Code, articles 10 and 13.

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Yes, immediate equal access to all types of social security protections
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in: 1) The Constitution of Colombia, articles 48, 13 and 100; 2) The Labour Code, articles 10 and 13; 3)Law 100 of 1993, articles 3, 4, and 153. Colombian law guarantees the access to the social security system to all habitants including migrants. Article 100 of the Constitution mentions that “foreigners in Colombia will enjoy the same civil rights that are granted to Colombians.” Under the Colombian legal system, the employer has the obligation to affiliate every employed to the social security system. Law 100/1993 mentions, "The affiliation to the General Social Security System in Health is mandatory for all inhabitants in Colombia. Consequently, it is up to every employer to affiliate its workers to this system and to facilitate affiliation to those who lack an association with an employer or ability to pay."

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: Yes, immediate equal access to all types of unemployment benefits.
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right in: 1) The Constitution of Colombia, articles 13, 25, and 100 ; 2) The Labour Code, articles 10, 13, 65, and 249. According to article 100 of the Constitution, every foreign person has the same civil rights as Colombian citizens. Therefore, migrants are covered by the national labour system which has established that every employer has the right to pay at the end of the contract, one month of salary for each year of service and proportionally per fraction of a year (Substantive Labour Code (SLC), article 249). At the same time, the employer has the obligation to make effective the severance pay according to the national law (Substantive Labour Code (SLC), article 65).

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are met.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for temporary migrant workers in Resolution 5477 of 2022 for any foreigner (temporary migrant worker) who accomplishes certain requirements such as living without interruptions inside the national territory over the past five years and who has stayed in the country with a migrant (M) visa. However, this law does not provide an avenue for foreigners, who hold a Visitor Visa due to temporary work or technical activities, to become lawful permanent residents.

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

Opportunity for temporary migrant workers to switch to permanent status after certain conditions are met is conditional on visa type.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, but limited.
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for migrants working in regular status with a formal contract in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 13, 25, and 100 ; and the Labour Code, articles 10, 13 and 64, 65, and 249. According to article 100 of the Constitution, every foreign person has the same civil rights as national citizens have. Therefore, they are protected by the national labor system which has established that every employer has the right to compensate the employed in the same way they would to a national citizen (Substantive Labour Code (SLC), article 65).

### q135 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of race or ethnicity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Colombia prohibits discrimination against migrants on the ground of race or ethnicity in: 1) The Constitution of Colombia, article 13; 2) Law 2136 of 2021, article 4. Pursuant to the Constitution of Colombia, article 13 "all people are born free and equal before the law, they will receive the same protection and treatment by the authorities and they will enjoy the same rights, freedoms and opportunities without any discrimination for reasons of sex, race, national or family origin, language, religion, opinion political or philosophical."

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in Colombia prohibits the detention of children on the basis of their immigration status in the following regulations: - Constitution of Colombia, articles 28, 44. - Police Code, article 168. -Childhood and Adolescence Code, article 21 and 187.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

National law in Colombia does not prohibit all detention of migrants on the basis of their migration status. However, national law strictly limits migrant detention and prohibits it in most circumstances. Immigration officers may retain migrants in order to verify their identity and/or situation of permanence in the national territory or when an administrative procedure is being carried out against them and they are required to do so. They could preventively be retained maximum for up to thirty-six (36) hours (Decree 1067/2015, article 2.2.1.13.3.2.). This does not constitute a detention under national law. More so, in case they do, officers will have to justify the verification proccess and the duration of it.

## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in Colombia does not explicitly prohibit the use of force by its officers or agents as a means of preventing migrants from entering the state's territory. The Public Forces and the National Police may use force on several circumstances but this action is limited as a last physical resource to protect the life and physical integrity of people, including their own, without a prior written order, to prevent, impede or overcome the threat or disturbance of coexistence and public safety. The use of force in hand of the National Police, is regulated in the Police Code articles 22, 149, 166 and 168. At the same time, Resolution 2903 of 2017 regulates the measure of the force. Besides, the Public Force must measure the use of force following the International Human Rights Law, Operations Law Manual 2015, and the Army must follow the Manual 627 in specific. Is important to stress that migration officers are not allowed to use force and they need to work in conjuction with the national police and public forces.The national officers decision to allow the entrance of refugees and migrants is detailed and developed through an specific procedure regulated, for example, in the Decree 1067/2015, chapter 11 sections 4,5 and 6.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right in the Police Code, section 166; Resolution 2903 of 2017, section 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13; and the Article 11 of the National Constitution. Requirements to allow the entrance of migrants inside de Colombian territory are outlined in National Decree 1067/2015, chapter 11 sections 4,5 and 6.

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: Yes, the national law provides exemption.
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right in the Criminal Code, article 188. - Law 985 of 2005 - Decree 1069 of 2014, article 2.


## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Only major convictions (e.g. criminal offenses) can lead to loss of citizenship status.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia provides that convictions for criminal or other offenses leads to a loss of the citizenship status of naturalized migrants in Law 43 of 1993, article 20. Article 24 of the same law establishes the reasons that may lead to immigrants' loss of citizenship status.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Colombia provides a pathway to citizenship for economic migrants via accumulation of time with a residence visa in Law 43 of 1993, article 5. Pursuant to this law, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs may issue a naturalization letter to the foreigners who during the 5 years immediately prior to the date of submission of the application have been domiciled in the country continuously. If the aforementioned foreigners are married, or are permanent companions of a Colombian national, or have Colombian children, the term of continuous domicile will be reduced to 2 years. There are also advantages available to migrants who are Latin Americans and the Caribbean.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia provides a pathway to citizenship for humanitarian migrants via accumulation of time with a residence visa in Law 43 of 1993, article 5. Additionally, National Decree 216/2021, article 11, in Colombia grants Venezuelan migrants the opportunity to accumulate time with the Special Protection Permit to apply to a Residency visa.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National Law 43/1993 article 5 in Colombia explains when migrants can get the Colombian citizenship. National Resolution 5477/2022 in Colombia, articles 3 and 90 specifies when family migrants can apply to a residency visa.

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: Yes, all migrants can obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin.
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Colombia allows migrants to obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin in Law 43 of 1993, article 14.

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to regularize after certain conditions are met
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right in Resolution 2357 of 2020, article 16. Furthermore, in relation to the case of Venezuelan migrants, National Decree 216/2021 and Resolution 971/2021 in Colombia create and implement the Permiso por Protección Especial (Special Protection Permit).


## Opinion and expression

### q83 · Right to freedom to hold opinion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 13, 20 and 100. In the Colombian Constitution's article 100, it is mentioned that “foreigners in Colombia will enjoy the same civil rights that are granted to Colombians.” Therefore, they have equal rights as the national citizens to enjoy their freedom as it is stressed on articles 20 of the Constitution.

### q85 · Right to freedom of expression.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 13, 20 and 100. Pursuant to the Constitution of Colombia, article 20, “every person is guaranteed the freedom to express and disseminate their thoughts and opinions, to inform and receive truthful and impartial information... There will be no censorship.” Likewise, pursuant to article 13, “all people are born free and equal before the law, they will receive the same protection and treatment by the authorities, and they will enjoy the same rights, liberties and opportunities without any discrimination for reasons of sex, race, national or family origin, language, religion, opinion political or philosophical. ## Peaceful assembly and association

### q87 · Right to freedom of association.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 38, 39, 13 and 100. National Labour Code, articles 353 and 354 in Colombia protect freedom of association. Pursuant to the Constitution of Colombia, article 38 “the right of free association is guaranteed for the development of the different activities that people carry out in society. “ Likewise, pursuant to article 39 “workers and employers have the right to form unions or associations, without the state's intervention. Its legal recognition will take place with the simple registration of the constitution act.” Articles 13 and 100 extend this protection to foreigners without discrimination.

### q89 · Right to freedom of assembly.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 37, 13 and 100. Pursuant to the Constitution of Colombia, article 37 “everybody can meet and demonstrate publicly and peacefully. Only the law may expressly establish the cases in which the exercise of this right may be limited.” Articles 13 and 100 extend this right to foreigners without discrimination. Article 100 mentions that “foreigners in Colombia will enjoy the same civil rights that are granted to Colombians."


## Thought, conscience, and religion or belief

### q81 · Right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Sometimes

National law in Colombia protects this right in the Constitution of Colombia, articles 13, 18, 19, 20, and 100. Pursuant to article 13, “all people are born free and equal before the law, they will receive the same protection and treatment by the authorities, and they will enjoy the same rights, liberties and opportunities without any discrimination for reasons of sex, race, national or family origin, language, religion, opinion political or philosophical. The State will promote the conditions for equality to be real and effective and will adopt measures in favor of discriminated or marginalized groups.”


## Victims of crime

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right for all categories of migrants in: - The Criminal Procedure Code, article 4. - The Constitution of Colombia, articles 29, 100, 13. Additionally, National Law 906/2004 in Colombia, article 11 grants every victim access to the judicial system.

### q45 · Right of victims of crime to immigration status or relief from expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National Decree 1067/2015 and Resolution 2357/2020 are the national norms that establish when the Autoridad Administrativa Especial Migración Colombia (the national authority in charge of sanctioning foreigners) applies an administrative sanction such as a deportation or expulsion. Both norms explicitly stress the exoneration circumstances (Decree 1067/2015: article 2.2.1.13.2 and Resolution 2357/2020: article 23) that may relief an expulsion. Being a victim of crime is not included among these circumstances.


## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right with articles 5, 13, 44 of its Constitution. This right is also protected by the following legislation: National, Law 12/ 991; National Law 1098/ 2006; National Law 2136/2021; Integral Migratory Policy (Law 2136 of 2021), article 3, 4. Decree 216 of 2021, article 12, paragraph 1. Resolution 971 of 2021, articles 25-35. Resolution 2357 of 2020, article 11, 16. Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 93.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes

National Law in Colombia protect this right in Law 360 of 1997; Law 906 of 2004; Law 1146 of 2007; Law 1257 of 2008 and Law 1719 of 2014; National Law 985/2005; Integral Migratory Policy (Law 2136 of 2021) article 2; Decree 1069/2014; Decree 1066/2015. National judicial decision in Colombia protects the rights in Decision T-075/19 and Decision T-298/19. Additionally, the conditions to activate the legal mechanism known as “tutela" is very accessible for any citizen or foreign person who lives inside the Colombian territory. In recent years, it has become a fundamental rights mechanism for helping the migrant population, especially Venezuelan people such as women who remain in the territory with an irregular status, to assert rights such as due process, family union or granting their proper access to the medical system. As an example, the jurisprudential precedent (decisions: T-074/ 2019] and T-298/2019) grant migrant and refugee women who have an irregular status in Colombia to get medical assistance. Specially, it has granted pregnant mothers provisional measures to have the chance to carry out prenatal check-ups, required medical exams, and any other health care related to their pregnancy period.

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Colombia protects this right in Resolution 5477 of 2022, article 93 and National Law 2136/2021. Individuals with disabilities may apply for a beneficiary visa of a main visa holder, regarless of the age.
