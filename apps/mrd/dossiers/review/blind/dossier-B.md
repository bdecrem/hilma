# Dossier B — Jordan


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Article (16) of the Jordanian Constitution for the year 1952 specifies that political rights such as the right of assembly and the right to form political parties is a right that is granted to Jordanians only: "(i) Jordanians shall have the right to hold meetings within the limits of the law. (ii) Jordanians are entitled to establish societies and political parties provided that the objects of such societies and parties are lawful, their methods peaceful, and their by-laws not contrary to the provisions of the Constitution. (iii) The establishment of societies and political parties and the control of their resources shall be regulated by law."

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan provides migrants with the right of equal protection of the law before administrative, civil, and criminal courts and tribunals, commensurate with citizens in Article 101 and 102 of the Jordanian Constitution for the year 1952. Article 101 states the following: "(i) The courts shall be open to all and shall be free from any interference in their affairs. (ii) The sittings of the courts shall be public unless the court considers that it should sit in camera in the interest of public order or morals." Article 102 states the following: "The Civil Courts in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan shall have jurisdiction over all persons in all matters, civil and criminal, including cases brought by or against the Government, except those matters in respect of which jurisdiction is vested in Religious or Special Courts in accordance with the provisions of the present Constitution or any other legislation in force." Further to this; national law in Jordan provides in the Code of Judicial Conduct for the year 2021 detailed provisions which oblige judges to treat all litigants equally and without discrimination on the bases of nationality, gender, religion in Article 6.

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Jordan grants migrants the right to interpretation at government expense in criminal proceedings in Article (277) of law number (9) for the year 1961 the Jordanian Code of Criminal Proceedings which states the following (unofficial translation): "1. If the defendant or the witnesses are unable to speak Arabic, the Chief of the Court will assign an interpreter whose age is above 18 and will be sworn to interpret with integrity and honesty. 2.All procedures done without compliance to this Article shall be null."

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in Jordan does not protect migrants from civil or criminal sanctions. Article (153) bis of Law number (16) for the year 1961 (the Jordanian Criminal Code) criminalizes the unlawful entering of the country and puts a penalty on it. Said Article states the following (unofficial translation): "Notwithstanding what is stated in any other law: 1- Anyone who illegally enters or exits the Kingdom shall be punished by imprisonment for a period of no less than three months, and whoever helps him to do so shall be punished with the same penalty. 2- The person’s penalty shall not be less than two years imprisonment if he is one of the carriers or employers in the border posts, whether they are employed by the public or private sector."


## Education

### q127 · Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Public adult education institutions are not freely accessed by Jordanians. Access to these institutions for migrants is allowed. However, it is limited by a certain quota and the fees payable by migrants in such institutions are notably higher than those payable by Jordanians.

## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

### q47 · Right to maintain immigration status after convictions.
- Law: Only major convictions can lead to loss of residence status.
- Implementation: Sometimes; Rarely

National law in Jordan regulates the ability of migrants' criminal and other convictions affecting residence statues in several legal provisions, whereby only major criminal convictions may lead to the migrant losing its statue. These include: Article (19) of the Regulation on the Residence, Employment and Workers' Regulations in Development Zones and Free Zones number 80 for the year 2016 which provides for loss of status following conviction by a competent court of a peremptory sentence of a misdemeanour against honour, honesty, public morality or a felony of any kind. Additionally, Articles (5) and 29 of Visa instructions to enter, work and stay in the Aqaba Special Economic Zone number 28 for the year 2002 specifies similar grounds.

### q49 · Right to not return to a risk of torture or inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan bans refoulment on the basis of a risk of return to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment in Article 3 of the Convention against Torture and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment. Jordan ratified the convention and transferred it into its national law through publication in issue (4764) of the official gazette on page (2246) on 15/06/2006; by decision number (1) for the year 2020 issued on 11/05/2020 the Constitutinal Court stipulated that under the Jordanian Constitituon international conventions and agreements take precedence over conflicting national legal provisions. In addition, the MOU signed on 16/09/1997 between the Jordanian government and the UNHCR on 16/09/1997 obligates Jordan to not return refugees who fear persecution.

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in Jordan bans refoulment on the basis of a risk of return to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment on the basis of a risk of return to persecution on the basis of race, relgiion, nationality, memebr of a particular social group, or political opinion under the MOU signed on 16/09/1997 between the Jordanian government and the UNHCR on 16/09/1997 obligates Jordan to not return refugees who fear persecution; by decision number (1) for the year 2020 issued on 11/05/2020 the Constitutinal Court stipulated that under the Jordanian Constitituon international conventions and agreements take precedence over conflicting national legal provisions.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Although Jordan is not a signatory to the 1951 Geneva Refugee Convention, Jordan is bound to the principle of non refoulment in accordance with the Memoranda of Understanding signed on 16/09/1997 between the Jordanian government and the UNHCR on 16/09/1997, which describes the right of non-refoulement in relation to fear of persecution and not in relation to the broader protection derived from the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in Jordan does not require a person to be advised of the possible availability of asylum before being expelled or prevented entry.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.

### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.

- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Article (37) of the Residency and Foreign Affairs Law number 24 for the year 1973 grants the Minister of Interior wide discretion to expel any foreigner without having to justify such expulsion.

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.

### q77 · Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Same sex marital relationships are banned in Jordan in pursuance with Article (279) of the Jordanian Penal Code number 16 for the year 1960 which states that: "Anyone who conducts marriage decrees or is a party to such decrees in a manner inconsistent with the provisions of the Personal Status Code or any other legislation in force shall be liable to one to six months' imprisonment." The law of Personal Statues Code does not specify any provision against same sex marriage. However, the Personal Statues Code in Jordan is based on Sharia Law. Additionally, Article (2) of the Jordanian Constitution for the year 1952 states that the State is an Islamic state and same sex marriage accordingly goes against the public policy of the Jordanian law.

### q137 · Right to citizenship for stateless migrants.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Although, granting migrants or migrants children citizenship if they would otherwise be stateless is not stipulated under the Jordanian law, The Jordanian Nationality Law grants citizenship According to Article (3) for a child whose father is stateless and whose mother is Jordanian (the law does not generally grant children of a Jordanian mother citizenship and the citizenship is transferred according to law as a general and original rule from the Jordanian father rather than the Jordanian mother). Additionally, in the case of a child whose both parents unknown and was found in Jordan, Article (3) of the Jordanian Nationality law grants this child immediate citizenship.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants).
- Implementation: Sometimes; Effectively Always

National law in Jordan guarantees access to free primary education to migrant children commensurate with citizens in Article 10 (a) of the Jordanian Education Law number 47 for the year of 1994 which stipulates that primary education is mandatory and free in all public schools. These protections also apply to refugees, but not irregular migrants, since migrants require identification documents to enroll in school.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

Although there is no formal requirement that migrants be paid equivalent to citizens, the Tripartite Committee on Labour affairs has raised the minimum wage of migrant workers to a level commensurate with citizens. This committee is formed in accordance with Article 52 of Jordanian Labor Law No. 8 of 1996. The decision states the following: "The minimum wage for Jordanians shall be 260 JOD per month starting from 01/01/2021.The minimum wage for non-Jordanian workers shall be 230 JOD per month starting from 01/01/2021. This minimum wage shall be increased over a period of two years starting from 01/01/2022 to become equivalent to the minimum wage set out for Jordanian citizens in the above-mentioned paragraph during 2022-2023." This process was completed in May of 2023.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Employment is tied to specific employer; change of employer possible, but requires new work permit application.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan protects this right, in a limited way, in Article 12 (b/2/a) which states: "it is not permitted for a migrant worker to change from an employer to another before working for said employer for a duration of six months." Migrants' employment is tied to specific sectors. In addition, the new employer has a legal obligation to issue a work permit for the migrant workers and provide a monetary bond before the Ministry of Labour pursuant to the issued Instructions and procedures for the employment and recruitment of non-Jordanian workers and amendments thereto for the year 2012.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment implies loss of immigration status unless a new job is found within a specified time period.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan protects the right of the migrant worker not to lose immediately his work permit upon losing his employment in Article 11 (d) of the Instructions and procedures for the employment and recruitment of non-Jordanian workers and amendments thereto for the year 2012.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections.
- Implementation: Usually; Rarely

National law in Jordan grants migrant workers the same rights to equal employment conditions and protections such as overtime, hours of work, weekly rest, paid holidays, sick pay, health and safety at work, protection against dismissal commensurate with that afforded to citizens in Article (2) of the Jordanian Labour Law number 8 for the year 1996 defines an employee without differentiating between Jordanian and non- Jordanian citizens, as it states in its relevant part the following: "Any person, whether male or female who works in exchange for wage under the Employers' command including juveniles and persons under probation or training". Refugees who are authorized to work, are entitled to the same conditions as migrant workers. For irregular workers, there are no provisions that state whether they are protected one way or another. Judicial cases may apply the same rights to both regular and irregular workers when a valid contract is brought before the courts.

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Access to some, but not all types of social security protections (e.g., no equal access under this program; or access depending on visa type).
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan protects this right for some, but not all migrant workers in Article 4 of the Jordanian Social Security Law number 1 for the year 2014. In addition, Article 11/a of the Instructions and procedures for the employment and recruitment of non-Jordanian workers and amendments thereto for the year 2012 further protects this rights by guaranteeing that the migrant worker obtains his entitlements from the Social Security Department before leaving the Country. However, domestic worker are excluded from social security protections.

### q111 · Right of equal access to unemployment benefits.
- Law: No access to any unemployment benefits.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q113 · Right of temporary migrants to become lawfully settled.

- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The concept of permanent residency is not implemented under the Jordanian law and only temporary residency may be granted subject to the requirements under the Jordanian Residency and Foreigners Affairs law number 24 for the year 1973.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, but limited
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in Jordan protects migrant workers right to redress if the terms of their employment contract have been violated by their employer in accordance with the Jordanian Labour law number 8 for the year 1996 on the basis of equality with Jordanian citizens. In addition, refugees with authorization to work have the same rights as other migrant workers. Irregular workers are not explicitly protected by statutory law, but the courts have recognized some employment rights of irregular workers, assuming a valid complaint has been brought before the court, for example by penalizing employers who employed irregular migrants.

## Legal personhood

### q27 · Right not to have identity documents confiscated.
- Law: Yes, right implied by existing laws (e.g., constitutional laws).
- Implementation: Usually

This right in implied by law, in so far as only officials duly authorized by law have the right to confiscate the identity documents of another person, including a migrant, and the criminal law prohibits impersonation of such an official in Article 202(1), which states that, "Whoever commits one of the following acts shall be punished by imprisonment from one month to one year: a. Impersonates an officer in the public civil or military service at an event at which said officer was to attend, as part of his / her duty, or was to perform a task at such event, or; b. Pretends without the right to do so that he/she is an official in the military or civil public service and claims that he/she has the right to do 38 any of the actions or report to a places in order to carry out any function according to his/her post 2. The perpetrator shall be punished by imprisonment from three months to two years if he/she committed one of the acts stipulated in the previous two paragraphs while wearing a public official uniform or badge."

### q29 · Right to birth registration.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Accoarding to the requirments for birth registration, as published by the Civil Status Department, a child can only be registered if his parents are legally resident in Jordan. Therefore, if the parents are not documented there is no requirment to register their children. For refugees, the situation is different, though, and it should be noted that the Jordanian government has implemented active procedures to ensure that child refugees (in particular Syrian refugees children) have birth registration certificates, including establishing mobile civil registration services in all Syrian refugees camps.

### q31 · Right to government document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Jordanian law provides residence permits, but not identity documents, in Residence and Foreigners Affairs Law number 24 for the year 1973. Article (18) of said law states that: "Any foreigner who resides or wishes to remain in the country shall have a residence permit in accordance with the provisions of this Law and shall leave the territory of the Kingdom at the expiration of the period of residence permit unless it has been renewed."

### q33 · Right against destruction of document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

Although Jordan is signatory to the International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families, Jordan did not ratify and transfer the convention into national law, and accordingly there exists no legal provision in Jordanian law that expressly tackles this issue.


## Liberty

### q15 · Right to communicate with consular officials.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan entails an obligation to allow consular officials and migrants (who are nationals of the consular sending state) to communicate in Article (36) of the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations 1963 published in issue number (4910) of the official gazette on page number 2076 on 01/06/2008.

### q17 · Right of child migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan prohibits the detention of children on the basis of their status as a migrant in Article (37/b) of the Convention on the rights of child published in issue number (4787) of the official gazette on page number 3991on 16/10/2006.

### q19 · Right of migrants to be free from detention on the basis of migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Article (37) of the law on residency and foreign affairs number 24 for the year 1973 grants the Minister of Interior wide powers to deport any foreigner without setting any criteria for this decision by stating: "The Minister shall have the right to deport foreigners and may order the temporary arrest of those who are to be deported until deportation proceedings are completed. The alien previously deported shall not be allowed to return to the territory of the Kingdom except with the Minister's special permission."

### q21 · Right to appeal legality, conditions, and length of detention
- Law: Yes (legality)
- Implementation: Rarely

National law in Jordan allows migrants to appeal legality, but not conditions, or length of detention in Article 5 (a) of law number 27 for the year 2014 on Administrative Judiciary Law, which states the following: "The Administrative Court shall have exclusive jurisdiction to hear all appeals concerning final administrative decisions." There is only one Administrative Court in Jordan and it is located in Amman (capital city); in addition, the appellant needs to be represented before the Court by a licensed lawyer of not less than 5 years post qualification experience.

### q23 · Right to an individualized detention decision.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

Although national law in Jordan does not tackle the issue of individualized detention, the Jordanian law sets general principles that show that this right has foundation in the law. In Article 9 (a) of the International Covenant on Civil and political rights publishes in issue number (4764) of the official gazette on page (2227) on 15/06/2006 which obliges all signatory states to refrain from arbitrary detention.

### q25 · Right to be free from torture and inhumane treatment.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan protects this right in Article (1) and Article (16) of the convention against torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment published in issue number (4764) of the official gazette on page number 4764 on 15/06/2006.


## Life

### q9 · Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q11 · Right to be free from use of lethal force to prevent entry to territory.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in Jordan does regulate the use of force in relation to crowd control, Article 167(1) of the Penalty Code, which states that, "If people gathered according to the way described in Article 164 they shall be warned to disperse by one of the administrative authority representatives or the police chief or the region’s commander or any police officer through using a horn or a whistle or any other similar mean or through the use of flair gun. 2. If they continued to crowd with the intent to riot after being notified to disperse using the means described in the pervious paragraph; or after being ordered to disperse; or if they used force to resist dispersion, then any of the offices mentioned in the previous paragraph in addition to the police and or any person who helps them, has the power to take any needed measures to disperse the crowd or to arrest anyone of them. If any of the gathered persons showed any resistance, then any of the officials previously mentioned has the right to use necessary force within the limits of logic to subdue them." To the extent that the use of force against migrants at the border would involve the military, the content of the regulation of military use of force, including any rules of engagement, is deemed a state secret. Article 3 (b) of the Protection of State Secrets and Documents Code (1971) states that, "B. Plans and details of military operations, public security measures, general intelligence, or any plan related in general to military operations, or internal security measures, whether economic, catering, construction, or transportation."

### q13 · Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.
- Law: No, government may punish trafficked and smuggled migrants.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right but Article 12 of the Law #9 of the Jordanian Human Trafficking Prevention Law and its amendments (2009) provide a discretion to stop the prosecution of victims of human trafficking. Additionally, there is a human trafficking victims assistance fund and a technical committee to prevent human trafficking as outlined in Regulation No. (6) of 2023 - Regulation of the Human Trafficking Victims Assistance Fund) published on the official gazzete issue (5840) on 11/01/2023.


## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Only major convictions (e.g. criminal offenses) can lead to loss of citizenship status.
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan provides that convictions for major criminal convictions lead to a loss of the citizenship status of naturalized migrants. There are no specific legal previsions addressing the naturalization of migrants in Jordan, and therefore, the general provisions under the Jordanian Citizenship Law number 6 for the year 1954 apply to the naturalization of migrants. Subsequently, Article (19) of the Jordanian Citizenship Law states that upon naturalization, citizenship can only be withdrawn in two cases; conducting or attempting to conduct an action that is considered to be dangerous to the safety and security of the state, and if the information on which the receipt of citizenship turned out to be false or forged.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Even if the migrant meets all the criterion set by the Jordanian Nationality law to apply for citizenship, acquiring the citizenship is not a right to the migrant as the power to decide on granting the citizenship to anyone is upon complete discretionary power the Jordanian Cabinet according to Article (13) of said law.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The provisions in the Jordanian Nationality law number 6 for the year 1954 do not provide a right to a pathway for humanitarian migrants to become citizens. However, if a humanitarian migrant meets the criterion set out by Article 12 of the Jordanian Nationality law (a. have been a resident in Jordan for at least 4 years prior to the application; b. no history of criminal history for crimes of honor and/or morality; c. have the intention of residing in Jordan; d. have the knowledge of Arabic language reading and writting; e. have a good reputation; f. must be of sound mind; g. have a legitimate means of earning), they can apply for citizenship, however, even if they meet all these criteria the power to confer citizenship is at the complete discretionary of the Jordanian Cabinet under to Article 13.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: No, no migrants may obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in Jordan does not allow migrants to obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin in accordance with Article 13 (3) of the Jordanian Nationality Law number 6 for the year 1954, which states in its relevant part the following: "3. A Jordanian citizenship certificate shall not be granted to any person unless he or she loses the citizenship of which he or she was a member on the date of his or her naturalization..."

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: Yes, there is an opportunity to regularize after certain conditions are met.
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

The right of irregular migrants to regularize is protected only for migrants who become irregular by overstaying after an initial period of regular status. This right is not protected for migrants who enter without authorization. National law in Jordan provides a mechanism for an irregular migrant worker to regularize his stay in Jordan in Article (34) of the Jordanian Residency and Foreigners Affairs Law number 24 for the year 1973, which states the following: "a. Any foreigner who has entered the Kingdom legally and who has not obtained temporary residence or exceeded the period of such residence granted to him, or who has not applied for renewal of his annual residence permit within one month of its expiration, shall be fined in the amount of 45 dinars for each month of the excess or part of the month in the amount of 1/2 dinars for each day of that part. b. The Minister shall grant the Secretary-General of the Ministry an exemption from these fines if they do not exceed two hundred and fifty dinars. If they exceed this amount, the exemption shall be granted by decision of the Prime Minister upon the Minister's placement".


## Opinion and expression

### q83 · Right to freedom to hold opinion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan provides migrants with full freedom to hold opinions without interference commensurate with that afforded to citizens in Article (26) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which was ratified and published in issue number (4764) of the official gazette, page number 15/06/2006. Additionally, Article 15 of the Constitution of Jordan states that, "(i) The State shall guarantee freedom of opinion. Every Jordanian shall be free to express his opinion by speech, in writing, or by means of photographic representation and other forms of expression, provided that such does not violate the law." Though the provision explicitly mentions "every Jordanian", it has been interpreted to include all residents of Jordan, including migrants.

## Thought, conscience, and religion or belief

### q81 · Right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in Jordan recognizes and protects the right of freedom of thought, conscience and religion or belief of migrants commensurate with citizens In Article (14) of the Jordanian Constitution for the year 1954 whereby the Article does not make any distinction between Jordanians and non-Jordanians and it applies to all persons in the Country, whereby Article (14) of the Constitution states that: "The State shall safeguard the free exercise of all forms of worship and religious rights in accordance with the customs observed in the Kingdom, unless such is inconsistent with public order or morality"


## Victims of crime

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always

National law in Jordan provides migrant victims of crime with the right of access to criminal proceedings commensurate with citizens and they are allowed to serve as complainants in Article (101) of the Jordanian Constitution for the year 1952 which states the following (unofficial translation): "The courts shall be open to all and shall be free from any interference in their affairs. The sittings of the courts shall be public unless the court considers that it should sit in camera in the interest of public order or morals."

### q45 · Right of victims of crime to immigration status or relief from expulsion.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

The national law in Jordan provides migrant victims of trafficking relief from expulsion as a result of being a victim of a crime in Articles (7, 12) of the law on the Prevention of Human Trafficking number 9 for the year 2009, whereby Article (7) of said law specifies the following: "The Council of Ministers shall, on the basis of placement of the Committee, establish or accredit one or more homes to accommodate victims and persons affected by human trafficking offenses, specifying the grounds for entry into and exit from them, the physical, psychological and social recovery programs offered to its inmates, the manner in which they are administered and the conditions for its employees under a system issued to that end." Article (12) of said law specifies that: "a. Where possible, the competent authorities shall ensure the victim's protection, psychological, physical and moral integrity and shall endeavour to create conditions conducive to the victim's health, psychological, educational and social welfare, rehabilitation and reintegration in a manner that respects his or her humanity and facilitates his or her safe and expeditious return to his or her home country, taking into account the best interests of the child. b. Where possible, the competent authorities shall guarantee the victim the following rights: 1- To provide him with an opportunity at all stages of evidence, investigation or trial to indicate, identify, classify and determine his identity, nationality and age in order to ensure that the perpetrators' hands are removed from him. 2- Temporary residence in the Kingdom until the necessary investigative and trial procedures have been completed. 3- Obtaining the necessary legal assistance. 4- Provide appropriate translation. 5- Provide legitimate means of obtaining fair compensation for material and moral damage in accordance with the law's provisions. 6- The confidentiality of information relevant to the crime of trafficking in human beings shall be bound within the limits of the law. 7- To provide suitable accommodation for him and his relatives. 8- Provide the necessary security protection to him and his relatives so as to ensure that they do not affect him physically or morally whenever necessary. 9- To ensure contact with one's relatives if they find it or the embassy of the State of which they are nationals". In addition to the above, the government found a shelter for human trafficking crimes victims in accordance with the Regulation of shelters for victims of human trafficking crimes and those who are affected by the crimes for the year 2012, whereby Article (4) of said regulation determines its goals in the following: "The House aims to achieve the following: a. To ensure the victim's protection and temporary accommodation until his or her problem is resolved or returned to his or her country of origin or any country of choice and consent to his or her receipt. b. Provide the necessary social care, living, psychological, health, extension, cultural and legal services for the victim or the victim. c. Build a victims' and affected information base."


## Vulnerable migrants

### q3 · Right of child migrants to special protections.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The legislation on child rights number 17 (2022) does not specify in its provisions that the scope of protection is limited to Jordanian children. It generally defines a child as any individual under the age of 18. There is no specific provision on protection measurements for migrant children.

### q5 · Right of women migrants to special protections.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Although migrant women are protected under the Jordanian law with general protection applicable to women, there is no direct legal provision that provides migrant women who are vulnerable special protections in comparison to other migrants.

### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Jordan does protect the rights of people with disabilities under law number 20 of 2017, but there is no legal provision specific for the protection and special measures in regard to migrants with disabilities.
