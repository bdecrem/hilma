# Dossier I — India


## Civil and political life

### q91 · Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.
- Law: No right to vote in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Only citizens may vote under the Constitution of India, Article 326.

### q93 · Right of regular migrants to stand for election.
- Law: No right to run for public office in any election
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Only citizens over 18 years old may run for publiuc office under Constitution of India, Article 58, 66, 124, 217 and Representation of the People Act, 1951, Part I(2)(e).


## Due process

### q35 · Right to equal protection of the law before courts.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually

National law in India protects this right in Article 14 of the Constitution of India, which states that: "The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India."

### q37 · Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right for all migrants. Legal aid to the accused can be allowed only under certain cases in the Criminal Procedure Code, Clause no. 304. These exceptions are not specifically aimed migrant, but the deprived sections of the population.

### q39 · Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in India protects this right in the Criminal Procedure Code, Clause no. 277-82, 333, which mandates that the state must provide an interpretor for interpretation of any evidence or statement.

### q41 · Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in India enforces authorised entry through the Passport (Entry into India) Act, 1920, Clause 4(1),4(2): Power to arrest, and Foreigners Act, 1946, No. 31 of 1946, Article 3(2)(g). According to these provisions, any unauthorized entry can lead to to civil or criminal sanctions.

## Equal protection

### q131 · Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.

## Expulsion, asylum, and non-refoulement

- Implementation: Does Not Apply

### q51 · Right to not return to a risk of persecution.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. There is no ban on refoulement. India is neither party to the 1951 Convention on Refugees nor the 1967 Protocol. The lack of specific refugee legislation in India has led the government to adopt an ad hoc approach to different refugee influxes. The status of refugees in India is governed mainly by political and administrative decisions, rather than any codified model of conduct.

### q53 · Right to not return to a deprivations of fundamental human rights.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. There is no ban on refoulement, but in practice such protection is de facto provided under the "Right to Life" provided by the Constitution of India. India is neither party to the 1951 Convention on Refugees nor the 1967 Protocol. The lack of specific refugee legislation in India has led the government to adopt an ad hoc approach to different refugee influxes. The status of refugees in India is governed mainly by political and administrative decisions, rather than any codified model of conduct. In a decision by the Supreme Court of India on 8 April 2021 in Mohammad Salimullah and Anr v. Union of India and Ors., the court refused to grant interim relief in a petition seeking the temporary release of Rohingya refugees from detention in the State of Jammu and Kashmir and their pending deportation from India to Myanmar. In this judgment, the Supreme Court clarifies that India is not a signatory to the United Nations 1951 Refugee Convention and Protocol, indicating that this is sufficient for the non-compliance of the non-refoulement policy.

### q55 · Right to be advised of availability of asylum before expulsion.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. There is no obligation under Foreigners Act, 1946.

### q57 · Right to be protected from chain refoulement.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The Foreigners Act, 1946 has no protections against expulsion on the grounds that a migrant is likely to be subjected to refoulement through a subsequent expulsion. India is neither party to the 1951 Convention on Refugees nor the 1967 Protocol. The lack of specific refugee legislation in India has led the government to adopt an ad hoc approach to different refugee influxes. ### q59 · Right to protection against expulsion except following a legal judgment.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. National law in India allows the Central Government to order the departure of an individual or group of foreigners in the Foreigners Act, 1946, Article 3(2)(C). In practice, these determinations are justified by facts relevant to the individual concerned and pursuant to the powers granted to the state. This is not formally required in all cases.

### q61 · Right to submit reasons against expulsion and have case reviewed before deportation.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. There is a specific law for the expulsion of immigrants from Assam, i.e., The Immigrants (Expulsion from Assam) Act, 1950. However, there is no national law providing migrants with an opportunity to submit the reasons against expulsion and to have their case reviewed by the competent authority before being expelled.

## Health

### q119 · Right of access to health care services.
- Law: Yes, for all visa types (including irregular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in India protects this right in the Constitution of India, Article 21, as interpreted in Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (AIR 1984 SC 802). Article 21 of the Constitution guarantees protection of life and personal liberty to all persons. In Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (AIR 1984 SC 802), the Supreme Court has held that the right to live with human dignity, enshrined in Article 21, derives from the directive principles of state policy and therefore includes protection of health.

### q123 · Right of access to public housing programs.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. The Central Government and various Indian States have enacted several schemes ensuring adequate housing for low and middle income strata of the populations, such as the National Urban Housing & Habitat Policy, the Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal and the Indira Aawas Yojana. However, access to public housing does not extend to migrants.

### q125 · Right to equal access to free primary education for migrant children.
- Law: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants)
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in India protects this right for only regular migrants in the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009, which provides for free and compulsory elementary education of equitable quality to all children of 6-14 years age. This right does not apply to irregular migrants.


## Labor

### q95 · Right to equal pay for the same work.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. India's Equal Remuneration Act, 1976, does not address wage discrimination on the basis of migrant status and applies only to preventing gender based wage discrimination.

### q97 · Right to freedom of choice of employment.
- Law: Employment is tied to a specific employer and no change of employer possible
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. According to the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) regulations, "the applicant [migrant] is not permitted to change employer for the entire duration of the employment visa." However, the MHA has clarified that this excludes a change of employers from a registered holding company to its subsidiary or vice versa, or between subsidiaries of a registered holding company.

### q99 · Right of spouse/partner of principal migrant to work.

- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Each individual must apply separately,

### q101 · Right to maintain immigration status after loss of employment.
- Law: Loss of employment automatically implies the loss of immigration status
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Employment visas are subject to annual renewal conditional on producing necessary documents in support of continued employment.

### q103 · Right to equal employment conditions and protection.
- Law: Yes, migrants have a right to the same employment conditions and protections
- Implementation: Sometimes

Constitution of India, Article 14, Equality before law and equal protection of laws applies to both citizens and foreigners, making foreigners (migrant workers) in India eligible for equal protection under Indian labour laws. Furthermore, India is a signatory of the International Labor Organization's (ILO) Equal Remuneration Act, 1979, page 8, Hours of Work (Industry) Convention, 1919, No. 1, Forced Labour Convention, 1930, No. 16 and Prevention of Major Industrial Accidents, Convention No. 174, and international treaties have been incorporated into Indian domestic law by the Constitution of India, 1949, Article 51(c) which states that to: “foster respect for international law and treaty obligations in the dealings of organised peoples with one another; and encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration," and the decision of Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan, AIR 1997 SC 3011, which holds that “it is now an accepted rule of judicial construction that regard must be given to international conventions and norms for construing domestic law where there is no inconsistency between them and there is a void in the domestic law."

### q109 · Right to equal access to social security protections.
- Law: Access to some, but not all types of social security protections (e.g., no equal access under this program; or access depending on visa type)
- Implementation: Usually

National law in India protects this right in the Employees Provident Fund (EPF) (Third Amendment) Scheme, 2008. Every foreign worker employed with an establishment to whom the EPF applies must become a member of the provident fund (PF) from the first date of his/her employment. There is no minimum period of stay in India for activation of PF compliance. However, Public Sector Social Security Schemes are not applicable in cases of non-citizens.

- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q115 · Right of temporary migrants to settle regardless of initial status.
- Law: NA
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

There is no established official procedures for foreign nationals of non-Indian descent to acquire Indian citizenship or permanent residence. India bestows citizenship in exceptional circumstances, subject to meticulous individual evaluations. The sole avenue for a foreigner to attain permanent residency in India is through investment-based pathways or thtrough naturalization to citizenship.

### q117 · Right to redress following employer breach of employment contract.
- Law: Yes, same as citizens
- Implementation: Sometimes

National law in India protects this right in 1) the Indian Contract Act, 1872. 2) Article 14 of the Constitution of India, which prevents the State from denying to any person the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India. Under Indian law, there are no formal legal requirements as to the form and content of employment agreements and general rules governing contracts as laid in the Indian Contract Act 1872 are applicable to breaches or violations of any employment agreement. 3) Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Nirmal Kumar Gupta in the Court of Additional District Judge - 07, (Central District) TIS Hazari Courts, Delhi, 214/2016, 618236/2016. The plaintiff company was engaged in the business of providing satellite communication and broadcasting services including teleport services for TV Channels and OB Vans. The defendant was recruited as Engineer (Operation & Customer Support) vide appointment letter dated 01.07.2009 by accepting the terms and conditions of the Contract which restrained him from seeking alternative employment (as is the case with most migrant contract workers) for two years after leaving the job by giving one month's notice. The dendent resigned from the plaintiff company in 2014, and joined a competitor company. The plaintiff filed the case for compensation and restraint on the defendant. The defendant submitted that clauses no. 7 and 8(i) of the appointment letter dated 01.07.2009 violated Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, arguing that the said clauses were against the public policy and the same amounted to restrain of trade against the defendant and therefore, the same were void in terms of Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. The Court decided, in 2020, in favour of the defendant and against the plaintiff. Accordingly, the suit of the plaintiff was found not maintainable and hit by Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 and was dismissed.

## Legal personhood

### q27 · Right not to have identity documents confiscated.
- Law: Yes, right implied by existing laws (e.g., constitutional laws)
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in India protects this right in 1) Article 14 of the Constitution of India, which states that: "The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India." 2) Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976. The Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act has been interpreted by the judiciary to include indicators of bonded/forced labour, "Any factor which deprives a person of a choice of alternatives and compels him to adopt a particular course of action," in People's Union for Democratic Rights and Others vs Union of India and Others [1982 II LLJ 454 SC (1982) 3 SCC 235].

### q29 · Right to birth registration.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in India protects this right in Section 4(1) of Citizenship Act, 1955, which states that the birth of migrant children is required to be registered with a Foreign Regional Registration Officer (FRRO).

### q31 · Right to government document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: No
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. However, a foreign citizen can get an Aadhaar Card (UID or Unique Identity number) according to the Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939 , which is a government-issued document that serves as proof of identity and residence address in India, not work status. This is not mandatory, though. Foreign citizens, can apply for an Aadhaar Card if they are staying in India for a considerable period of time. Wok Permit or Work Visa is the document mandated by the government of India that recognises a foreigners work status in India.

### q33 · Right against destruction of document recognizing residency, work status, and identity.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in India protects this right in 1) Indian Penal Code (IPC). Passport and travel are property of Central Government. Destroying government property is a punishable offense under Section 425 of Indian Penal Code (IPC). 2) Clause 17, Passport Act, 1967. 3) Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976, which has been widely interpreted by the judiciary to include indicators of bonded/forced labour, including withholding/destroying identity documents by employers. 3) The decision in Bandhua Mukti Morcha (People’s Union for Democratic Rights) v. Union of India, A.I.R. 1982, S.C. 1473 (19 September 1982), which provided interpretation of the Bonded Labour System (Abolition Act, 1976). The issue before the Supreme Court was whether the forced labour provision of Article 23 of the Indian Constitution was applicable to a situation of workers being paid less than the minimum wage. The decision states, ”In the ordinary course ...where he is required to supply forced labour for no wage or for nominal wage, unless he has received some advance or other economic consideration from the employer, and unless, under the pretext of not having returned such advance or other economic consideration, he is either required to render service to the employer (and his subsequent generations of successors), or is deprived of his freedom of employment or of the right to move freely wherever he wants." The Court realized that it would be extremely onerous if every labourer had to prove that he had received an advance or other economic consideration from his employer. The labourer was likely to be illiterate and to have no documentary evidence of such an advance, and the employer was likely to deny ever having made the advance.

## Nationality

### q63 · Right to maintain citizenship status after convictions.
- Law: Only major convictions (e.g., criminal offenses) can lead to loss of citizenship status
- Implementation: Usually; Rarely

National law in India protects this right in Citizenship Act, 1955, Sections 5(1)(a) and 10(1), (2)(d). Citizens who are naturalized, or registered under Section(5)(1)(a)(persons of Indian origin), can be deprived of citizenship by Central Government under Section 10(2(d) order if (d) that citizen has, within five years after registration or naturalisation, been sentenced in any country to imprisonment for a term of not less than two years.

### q65 · Right of economic migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Sometimes; Usually

National law in India protects this right in the Citizenship Act, 1955, Clause 5 and 6, and Schedule III. Economic migrants have pathways to citizenship through Registration (Clause 5) and Naturalization (Clause 6). Residency requirements, under Schedule III(d) sets out a residence requirement of 12 years within the last 15 years; however, this residency requirement can be waived under special circumstances specified in Schedule III (c) and the proviso at the conclusion of Schedule III.

### q67 · Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Sometimes

National law in India protects this right in the Citizenship Act, 1955, Clause 5 and 6. Recently, the Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) 2019 has provided a pathway for religiously persecuted non-Muslim undocumented migrants from neighbouring countries.

### q69 · Right of family migrants to citizenship.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Effectively Always; Usually

National law in India protects this right in the Citizenship Act, 1955, Clause 5 and 6. Citizenship Act Amendment (CAA) 2019 benefits are extended to family migtants.

### q71 · Right to dual nationality.
- Law: No, no migrants may obtain dual nationality by retaining their nationality of origin
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right.

### q133 · Right of irregular migrants to regularize their status.
- Law: No, there is no opportunity for irregular migrant workers to regularize.
- Implementation: Does Not Apply

No protection of this right. Irregular migrants are excluded from both registration and acquisition of citizenship through birth, registration, or naturalization, however, the Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019, No. 47 of 2019, provides a pathway to regularization for any migrant belonging to the Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi or Christian communities from Afghanistan, Bangladesh or Pakistan, and who were present in India on or before December 31st, 2014.

### q43 · Right of victims of crime to access criminal proceedings.
- Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in India protects this right in Article 14 of the Indian Constitution, which protects the fundamental right to equality before law and equal protection of laws, and applies to both citizens and foreigners in India. - Law: Yes
- Implementation: Usually; Sometimes

National law in India protects this right in 1) National Commission for Women Act, 1990. ### q7 · Rights of migrants with disabilities to special protection.
