# A year of Takt: 20 levels (proposal, 2026-11-03)

Bart's goals for one year:

1. Move the hands correctly at all times.
2. Know the scales: C major, all the minors, and hop to the pentatonics easily.
3. Jam I IV V in any of those keys.
4. Improvise in those scales.
5. Jam blues tracks nicely.
6. Basic ear fluency ("no, that needs to go up a whole step"), for improvising and production.

**Reading of goal 2.** "All the minors" means the natural minor in all 12 keys, with the minor pentatonic and blues scale on each. Harmonic minor comes in only for the V7 of a minor key. Major scales are C, G and F, plus whatever the blues keys need.

**Pace.** 20 levels over 52 weeks is about 2½ weeks a level, at 20–30 minutes a day, five days a week.

**Hours.** Each level carries an estimate: the focused practice an adult beginner needs to pass its gate, the band playing. They add up to about 124 hours: 52 for the Delta, 72 for Memphis. That is 20 minutes a day, every day, or about 2½ hours a week.

- The coordination levels cost the most: hands together (7), two octaves hands together (12), all twelve minors (16), the boogie left hand (18), any key (19).
- Concept levels cost the least (1, 2, 5, 11).
- Treat each figure as ±50%. A level that runs past double its estimate is a sign to drop back a level, not to grind on.

**Shape.** The map's two episodes are the two halves of the year.

- **Delta (levels 1–10): the foundations.** The white-key minors, the C blues, and the first improvising.
- **Memphis (levels 11–20): fluency.** Every key, sevenths, the ear, and jamming any track.

Every level moves all the strands a little: one new thing per strand, never a jump in two at once. This is the graded syllabi's rule (RCM, ABRSM) and the one `misc/scales-and-chords-lessons.txt` is built on.

**The gate.** Each level has one gate, measured by MIDI where MIDI can see it. The gate is what clears the venue: this level's lesson plus this level's song test, not any lesson plus any song.

**What MIDI can't see.** Goal 1 (hands) can't be measured directly, because MIDI doesn't know which finger hit a key. The app can measure the proxies:

- Evenness: timing spread and velocity spread across a scale.
- No gap or bump at a thumb crossing.
- No wrong-note detours.
- Hands together without either hand drifting.

A bad fingering shows up as exactly those. The fingering itself is shown on screen and self-checked.

Marks below:

- **(built)** means a lesson in Takt already covers it.
- **(new)** means content still to make.

## Delta: the foundations (months 1–6)

### 1. Dockery Farms: home position and the form · ~3 h
- **Hands:** five-finger position, curved fingers, a level wrist, eyes on the screen and not the keys.
- **Chords:** roots on the 1 through the 12-bar in C (built, A1 "Roots on the 1").
- **Ear:** feel bar 5's move to IV before you see it.
- **Gate:** three choruses of roots, no missed change, at 80.

### 2. King Biscuit Time: the shuffle and three triads · ~4 h
- **Hands:** left-hand root–fifth shuffle (built, A1 "The shuffle" to "Hold it at 90").
- **Chords:** right-hand C, F and G in root position.
- **Ear:** name I, IV or V when the band plays it (new: an I/IV/V drill).
- **Gate:** the shuffle at 90, with the band.

### 3. Riverside Hotel: thumb under · ~5 h
- **Scales:** C major, one octave, hands separate, up and down (built, S1 1–3).
- **Hands:** the thumb passes under without a bump. Fingering: right hand 123 1234(5), left hand 54321 321.
- **Ear:** a half step or a whole step? (new; it's the core of goal 6).
- **Gate:** C major in eighths at 60, even (timing spread under 25 ms, no velocity jumps at the crossing).

### 4. Hopson Commissary: the relative minor · ~5 h
- **Scales:** A natural minor: same keys as C, new home (built, S1 "The relative minor").
- **Chords:** i iv v in A minor (Am Dm Em), then E7 for the v (the one borrowed note, G♯).
- **Ear:** major or minor chord? (built, Ear Trainer levels 1–2).
- **Gate:** A minor one octave each hand at 70, plus Am Dm E7 on the minor band.

### 5. Blue Front Cafe: the first notes of your own · ~4 h
- **Scales:** A minor pentatonic and the blues scale (built, S1m 6–7).
- **Improv:** call and response. The band plays two bars, you answer for two, with three notes (A C D), then five (new: an answer mode).
- **Ear:** up or down? Step or skip?
- **Gate:** eight answers, every note in the scale, and the rests kept (you don't play over the call).

### 6. Club Ebony: move less · ~5 h
- **Chords:** I IV V in C by the nearest inversion: C (root position), F/C (second inversion), G/B (first inversion). The hand barely moves (built, H "Inversions", "Moving less").
- **Hands:** change chords without looking; the wrist stays put.
- **Ear:** hear whether the top note of the chord moved.
- **Gate:** a chorus of inversion comping at 90, no gap before a change.

### 7. Po' Monkey's: hands together · ~8 h
- **Hands:** left-hand shuffle and right-hand chords at once, the big coordination milestone. Slow first, the band waits.
- **Blues:** C blues comping, the whole form.
- **Gate:** two choruses hands together at 80, neither hand drops a beat.

### 8. Red's Lounge: three more minors · ~6 h
- **Scales:** E, D and G minor, natural and pentatonic. They share A minor's fingering (built partly, S1m "E minor", "D minor").
- **Chords:** i iv v in each (built, M: E, D, G minor triads).
- **Ear:** intervals: minor third, fourth, fifth (built, Intervals game).
- **Gate:** all four minors one octave hands separate at 70, and i iv v in each on call.

### 9. Junior's Place: the blues vocabulary · ~6 h
- **Improv:** C minor pentatonic and blues scale over a C blues. The minor scale over major chords is the blues sound.
  - The ♭5 grace slide.
  - The ♭3 to 3 rub.
  - Three stock licks.
- **Blues:** a turnaround in C, an intro, an ending (new).
- **Gate:** a Stage song in C on Medium, plus a solo chorus with the turnaround on time.

### 10. Ground Zero: the Delta finale · ~6 h
- **Blues:** a full C blues: comp a chorus, solo a chorus, comp a chorus, hands together. The same in A minor blues.
- **Ear:** the blind form: the screen hides the chord, you follow the band by ear.
- **Gate:** Stage on Adaptive reaching the top level on one blues, plus a blind chorus with no missed change.

## Memphis: fluency (months 7–12)

### 11. Church Park: the hop · ~5 h
- **Scales:** C major pentatonic against C minor pentatonic. The trick: A minor pentatonic is the same five notes as C major pentatonic, so the hop is one shape and two homes (built, S1 "Major pentatonic").
- **Improv:** major pentatonic on the I, minor on the IV. Hear the color change.
- **Gate:** a solo chorus that switches scale on bar 5 and back on bar 7, every note in the scale it's meant to be in.

### 12. Pee Wee's Saloon: two octaves, hands together · ~8 h
- **Scales:** C major and A minor, two octaves, hands together at 80 (built, S1 "Two octaves", "The left hand"). The two thumbs cross at different moments; this is where hands learn to stop copying each other.
- **Gate:** both scales, two octaves, hands together, eighths at 80, hands within 20 ms of each other.

### 13. Daisy Theatre: the minors that break the pattern · ~7 h
- **Scales:** C, F and B♭ minor (fingering exceptions, black keys), natural and pentatonic.
- **Chords:** i iv v in each (built, M).
- **Ear:** whole step or half step inside a melody, not just two notes alone (new).
- **Gate:** the three, one octave hands together at 70.

### 14. The Peabody: sevenths · ~7 h
- **Chords:** the blues' real chords, I7 IV7 V7. Right hand plays shells (3 and 7), left hand the root (built, H "The shell"; new for dominant 7).
- **Blues:** blues in G and F, plus the quick change (IV in bar 2).
- **Gate:** a G blues with shells at 90, plus the quick change on time.

### 15. Handy Park: play it back · ~6 h
- **Ear:** the band plays a phrase of two to four notes in the pentatonic, you play it back (new: the ear drill that powers goal 6). It starts on a given note, then the starting note is yours to find.
- **Improv:** steal a phrase you just played back and answer with it.
- **Gate:** eight of ten phrases back right on the first try, in two keys.

### 16. The Palace: all twelve · ~9 h
- **Scales:** the rest: B, F♯, C♯, G♯/A♭, E♭ minor. The natural minor and minor pentatonic are now done in all 12 keys (built: M triads in all 12; new: scale checks in all 12).
- **Gate:** a random key called, play its minor scale and pentatonic one octave at 70, ten keys in a row.

### 17. Club Handy: improvising with intent · ~7 h
- **Improv:** land on a chord tone at the change (the 3rd of the IV at bar 5, the root at bar 11). Make a motif and repeat it, varied. Leave space.
- **Measured:**
  - The share of phrase endings on a chord tone.
  - Rests longer than a beat.
  - Repeats of a motif.
- **Gate:** two solo choruses, over half the phrase endings on chord tones, at least four real rests.

### 18. WDIA: the left hand on its own · ~9 h
- **Hands:** boogie bass in the left hand, steady eighths, against right-hand riffs and stabs (new). Plus a slow blues in 12/8.
- **Gate:** a chorus of boogie left hand at 100 with right-hand riffs, the left hand never drifts more than 30 ms.

### 19. Sun Studio: any key, now · ~8 h
- **Chords:** I IV V and i iv v in any key, on call within two bars. The blues in C, G, F, A and B♭. Club keys too: D minor, F minor, G minor.
- **Gate:** twelve random keys called, I IV V (or i iv v) in nearest inversions, each within two bars.

### 20. Royal Studios: jam · ~6 h
- **Blues:** any blues track in Takt, four choruses: comp, solo, solo, comp, in the right scale, with an ending.
- **Ear:** hear an eight-note melody, find it on the keys.
- **Production:** record your best chorus, export it as MIDI, keep it.
- **Gate:** the four choruses at Hard or the top of Adaptive, and the melody found in under three tries.

## What's built and what's new

**Built** covers roughly half of it:

- Play the Form (A1).
- Scales S1 and S1m.
- Minor Chords (M), all 12 triads.
- Chord Shapes (H): inversions, the shell, moving less.
- The Ear Trainer and Intervals.
- The Stage songs.

**New, in rough order of need:**

1. Half step or whole step, alone and in a melody (L3, L13).
2. Name I, IV, V by ear (L2).
3. The answer mode: call and response improvising, checked against the scale (L5).
4. Turnarounds, intros, endings (L9).
5. The blind form (L10).
6. Dominant 7 shells and the quick change (L14).
7. Play it back (L15).
8. Scale checks in all 12 minors and the random-key gate (L16, L19).
9. Chord-tone, rest and motif measures for solos (L17).
10. Boogie left hand and 12/8 slow blues (L18).
11. Blues bands in G, F, A, B♭ (L14, L19).

**Wiring.** The map's `levels` would point each venue at this plan's lesson. The clearing rule would become "this level's lesson + this level's gate", replacing "any lesson + any song".
