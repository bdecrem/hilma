# Stage 1: the brief

Before any design, write these five lines at the top of `design/notes.md`. Each line is one
sentence, specific to this page. If you cannot write a line, that is the question to ask.

1. **For whom:** who opens this page, and what they know already. ("Tenants of 410 Grand St, most
   on phones, many reading English as a second language.")
2. **The one job:** the single thing the page must get done. One verb. ("Get tenants to come to the
   meeting on March 12 at 7 pm.") A page with three jobs has none.
3. **The content that exists:** list the real material in the person's files: names, dates, places,
   numbers, quotes, tables, documents. Open the files and copy the facts; do not paraphrase numbers.
4. **What is missing:** a fact the page needs that no file holds (a time, a contact, a price).
5. **Where it is seen:** phone, laptop, printed, projected, sent as a link in a text message.

## Missing content

- Ask the person when a fact is essential and cannot be inferred (the date of the event, the price).
- Otherwise write real draft copy in the person's voice, mark it in the page with `data-draft`
  and say in the hand-off which lines are drafts. A draft is a real sentence about this subject,
  never filler text.
- Never invent numbers, quotes, names, logos or testimonials.

## Words

- Plain American English, short sentences, the reader's words. Headlines say the thing
  ("Rent strike meeting, Thursday March 12, 7 pm"), not a slogan ("Together we rise").
- Buttons say what happens ("Save my seat"), not "Submit" or "Learn more".
- Numbers keep their units and their source.
- No em dashes: a period, a comma or a colon instead. No exclamation marks.

## A worked example

> For whom: members of the Eastside Food Co-op, on phones, between errands.
> One job: get members to vote on the new hours before Friday.
> Content: hours.md (old and new hours, the reason, the vote link), turnout.csv (votes so far: 212 of 1,140).
> Missing: who to ask about the change. Draft: "Questions? Ask any cashier." marked data-draft.
> Seen: a link in the co-op's text message; almost all phones.
