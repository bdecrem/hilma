# Stage 2: find the idea

A designed page has one picture that comes from its subject. Everything else is set quietly around
it. Decoration (blobs, gradients, sparkles, stock icons) is what a page has when it has no idea.

## Ask these, in order, and write the answers

1. What is the single most important fact on this page? (A date, a number, a finding, a deadline.)
2. What shape does the material have? Pick one:
   - **a moment**: a date, a time, a place: the calendar leaf, the ticket, the poster
   - **a sequence**: steps, a history, an agenda: the timeline
   - **a ledger**: many rows of the same kind: the table, set beautifully
   - **a comparison**: before and after, us and them, two options: the side-by-side
   - **one number**: a count, a share, a total: the large numeral with its unit and source
   - **a place**: streets, regions, rooms: the map or the floor plan, drawn simply
   - **a document**: a report, a law, a letter: the page itself, with margins and a table of contents
   - **a voice**: what people said: the quote, set large, with who said it
3. What would a good newspaper's graphics desk, a museum label or a well-made government
   statistics page do with this exact material?
4. Which real thing from the content can be drawn in a few lines of SVG or set in type, so the
   picture explains instead of decorates? (The bars of the actual data. The building's address set
   like a street sign. The agenda as a ruled timetable.)

Write the idea as one sentence: "The page is a ___, because ___."

## Worked examples

| Subject | The idea | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| Tenants' meeting | A poster: the date and time as the largest type on the page, the building and room under it, the agenda as a ruled timetable | A hero with a stock photo of hands and "Join the movement" |
| Survey results | The finding as a sentence with its number large ("61% want later bus service"), then the comparison chart of the regions, then the table | Four rounded cards with icons and a pie chart |
| Volunteer dashboard | A ledger: this month beside last month, one row per measure, a small bar per row; the one number that matters set larger | A grid of glowing tiles with big gradients |
| A report site | The document: a narrow reading column, numbered findings, a table of contents in the margin on a laptop, sources at the foot of each section | A landing page with a slogan and a "Read more" button |
| A sign-up flow | The steps as a short numbered line (1 of 3), one question per screen, and what the person gets, said once | Many fields on one screen with a decorative illustration |
| A law library | The legal text itself: article numbers in the margin, the cited line highlighted | A search box floating on a gradient |

## When the subject has no picture

Some pages are only words (an announcement, a policy). Then the idea is the typography: one
confident display size for the headline, a reading column, and a single rule or a margin note.
That is still an idea. Bare is when there is no decision at all.
