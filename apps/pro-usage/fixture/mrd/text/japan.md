# Japan: Migrant Rights Database, 2023

## Civil and political life

**Q91 [Protection]** Right of regular migrants to vote in local and/or regional elections.

Answer: No

> No protection of this right. Supreme court in Japan held that freedom of peaceful assembly for foreigners is not necessarily recognized to the extent that it affects political decision-making or implementation in Japan. For example, the Supreme Court Judgement (McLean case, as of October 4, 1978), recognized that participation in demonstrations against the Vietnam War as a reason for inadequate renewal of the period of stay. National law in Japan protects this right, in a general way, in Article 21 of the Constitution.

Answer: Does Not Apply

**Q93 [Protection]** Right of regular migrants to stand for election.

Answer: No right to vote in any election.

> No protection of this right. National law in Japan bans a legal right for migrants to vote in local elections in Article 18 of the Local Autonomy Act, and in national elections in Article 10 of the Public Office Election Act.

## Due process

**Q35 [Protection]** Right to equal protection of the law before courts.

Answer: Yes (subscore 100)

> National law in Japan protects this right in Article 31 and 32 of the Constitution. Article 32 of the Constitution provides that "No person shall be denied the right of access to the courts." On September 23, 2021, the Tokyo High Court issued a final and binding decision, ruling that sending a refugee applicant back on a government-chartered plane without guaranteeing their right to a trial violated Article 32 of the Constitution. The relevant section of the ruling states, "The fact that the immigration officials, on the premise that the appellants were subject to mass deportation, delayed the notification of the decision to dismiss the objection to the non-recognition of refugee status until just before the deportation, and deported them without allowing them to contact a third party, should be seen as having effectively deprived them of the opportunity for judicial review for refugee status, thus violating their right to a trial guaranteed in Article 32 of the Constitution, and the guarantee of due process in Article 31 and Article 13 of the Constitution linked to that."

**Q36 [Implementation]** To what extent is this migrant right protection implemented by the relevant authorities?

Answer: Sometimes (subscore 50)

**Q37 [Protection]** Right to government-provided counsel in proceedings related to migrant status.

Answer: No

> National law in Japan does not protect this right for all migrants since irregular migrants are excluded from this right. For regular migrants, national law protects this right in Article 30 (2) of the Comprehensive Legal Support Act, which provides that "services as follows for providing assistance to Japanese citizens and foreign nationals lawfully residing in Japan (hereinafter collectively referred to as "citizens and legal foreign residents") who lack the financial means to pay the necessary expenses incurred in preparing to exercise and exercising their own rights in civil, family, and administrative court proceedings or administrative complaint filing procedures, or who would experience serious financial difficulties if they were to pay those expenses themselves."

**Q38 [Implementation]** To what extent is this migrant right protection implemented by the relevant authorities?

Answer: Does Not Apply

**Q39 [Protection]** Right to government-provided interpretation in criminal proceedings.

Answer: Yes (subscore 100)

> National law in Japan protects this right in Article 31 of the Constitution and Article 14 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights as self-executing treaty. Additionally, national law protects this right in articles 175 and 181 of the Code of Criminal Procedure and Article 2 of the Act of Criminal Proceeding Costs given a February 3, 1993 ruling of the Tokyo High Court in the Luther Case (decided with reference to the international covenant on civil and political rights).

**Q41 [Protection]** Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.

Answer: No

> No protection of this right. National law in Japan criminally sanctions unauthorized entry in Article 70 of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, but does not civilly sanction it. The only exemption happens when migrants are refugees and enter Japan directly from a territory in which their life, body or physical freedom was likely to be persecuted in Article 70-2 of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act.

## Education

Answer: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses (e.g., economic, family, humanitarian, temporary or regular migrants). (subscore 50)

## Equal protection

**Q131 [Protection]** Right to be free from discrimination on the basis of migrant status.

Answer: Yes, but only for certain visa types and migrant statuses. (subscore 50)

> National law in Japan protects this right, in a conditional way, in Article 90 and 125 of the School Education Law and Article 26 of the Constitution. In conjunction with Article 13 (2) (c) of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, "higher education shall be made equally accessible to all." Vocational Abilities Development Promotion Act does not have nationality requirement for vocational training, there are some special public vocational training courses for migrants. Foreign residents residing in Japan for the purpose of engaging in labor, or refugees from Ukraine with the status of residence that permits working in Japan can access to this "Vocational Training Course for Long-term Resident Foreign Nationals". However, they need to register as job seekers with the Public Employment Security Office. Since they need to have status of residence that permits working in Japan, undocumented migrants and non-workable status migrants do not have the right to access to public vocational training.

## Family

**Q73 [Protection]** Right of settled migrants to family reunification.

Answer: No

> No protection of this right for both adult migrants and migrant children. However, national law in Japan guarantees citizenship to children of migrants who would otherwise be stateless only if they were born in Japan according to Article 2 (iii) of Nationality Act. No such protection for adult migrants who are stateless other than seeking to naturalize.

**Q74 [Implementation]** To what extent are the legal avenues available to settled migrants to facilitate their reunification with immediate or non-dependent family members implemented by the relevant authorities?

Answer: Does Not Apply

**Q75 [Protection]** Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.

Answer: Conditional (subscore 50)

> National law in Japan provides settled migrants with legal avenues to facilitate reunification with immediate or non-dependent family members in Article 2-2 and the Appended Table I and II of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, and Public Notice on establishing the Status listed in the lower column of the "Long-Term Resident" section of Appended table II of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act pursuant to the provisions of Article 7, paragraph (1), item (ii) of the same Act., which provide "status of residence" as settled migrants.

**Q76 [Implementation]** To what extent is the judicial remedy (to appeal the refusal by the authorities to allow family formation/reunification) implemented by the relevant authorities?

Answer: Sometimes; Effectively Always (subscore 75)

**Q77 [Protection]** Right to recognition of same-sex marital relationships.

Answer: Yes (subscore 100)

> National law in Japan provides judicial remedy to appeal the refusal by the authorities to allow family formation/reunification in Article 9(1) of the Administrative Case Litigation Act, which provides that "An action for the revocation of an original administrative disposition and an action for the revocation of an administrative determination (hereinafter referred to as "actions for the revocation of administrative dispositions") may be filed only by a person who has legal interest to seek the revocation of the original administrative disposition or of the administrative determination (including a person who has legal interest to be recovered by revoking the original administrative disposition or administrative determination even after it has lost its effect due to the expiration of a certain period or for other reasons)."

**Q78 [Implementation]** To what extent is the recognition of same sex marital relationships implemented by the relevant authorities?

Answer: Rarely (subscore 25)

**Q79 [Protection]** Right to family reunification without resource conditions

Answer: Conditional (subscore 50)

> National law in Japan recognizes same sex marriage under the family reunification framework for certain migrants in Article 7 (1) and "Designated Activities" in the Appended Table I (5) of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act. Japan does not recognize same-sex marriages by civil law, but if two foreign nationals are in a same-sex marriage and the civil laws of their respective home countries recognize such marriages, they will be granted a residency status called 'designated acitivities'. However, if a Japanese citizen and a migrant are in a same-sex marriage, even if civil law recognizes same-sex marriage in the migrant's home country, the migrant will not be granted such status of residence. Meanwhile, civil partnership agreements are not recognized under this family reunification framework.

**Q80 [Implementation]** To what extent do relevant authorities allow a right to family reunification regardless of resource conditions?

Answer: Usually; Effectively Always (subscore 87.5)

**Q67 [Protection]** Right of humanitarian migrants to citizenship.

Answer: Yes (subscore 100)
