**Q41 [Protection]** Right to be protected from prosecution for unauthorized entry.

## Education

**Q127 [Protection]** Right to equal access to public adult education institutions.

Answer: Yes (including irregular migrants) (subscore 100)

> National law in Mexico protects this right in 1) Article 4 of the Constitution, which states that every family has the right to enjoy a decent and respectable house. The constitution also stipulates that the law will create instruments and support necessary to achieve this objective. 2) Article 3 of the Housing law, which establishes that "the provisions of the Law require restrictions under the principles of equity and social inclusion so that every person, regardless of origin, gender, age, disability, social or economic status, conditions of health, religion, opinions, preferences or marital status, can exercise their right to housing." Under this provision, migrants can enter social programs for housing.

**Q128 [Implementation]** To what extent is this migrant right protection implemented by the education agency?

**Q129 [Protection]** Right of access to public adult education institutions regardless of status.

> National law in Mexico protects this right in 1) the Constitution, Article 3. 2) The Migration Law, Article 8, which recognizes the right to education for migrants independent of their migration status. Furthermore, it states the prohibition of establishing greater requirements to access this right than those provided for Mexican citizens. 3) The Education Law, Articles 8, 9(X), and 11. However, children without a regular status that lack a population ID ("CURP") have a difficult time to get certifications and diplomas from schools.

**Q75 [Protection]** Right to judicial remedy for denials of right to family reunification.

Answer: Conditional (subscore 50)

> National law in Mexico conditionally protects this right in 1) the Migration Law, Articles 44 and 58. 2) The Regulation of the Migration Law, 107-III, 109-I, and 100-113. 3) The Asylum Law, Article 44 and 58. 4) The Regulation of the Asylum Law, Articles 13, 80, 82. Temporal and permanent residents have the right to family reunification; they can either come with their family or request entry later. Likewise, in cases where a visa is granted for a job offer or for humanitarian reasons, Article 41 of the Migration Law provides the possibility of requesting a visa in the facilities of the National Immigration Institute. Temporary and permanent residents are allowed family reunification for the following members of their families: children of temporary resident and children of their partners (child or adolescents) while they are under their custody; spouse or partner in free union (according to Mexican law); father or mother of the temporary or permanent resident; siblings that are children or adolescents and under the custody of the resident (only for permanent residents). Refugees also have the right to family reunification for their children, spouse or partner in a free union (according to Article 58 of the Asylum Law) and her/his relatives economical dependant of the refugee until second grade; and blood relatives until the fourth grade

**Q103 [Protection]** Right to equal employment conditions and protection.

## Life

**Q9 [Protection]** Right to be free from use of force to prevent entry to territory.

Answer: No.

> No protection of this right. In principle, migration agents are not entitled to exercise the use of force against migrants. However, the Migration Law provides that the National Guard can support the Federal Police in immigration control and verification operations (Article 81). Additionally, the Law on the National Guard grants members of the National Guard the ability to review the documents of migrants (both on or within the border) to verify their migration status situation in coordination with the National Institute on Migration (except for the facilities for international transit), and, if applicable, proceed to escort them to migration authorities. In that sense, the National Guard is obliged to follow the standards on the use of force established in the National Law on the Use of Force (with no specific provision for migrants). There is no express prohibition to the use of force, however, and instead there are rather vague provisions stating that security forces can assist in national security activities. There is no prohibition. However, violence has been used to stop caravans; such "security measures" are not coded.

**Q10 [Implementation]** To what extent is this migrant right protection implemented by the relevant authorities?

Answer: Does Not Apply

**Q13 [Protection]** Right to be protected from trafficking or smuggling.

Answer: Yes, the national law provides exemption (subscore 100)

Normally, smuggled migrants are sent to immigration detention centers and deported to their countries unless they are considered as important witnesses in an investigation, in which case they can access a humanitarian visa.
