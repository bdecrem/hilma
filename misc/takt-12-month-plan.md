# 12 months of keys: minor scales, blues jamming, writing tracks

~3 hours a week. 24 chapters of 2 weeks, ~6 hours each.

**Every session:** 10 min scales, 20 min jamming over a track, 15 min on the chapter's own thing.
**Every 6th chapter:** a checkpoint. Record something.

## Scale tempo targets

Measured on 2 octaves, up and down, clean and even. Start at 60 and only bump 4–5 bpm when a pass is clean. Speed you can't hold evenly just trains the mistakes. The number moves when the playing does, not the calendar.

| When | Target |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | 60 bpm, eighth notes, hands separate. Get the fingering automatic. |
| Week 2 | 72–80 bpm, hands separate |
| Week 3 | 90–100 bpm hands separate; hands together at 60 |
| Month 3 | 100 bpm eighths hands together |
| Month 6 | 80 bpm sixteenths (twice as fast; licks start feeling like licks) |

## Q1 — one key, A minor

1. A minor pentatonic, both hands. 12-bar minor blues (Am–Dm–Em). Jam with 5 notes only.
2. Blues scale (add the b5). Call and response: 2 bars play, 2 bars rest. Left hand plays root + 7th.
3. A natural minor. See the pentatonic inside it. Land on a chord tone on beat 1.
4. Dorian (raised 6th) over Am7–Dm7–E7. This is the minor-blues sound.
5. Swing, triplets, space. Steal 3 licks, move them around the scale.
6. **Checkpoint:** record a 3-chorus solo. Write a first 12-bar with a hook.

## Q2 — more keys, more chords

7. Same material in E minor and D minor. Transpose by shape.
8. Harmonic minor: the raised 7th belongs over the V chord (E7), not everywhere.
9. Chord vocabulary: 7ths, 9ths, 6ths, sus. Rootless 3-note left-hand voicings.
10. Turnarounds: Bm7b5–E7 back to Am; quick change in bar 2.
11. Major blues: major pentatonic and mixolydian, mixing major and minor over a dominant chord. G blues.
12. **Checkpoint:** track 2, new key, with turnaround. Record it.

## Q3 — taste

13. Transcribe 2 short phrases by ear (Otis Spann, Pinetop Perkins, Ray Charles).
14. Motif development: 3 notes, repeat, vary, answer. Fewer notes, better ones.
15. Left hand: shuffle, boogie, walking bass. Right hand gets lighter.
16. Modes as colors over a static vamp: Dorian vs Aeolian vs Phrygian, one chord for 5 minutes. The techno-shaped chapter.
17. Beyond 12 bars: 8 and 16-bar forms, Am–F–G, i–bVII–bVI, the Andalusian cadence.
18. **Checkpoint:** track 3, not a 12-bar. Minor vamp with a melody.

## Q4 — range

19. Minor pentatonic and blues scale in all 12 keys. 1 key a day, 2 minutes each.
20. Substitutions: tritone sub, diminished passing chords, gospel-blues moves.
21. Altered dominants: b9, #9, 13 on the V. Harmonic minor meets the blues scale.
22. Two-hand independence: solo while comping. Trade 4s with the track.
23. Form: intro, head, solos, ending. Write a full arranged track.
24. **Final:** record a 3-track set in 3 keys and tempos, plus track 4. List next year's gaps.

Q1 is deliberately slow and in one key. Fluency in A minor beats shaky knowledge of 6 keys; everything after is mostly moving the same shapes around.
